## 1. How do Moroccan SMEs discover, trust, buy and pay for software today?

### Takeaway
Moroccan firms have adopted basic digital tools but rarely use specialised software (31% of firms; about 8% use an ERP). The market is mostly informal, cash-heavy and wary of tax scrutiny. Buyers prefer "do it for me" service to self-serve tools, and still often buy desktop or one-time licences. Cash and WhatsApp are the defaults. Card acceptance is growing quickly from a low base, and regulators are pushing it hard (interchange cap cut to 0.5%, CMI acquiring monopoly ended).

### Cited Findings
**Adoption level and depth**
- A 2024 World Bank survey of 1,256 manufacturing and services firms found basic digital tools widely adopted. Only 31% of establishments use specialised software and barely 8% have an ERP. Fewer than half use their tools for business administration or customer management. The World Bank calls this "incomplete digitalisation" and estimates productivity gains of up to 70% when firms move from basic adoption to intensive use. — [Hespress FR (World Bank analysis)](https://fr.hespress.com/483014-483014.html); see also [Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/economie/numerique-pourquoi-les-entreprises-marocaines-peinent-encore-a-accelerer/358014)
- A GIZ-backed study of TPME digital maturity was announced in November 2025, with first results expected at GITEX Africa 2026. The article notes Morocco has no structured, reliable system for measuring firms' digital maturity. — [Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/economie/digitalisation-une-etude-pour-mesurer-la-maturite-des-tpme-marocaines/312072/amp)
- A 2021 AUSIM/Devoteam survey of about 70 IT and digital leaders named the lack of a digital-strategy budget and the lack of support as major obstacles. — [FNH](https://www.fnh.ma/article/actualites-marocaines/rdvausim-resultats-d-une-enquete-inedite-sur-la-maturite-digitale-au-maroc)

**Buying psychology and trust barriers**
- An analysis of the Moroccan SaaS market says the typical Moroccan client prefers a "do it for me" service to self-service tools, which makes SaaS adoption "particularly difficult." It also cites low perceived value of automation, cash-flow pressure and fear of tax scrutiny. It adds that "no Moroccan SaaS company has yet managed to build a dominant position in its segment." — [HNB.ma](https://www.hnb.ma/p/lavenir-des-startups-saas-au-maroc)
- Distrust of cloud hosting is described as a barrier linked to fear of tax audits, especially in smaller firms. Data-protection law 09-08 and the CNDP framework are cited as trust levers. (Vendor blog; attribution from search summary.) — [aydi.ma](https://aydi.ma/cloud-computing-pme-solutions-roi/)
- Moroccan startups report payment delays from business customers averaging six months, with some cases reaching 13 months (roundtable, about 2023). — [LesEco](https://leseco.ma/maroc/delais-de-paiement-et-charges-fiscales-les-defis-financiers-des-start-up-au-maroc-video.html); [LesEco](https://leseco.ma/business/delais-de-paiement-les-startups-marocaines-ny-echappent-pas.html)

**Informality and firm structure (the addressable base)**
- About 70% of Moroccan entrepreneurs operate informally. There are about 2.7 million established entrepreneurs, a quarter of all employed people. The auto-entrepreneur status (created 2015) carries a 1% presumptive tax on service turnover under MAD 200,000. Estimates of the informal share of GDP run from about 11% (HCP) to 30% (Bank Al-Maghrib). — [OECD Economic Survey Morocco 2024](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2024/09/oecd-economic-surveys-morocco-2024_d4786047/full-report/component-6.html)
- MSMEs provide about 70% of jobs but only about 30% of GDP, and about 70% of economic units are informal. — [AfDB PAFE-Emploi programme document](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/projects-and-operations/morocco_-ar-_entrepreneurship_support_and_financing_programme_for_job_creation_pafe-emploi_a_publier_002.pdf)
- Morocco has more than 120,000 neighbourhood "hanout" shops, mostly under-digitised and outside formal financial services. — [The Startup Scene](https://thestartupscene.me/PowerLists/Inside-the-Billion-Dollar-Race-to-Digitise-Morocco-s-Small-Shops). Another estimate puts the count at about 126,000 shops handling more than 85% of food spending in a market of roughly $40B. — [Innovation Village](https://innovation-village.com/zsystems-raises-1-65-million-to-digitise-moroccos-40-billion-retail-supply-chain/)

**Price points and purchase format observed in Morocco**
- TPE invoicing software is said to cost 0–300 MAD/month (Zoho Invoice, Sage 50 Maroc, Facture.net, etc.). — [WINS](https://wins.ma/index.php/2026/08/27/logiciel_de_facturation_tpe_maroc/)
- One local POS (SystemeMaroc) is quoted at 350 MAD/month with 7-day-a-week support. The same guide cites a 9,900 MAD pack that costs 990 MAD after the Mowakaba subsidy. — [Oasis Technocloud guide](https://oasistechnocloud.com/blog/guide-logiciel-gestion-entreprise-maroc/)
- Typical SaaS spend for a 10–20 person PME is put at 500–3,000 MAD/month, with "light" SaaS from about 50 MAD per user per month. (Vendor guides; search-summary attribution.) — [Eurastech](https://www.eurastech.ma/fr/blog/saas-vs-sur-mesure-choisir-maroc); [aydi.ma](https://aydi.ma/cloud-computing-pme-solutions-roi/)
- Accounting software for fiduciaires is still largely desktop or licence-based. Sage Expert starts around 10,000 DH/year. Oasis's "Pack Fiduciaire" is a locally installed multi-client package from 14,900 MAD. KHABIR (Datalyse) is described as the reference for 1–10 person fiduciaires and offers a special partner rate to fiduciaires and accountants. GénéraFi claims more than 7,500 users. — [Oasis comparatif](https://oasistechnocloud.com/blog/logiciel-gestion-cabinet-comptable-maroc/); [Oasis Pack Fiduciaire](https://oasistechnocloud.com/pack-fiduciaire/); [NSE/KHABIR](https://www.nse-ma.com/fr/logiciel-de-comptabilite-khabir/); [GénéraFi](https://www.generafi.com/nos-solutions/comptabilite/)

**Payments: cash, cards, wallets**
- In 2025, bank-card payments reached 230.1M transactions worth MAD 70.8B, about 20% growth. Payments were 34% of card transactions by number (32% in 2024) but only 13% by value; withdrawals were 87%. Bank-issued mobile wallets carried about 2% of transaction value. — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/10/343358/morocco-sees-20-rise-in-bank-card-payments-in-2025/)
- Estimates of cash in circulation conflict: MAD 513B in 2025 versus MAD 491B (+18.5%). Bank Al-Maghrib has said growth in e-payments has not yet meaningfully reduced cash use. — [Hespress EN](https://en.hespress.com/147793-moroccans-withdraw-mad-460-billion-as-cash-use-outpaces-digital-shift.html); [Middle East Online](https://middle-east-online.com/en/cash-surges-record-levels-morocco)
- About 74% of Moroccan transactions were reportedly still cash-based in 2025 (law-firm guide, methodology unverified). — [Korte Law](https://korte-law.com/fintech-payment-licensing-morocco)
- Bank Al-Maghrib cut the domestic interchange cap on card payments to 0.5% from the 0.65% cap set in October 2024, to encourage acceptance. One outlet reports an even lower 0.15% cap for proximity merchants; no other source confirms it. — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/10/341181/bank-al-maghrib-cuts-bank-card-payment-commission-cap-to-0-5/); [7news.ma](https://en.7news.ma/morocco-cashless-payments-card-fees/)
- A Competition Council decision ended CMI's quasi-monopoly on merchant acquiring (effective around May 2025). Card payments are still often refused in small shops and restaurants. (Search-summary attribution.) — [Charipay blog](https://charipay.ma/en/blog/choisir-son-psp-au-maroc); [7news.ma](https://en.7news.ma/morocco-cashless-payments-card-fees/)
- A Mastercard SME trust index found 77% of Moroccan SMEs had not yet integrated a digital payment service (article about 2024). — [Hespress FR](https://fr.hespress.com/412467-au-maroc-77-des-pme-hesitent-a-adopter-le-e-paiement.html)
- A Visa-commissioned study reported that two-thirds of small merchants surveyed already prefer digital payments. Mobile-payment volume reportedly rose from 9.7M (2023) to 19.7M (2025). (Search-summary attribution.) — [Onyx.ma](https://onyx.ma/fr/transition-paiements-numeriques-maroc-opportunites-startups/)
- In 2025, Secretary of State Omar Hjira urged hanout owners to adopt digital payments and said it could unlock an extra MAD 166B in transactions. — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2025/06/215757/moroccos-moul-hanout-government-urges-shopkeepers-to-go-digital-amid-fierce-retail-competition/)

**Communication channels (WhatsApp, Darija)**
- The 2025 Sunergia barometer: WhatsApp is the dominant platform, used by 75% of the population, and 95% of its users connect daily. Overall social-media use is 81%. — [Hespress EN](https://en.hespress.com/112276-moroccans-flock-to-whatsapp-facebook-and-instagram-as-social-media-use-hits-81.html)
- Morocco has about 19M WhatsApp users (vendor estimate). — [Yazi](https://www.askyazi.com/articles/whatsapp-penetration-across-africa-statistics-by-country)
- Vendor claims, unaudited: 78% of Moroccan customers message businesses via WhatsApp, and about 40% of customer queries arrive as voice notes. Both point to Darija voice-note support as a real need. — [wasel.ma](https://wasel.ma/en/blog/ecosysteme-whatsapp-business-maroc-2026-english/); [hivevaultarc](https://hivevaultarc.com/blog/whatsapp-ai-chatbot-morocco-business-guide)

### Inferences
- The "do it for me" preference, low ERP use and fear of tax visibility suggest pure self-serve PLG will underperform in Morocco. A hybrid fits better: a free, WhatsApp-native first step, then human onboarding in Darija over WhatsApp or video, plus a partner who does the setup (fiduciaire, integrator or field agent).
- Fear of tax scrutiny matters a lot when the product records revenue (ledger, POS, invoicing). Products that help the merchant *get paid* or *get stock* (Chari, credit-ledger reminders) have an easier trust story than products that mainly *record* revenue, until a mandate such as e-invoicing forces recording.
- Cash dominance means software that must be paid by card monthly hits friction. Annual prepaid plans, payment by bank transfer or cash through agents, and subsidy-financed packs (Mowakaba) fit the market better. This is inferred from the price-point and payments evidence; I found no direct survey on how SMEs pay for software.

### Gaps
- No Moroccan survey found that asks SMEs how they *discover* software (word of mouth versus resellers versus ads) or how they *pay* for it (transfer, cheque, cash, card). The GIZ maturity study, due at GITEX Africa 2026, may fill this; its results were not found.
- No primary data found on in-person demo preference beyond the qualitative HNB analysis.
- Darija and WhatsApp-support effects rest only on vendor marketing claims.
- I could not open the World Bank report itself to confirm whether the 31% and 8% figures cover all firms or SMEs only.

## 2. Which distribution channels exist specifically in Morocco (WhatsApp/social, formateurs and affiliates, accountants, telecoms and banks, government programmes, the e-invoicing mandate)?

### Takeaway
Morocco's clearest distribution levers right now are: (1) the pending DGI e-invoicing clearance mandate, a ZATCA-style forcing function that has slipped but is still expected; (2) Maroc PME's Mowakaba subsidy, reportedly covering up to 80–90% of SME software spend; (3) the e-commerce "formateur"/affiliate ecosystem that YouCan has built (lifetime revenue share, large in-person events); (4) Ministry of Industry and Trade "Commerce 2030" framework agreements with hanout-digitising startups; and (5) telecom groups (Orange) acting as investor-partners rather than resellers. I found no evidence of telecom or bank *bundles* of SME SaaS.

### Cited Findings
**E-invoicing mandate (DGI) as a forcing function**
- Morocco is adopting a centralised clearance (CTC) model: B2B invoices must be validated by the DGI platform before they are legally valid, in UBL 2.1 or UN/CEFACT CII format with qualified e-signatures. A July 2026 briefing says the implementing decree had been sent to the government's General Secretariat but **was not yet published**, describing "slippage from the initial October 2024/2025/2026 roadmap." — [VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/07/02/briefing-document-podcast-e-invoicing-e-reporting-in-morocco/)
- In an April 2026 Médias24 interview, Director General of Taxes Younès Idrissi Kaitouni said rollout was still scheduled for 2026. Comarch reports the technical infrastructure is ready. — [VATupdate summary](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/07/02/briefing-document-podcast-e-invoicing-e-reporting-in-morocco/); [Comarch](https://www.comarch.com/trade-and-services/data-management/legal-regulation-changes/morocco-confirms-2026-mandate-for-electronic-invoicing/)
- Inyad (a Moroccan merchant-software vendor) publishes an *indicative* timeline: large taxpayers from January 2026, SMEs from 1 July 2026, micro-businesses 2027–2028. It also cites planned fines of 1,000–50,000 DH per untransmitted invoice. Inyad itself warns the dates may change. — [Inyad blog](https://inyad.com/blog/calendrier-facture-electronique-maroc-2026-2028); [Inyad PME](https://inyad.com/blog/facture-electronique-pme-maroc)
- **Conflict:** a WINS guide says digitised invoicing has been a legal obligation since 1 January 2026. — [WINS](https://wins.ma/index.php/2026/08/27/logiciel_de_facturation_tpe_maroc/). This contradicts the July 2026 VATupdate briefing that the decree was unpublished. Treat the start date as unconfirmed.
- Local vendors already market e-invoicing readiness to fiduciaires: ExpertC (browser SaaS for accounting firms, including e-invoicing), Fiducio (hosted in Morocco), Experio (AI pre-accounting, OCR, TVA/RAS e-filing), Bleez Maroc (subsidiary of a French SaaS publisher). — [ExpertC](https://www.expertc.ma/); [Fiducio](https://fiducio.ma/); [Experio](https://experio.ma/); [Bleez](https://bleez.com/fr-tn/); [Oasis](https://oasistechnocloud.com/blog/logiciel-comptabilite-maroc/)

**Government subsidy: Maroc PME "Mowakaba"**
- Mowakaba TPME (run by Maroc PME) reportedly co-finances digital transformation: up to 90% for very small firms and 80% for SMEs, with ceilings of about 40,000 MAD (TPE) and 400,000 MAD (PME). Eligible costs reportedly include ERP, CRM, accounting, payroll, e-commerce licences, implementation, training and first-year hosting. Firms must be registered and up to date with tax and CNSS. **All of these sources are consultancies or integrators selling subsidised packs, not the official Maroc PME guidelines.** — [gest360](https://gest360.ma/blog/mowakaba-financement-digitalisation-maroc-2026); [Karizma](https://karizma-group.com/subventions-digitalisation-maroc); [Oasis](https://oasistechnocloud.com/blog/digitalisation-pme-maroc/); [Agence KSUR](https://www.agence-ksur.com/subventions-maroc)
- Example of subsidy-driven packaging: a 9,900 MAD pack sold as 990 MAD net under Mowakaba. — [Oasis guide](https://oasistechnocloud.com/blog/guide-logiciel-gestion-entreprise-maroc/)
- The Digital Morocco 2030 strategy launched in September 2024. — [Wikipedia: IT in Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology_in_Morocco). A blog cites an 11B MAD first-phase budget (2024–2026); unverified. — [abmatech](https://www.abmatech.com/digital-morocco-2030-financement/)

**Government–startup partnerships for hanout digitisation**
- Under its Commerce 2030 strategy, the Ministry of Industry and Trade has signed partnerships with at least two startups. Woliz's framework agreement covers 20,000 shops in its first phase. — [The Startup Scene](https://thestartupscene.me/PowerLists/Inside-the-Billion-Dollar-Race-to-Digitise-Morocco-s-Small-Shops)
- Headline (October 2025): "Moroccan Startup Partners with Government to Digitize 50K Local Shops by 2030" (startup not confirmed from the snippet). — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2025/10/261198/moroccan-startup-partners-with-government-to-digitize-50k-local-shops-by-2030/)
- Woliz raised $2.2M (January 2026) to digitise neighbourhood retail. — [WeeTracker](https://weetracker.com/2026/01/06/woliz-raises-usd-2-2-m-to-digitise-moroccos-neighbourhood-retail-economy/). z.systems raised a $1.65M seed round ($2.7M to date); The Startup Scene lists it as a $1.5M pre-seed in April 2026, which conflicts. — [Yabiladi](https://en.yabiladi.com/articles/details/191829/moroccan-startup-systems-raises-million.html); [Innovation Village](https://innovation-village.com/zsystems-raises-1-65-million-to-digitise-moroccos-40-billion-retail-supply-chain/)
- Launch Base Africa calls this space "Hanout Wars: Three Startups, $10m, and One Corner Shop" (April 2026). — [Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/04/06/moroccos-hanout-wars-three-startups-10m-and-one-corner-shop/)

**E-commerce trainers ("formateurs"), affiliates and events (YouCan)**
- YouCan's official partner page shows "Earn 50%" in its affiliate section and claims 400K+ active stores. — [YouCan Partner Program](https://youcan.shop/en/affiliate)
- A Moroccan blog says affiliates earn up to 20% of referred merchants' fees for life, paid in MAD to Moroccan bank accounts. This conflicts with the "50%" on the official page; the basis of each rate is unclear. — [ribhonline](https://ribhonline.com/blog/youcan-affiliate-maroc)
- One Trustpilot reviewer says payout required at least 25 referred stores (single anecdote). — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/youcan.shop)
- YouCan's January event drew more than 4,500 participants, described as the largest e-commerce event in Morocco and the Arab world. There, co-founder and president Akram Benmbarek reported GMV growing from $88M in the platform's first year to $753M in 2023. — [YouCan blog](https://youcan.shop/en/blog/posts/17jan-event); [YouCan help blog](https://youcan.shop/en/help/blog/youcan-event)
- YouCan runs free hands-on e-commerce training cohorts (for example, 10 entrepreneurs with personal support). — [YouCan training](https://youcan.shop/en/help/blog/training-ecommerce.md)
- Moroccan e-commerce is cash-on-delivery driven; one vendor guide says COD is over 70% of online transactions (unverified). — [eGrow](https://www.egrow.com/en/blog/complete-guide-cod-ecommerce-morocco-2026)

**Telecom operators and banks**
- Maroc Telecom launched MT Cash mobile money on 1 June 2020, after inwi and Orange. — [Agence Ecofin](https://www.agenceecofin.com/operateur/0306-77184-maroc-telecom-a-rejoint-inwi-et-orange-dans-le-mobile-money-avec-sa-solution-mt-cash)
- Orange acts mainly as investor and partner. Orange Ventures co-led Chari's $12M Series A, and Chari partnered with Orange Maroc to bring telecom and payment services into its app. Orange MEA and AXA Assurance Maroc acquired a majority stake in DabaDoc in 2021. — [Disrupt Africa](https://disruptafrica.com/2025/10/16/moroccan-b2b-e-commerce-startup-chari-closes-12m-series-a-round/); [Dabafinance](https://dabafinance.com/en/news/chari-partners-with-orange-maroc-to-digitize-neighborhood-retailers); [AppsAfrica](https://www.appsafrica.com/orange-and-axa-to-acquire-stake-in-health-tech-dabadoc/)
- A search for telecom TPE bundles (invoicing or POS software) from Maroc Telecom, inwi or Orange Business found none. — [search result set incl. WINS](https://wins.ma/index.php/2026/08/27/logiciel_de_facturation_tpe_maroc/)

**Accountants and fiduciaires as a channel**
- KHABIR offers a "special rate for our partners: fiduciaries and accounting experts," evidence that incumbents already use fiduciaires as resellers or influencers. — [NSE/KHABIR](https://www.nse-ma.com/fr/logiciel-de-comptabilite-khabir/)
- Several Moroccan products target the fiduciaire as the buyer, with multi-client files, a shared agenda and client document management (ExpertC, Fiducio, Oasis Pack Fiduciaire). — [ExpertC](https://www.expertc.ma/); [Fiducio](https://fiducio.ma/); [Oasis Pack Fiduciaire](https://oasistechnocloud.com/pack-fiduciaire/)

### Inferences
- The DGI clearance mandate is Morocco's best equivalent of Saudi Arabia's ZATCA (see Section 4). If and when the decree is published, every VAT-registered business needs a DGI-connected invoicing path. Fiduciaires will be the main source of advice, so a product that is free or very cheap for micro-firms and gives fiduciaires a multi-client dashboard could copy Wafeq's "SMEs + accounting firms" positioning. The timing risk is real: the decree had slipped as of July 2026.
- Mowakaba (if confirmed at 80–90%) supports annual or one-time packaged pricing sold through integrators, not month-to-month self-serve. A SaaS could get approved as an eligible solution and recruit integrators who handle the subsidy paperwork. This is a reseller channel the subsidy itself pays for.
- YouCan shows that Moroccan e-commerce "formateurs" can be paid through lifetime revenue share and in-person mega-events, then turned into a distribution force. Any tool for COD e-commerce sellers (order confirmation, delivery reconciliation, returns) should plug into this network and into YouCan's app or partner ecosystem.
- Telecoms in Morocco behave as strategic investors (Orange → Chari, DabaDoc), not as SaaS resellers. A telecom partnership is more likely to mean an investment or co-marketing deal than a bundle with real distribution.

### Gaps
- Official Maroc PME Mowakaba terms (rates, ceilings, eligible SaaS, approval process) not verified; only consultancy pages were found.
- No data found on Facebook groups, WhatsApp groups or TikTok/Instagram "business influencers" in Morocco as a software channel (search budget ran out before this was covered).
- No data found on banks bundling SME software (for example, Attijariwafa, CIH or Bank of Africa TPE packs). The search was planned but the budget ran out.
- Official text and final dates of the DGI e-invoicing decree remain unconfirmed.

## 3. Moroccan case studies: how local SMB-tech companies acquired customers

### Takeaway
The best-documented Moroccan winners did not grow by selling software subscriptions to SMEs through ads. YouCan grew through an educator, affiliate and event ecosystem around COD e-commerce, reaching $753M GMV in 2023. Chari grew through a transactional wedge (FMCG ordering with cash on delivery) into a licensed payment institution. DabaDoc used a free-for-consumers booking marketplace funded by doctor subscriptions and insurers. Inyad bundled ledger, POS and invoicing and now markets heavily on e-invoicing compliance.

### Cited Findings
**YouCan (e-commerce platform, COD sellers)**
- Co-founded in 2020; Mohamed El Ghaissani is CEO and co-founder. — [Empower Africa](https://empowerafrica.com/mohamed-el-ghaissani-the-moroccan-entrepreneur-simplifying-e-commerce-for-everyone/)
- GMV went from $88M in year one to $753M in 2023 (reported at a 4,500+ attendee event). — [YouCan blog](https://youcan.shop/en/blog/posts/17jan-event)
- It bundled sourcing, storefronts, order confirmation, fulfilment and payments into one platform, and partnered with Glovo for sub-one-hour intra-city delivery starting in Casablanca. — [YouCan blog](https://youcan.shop/en/help/blog/youcan-event); [EcoActu](https://ecoactu.ma/?p=205551)
- Its affiliate/partner programme pays a lifetime revenue share (20–50%; sources conflict), and the company claims 400K+ active stores. — [YouCan Partner Program](https://youcan.shop/en/affiliate); [ribhonline](https://ribhonline.com/blog/youcan-affiliate-maroc)
- An early Challenge.ma profile: "Youcan.shop prend son envol au Maroc." — [Challenge.ma](https://www.challenge.ma/youcan-shop-prend-son-envol-au-maroc-274332/)

**Chari (B2B FMCG ordering for hanouts → fintech)**
- In 2021 it reached 10,000 retailers signed up. — [Trendtype](https://trendtype.com/news/moroccan-digital-buying-platform-chari-ma-reaches-milestone-of-10000-retailers/). VC4A reported about 4,000 monthly active users and 10% month-over-month growth. — [VC4A](https://vc4a.com/ventures/chari-co)
- Retailers pay cash on delivery while Chari pays FMCG suppliers on 30–45 day terms (search-summary attribution). — [Connecting Africa](https://connectingafrica.com/startups/hot-startup-of-the-month-morocco-s-chari)
- January 2022: valued at $100M in a bridge round "as it looks to pilot BNPL services" (TechCrunch headline cited on Chari's site). The site lists a merchant "credit ledger." — [Chari Group](https://chari.group/en)
- October 2025: a $12M Series A co-led by SPE Capital and Orange Ventures, called the largest Series A in Moroccan history, bringing total funding to $17M. Chari has onboarded over 20,000 food businesses in Morocco and expanded to Tunisia and Côte d'Ivoire. — [Disrupt Africa](https://disruptafrica.com/2025/10/16/moroccan-b2b-e-commerce-startup-chari-closes-12m-series-a-round/); [Techparley](https://techparley.com/moroccan-startup-chari-secures-12-million-to-build-africas-next-merchant-super-app-what-it-means-for-africa/)

### Inferences
- Moroccan SMB-tech winners entered through a *transaction or demand* wedge (sales via storefronts, stock via Chari, patients via DabaDoc, parcels via Sendit), not a "manage your business better" pitch. This fits the HNB finding that Moroccan buyers do not value automation for its own sake.
- "Built by e-merchants for e-merchants" (Sendit) and YouCan's trainer ecosystem suggest community credibility substitutes for brand advertising in Moroccan SME segments.
- Chari's path (ordering → credit ledger → payment licence → BaaS) shows that the long-term revenue engine is licence-gated payments and finance. A new SaaS will likely need to *partner* with a licensed PSP (Chari's own BaaS, or a new acquirer after the CMI opening) rather than build that layer itself.

## 4. Case studies of product-led / viral SMB software in comparable emerging markets

### Takeaway
The best-documented loop is the **customer-facing artifact sent over WhatsApp/SMS**. OkCredit reports about 3 in 10 reminder recipients are merchants themselves and about 2 organic merchants per paid one. Khatabook onboarded 400K+ businesses organically in a year. The strongest *monetising* cases in MENA and LatAm, however, are (a) **compliance mandates** (ZATCA → Qoyod 25K businesses, Wafeq 10K customers and 2M invoices/month) and (b) **software as a wedge into payments/GMV** (Foodics $13B GMV in 2025, Fresha with free software and payments revenue, Mercado Pago QR, Nuvemshop). Field-agent networks (Moniepoint's 40K agents, Wave's 150K agents) dominate where cash is king.

### Inferences
- The OkCredit numbers imply the reminder loop alone is **sub-viral but strongly subsidising**: about 2 organic per paid merchant lowers blended CAC by about two-thirds, but K is below 1. The loop works because a large share of a shop's customers are themselves small businesses. In Morocco this holds best in B2B-heavy segments (wholesalers ↔ hanouts, suppliers ↔ restaurants, COD sellers ↔ couriers), not in consumer retail.
- MENA evidence suggests compliance plus payments, not ledgers, are where SMB software reaches durable scale and revenue (Qoyod, Wafeq, Foodics, Paymob).
- In cash-heavy markets, the scaled players (Moniepoint, Wave, Chari) all rely on physical agent networks. "Self-marketing" products in Morocco should plan for at least a light agent, ambassador or integrator layer.

## 5. Which of these failed or stalled, and why? Lessons on monetization

### Takeaway
The free digital-ledger category acquired users virally in India and Indonesia but struggled to monetise. Khatabook's FY24 revenue was ₹102.7 Cr against a ₹116 Cr loss. BukuKas/Lummo liquidated in 2023 after about four strategy changes. BukuWarung shrank its payments business amid fraud (anecdotal). Dukaan moved upmarket away from kiranas, and Treinta appears to have refocused on US SMBs. Survivors monetise through payments, GMV or compliance rather than subscriptions.

- **Treinta:** now described as serving "SMBs in the US and Latin America." — [Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/treinta)
- **Wave counter-example:** zero or low fees won share but cut agent income from about 15,000 to about 6,000 CFA/day and forced industry-wide fee cuts. Price as the product carries ecosystem costs. — [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2022/how-wave-is-disrupting-francophone-africas-mobile-money-market/)

### Inferences
- **Lesson 1:** a free ledger with a viral reminder loop is a great *acquisition* engine but a poor *business* on its own. Khatabook earned about ₹100 Cr (≈$12M) per year at peak against about 10M active users, and Lummo liquidated. Plan the monetisation layer (payments, collections, credit, compliance, GMV) before scaling the free loop.
- **Lesson 2:** payments revenue in these companies is offset by gateway costs (Khatabook's other expenses +51%) and fraud (BukuWarung, anecdotal). In Morocco, the 0.5% interchange cap and licence requirements (Chari needed a BAM licence) make payment take-rates thin and slow to get. Partner with a licensed PSP rather than become one early.
- **Lesson 3:** several kirana/warung-focused companies drifted upmarket (Dukaan → D2C/enterprise; Treinta → US SMBs). In Morocco, the micro-merchant tier may serve better as a free *distribution* layer for a paid tier aimed at formal SMEs, fiduciaires and VAT-registered firms that the e-invoicing mandate will affect.

### Gaps
- Kippa's fate (pivot or shutdown) and OkCredit's recent financials were not found.
- No primary post-mortem from Lummo founders; the cause analysis rests on opinion pieces.

## 6. Mechanics catalog: built-in distribution mechanics, proof cases and growth data

### Takeaway
The mechanics with real numbers behind them: (1) customer-facing artifacts over WhatsApp/SMS (OkCredit: 30% of recipients are merchants, about 2 organic per paid); (2) compliance mandates (ZATCA → Qoyod 25K, Wafeq 10K); (3) software → payments/GMV (Foodics $13B GMV; Fresha's free software); (4) educator/affiliate ecosystems (YouCan $753M GMV, 4,500-person events, 20–50% lifetime revenue share); (5) field-agent hybrids (Moniepoint 40K agents). ### Inferences
- **Highest-fit mechanics for Morocco** (by evidence strength × local conditions):
  1. *WhatsApp artifact loops* (#1 + #2): reminders, invoices, order and COD confirmations and delivery-tracking links sent in Darija/French over WhatsApp, each carrying the brand and a one-tap "get this for your business" link. Best in B2B-dense chains (wholesaler → hanout, supplier → café/restaurant, COD seller → courier → end buyer).
  2. *E-invoicing mandate plus the fiduciaire multiplier* (#3 + #4): position as DGI-connected and free or near-free for micro-firms and auto-entrepreneurs, paid for SMEs, with a fiduciaire multi-client console and partner revenue share (KHABIR already pays partner rates).

  4. *Subsidy-backed integrator channel* (#10): get on Mowakaba's eligible list so integrators sell annual packs at roughly 10–20% of list price to the SME.
  5. *Payments/collections as the monetisation layer* (#5): through a licensed partner (Chari BaaS or a post-CMI acquirer), since subscriptions alone failed for ledger apps.

## 7. Monetization in low-ARPU markets: freemium conversion, pricing tiers, payments/finance as the real revenue

### Takeaway
Global freemium benchmarks are 3–5% "good" and 6–8% typical for self-serve, with sales-assisted freemium around 10–15%. Emerging-market ledger apps show that converting micro-merchants to subscriptions does not reach venture scale. Profitable emerging-market SMB platforms make most of their money from transaction flows (payments, GMV, collections, credit) or mandatory compliance. In Morocco, observed SaaS price points are 0–350 MAD/month for TPE/POS tools, and subsidies may cover 80–90% of a packaged sale.

### Cited Findings
- **Freemium benchmarks:** a ChartMogul/ProductLed survey of 200 B2B products found 25% convert free-to-paid below 2.5% and another 25% at 10–15%. It suggests 3–5% is good and 8–12% great for freemium with regular sign-up. — [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)
- Lenny Rachitsky's data (1,000+ products), relayed secondhand: self-serve freemium 6–8%, sales-assisted freemium 10–15%. — [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-to-premium/)
- Products that cap *usage* (seats, volume) reportedly convert 1.5–2x better than those that cap *features* (single secondary source). — [Artisan Growth Strategies](https://www.artisangrowthstrategies.com/blog/freemium-conversion-rate-benchmarks)
- **Emerging-market price points:** Vyapar charges about ₹499–₹3,399/year (≈$6–$40/year) for premium on a free mobile base. — [Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.in/software/180579/vyapar). In Morocco, TPE invoicing runs 0–300 MAD/month and POS about 350 MAD/month. — [WINS](https://wins.ma/index.php/2026/08/27/logiciel_de_facturation_tpe_maroc/); [Oasis](https://oasistechnocloud.com/blog/guide-logiciel-gestion-entreprise-maroc/). Saudi Qoyod uses tiered monthly and annual plans with a 14-day no-card trial. — [yespress Qoyod](https://yespress.io/qoyod)
- **Ledger subscription economics:** Khatabook's revenue of ₹102.7 Cr in FY24 against a ₹116 Cr loss, with planned monetisation through financial services and payment commissions. — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2024/11/ms-dhoni-backed-khatabook-clocks-rs-1027-cr-revenue-cuts-losses-7-in-fy24); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=157806)
- **Payments/GMV as revenue:** Foodics' revenue growth (+29% in H1 2025) coincided with surging fintech adoption on $6B in H1 GMV. — [MENAbytes](https://www.menabytes.com/foodics-h1-numbers/). Fresha's software is free, with revenue from payments and marketplace fees. — [EntrepreneurPlus](https://entrepreneurplus.co.uk/how-freshas-saas-business-model-went-from-being-free-to-1-billion/). BukuWarung's first income came from digital payments. — [DailySocial](https://en.dailysocial.id/post/financial-record-application-business-opportunities-for-stalls). Paymob grew revenue 3x in 18 months. — [Entrepreneur ME](https://mena.entrepreneur.com/growth-strategies/we-got-funded-egypt-based-paymob-raises-a-us35-million-pre-series-c-round-co-led-by-mubadala-and-ebrd)
- **Moroccan payments constraints:** the domestic interchange cap is 0.5% (possibly 0.15% for proximity merchants). A payment-institution licence from BAM is needed for acquiring, IBANs and cards; Chari was the first VC-backed startup to get one. — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/10/341181/bank-al-maghrib-cuts-bank-card-payment-commission-cap-to-0-5/); [Bitget summary](https://www.bitget.com/amp/news/detail/12560605015955)
- **Two-sided monetisation:** DabaDoc charges the professional side (doctor subscription) and the institutional side (insurer commission) and keeps consumers free. — [Liners](https://liners.com/dabadoc)
- **Embedded credit:** Chari has a "credit ledger" for merchants and piloted BNPL around 2022. — [Chari Group](https://chari.group/en)

### Inferences
- For a Moroccan SaaS, a realistic paid conversion from a free micro-merchant base is likely at the low end of global benchmarks (≤3%), given cash preference and low perceived automation value (HNB). This is an inference; no Moroccan conversion data was found. Monetisation should therefore stack: (a) a paid tier for formal SMEs and fiduciaires, especially for mandate compliance; (b) per-transaction revenue from collections or payment links via a licensed partner; (c) partner revenue (insurer, supplier, lender) in the Chari/DabaDoc pattern.
- Annual prepaid pricing sold through integrators and subsidised by Mowakaba may beat monthly card billing for the SME tier. Monthly WhatsApp-paid micro-plans (bank transfer or cash agent) may suit micro-merchants.
- The thin interchange margin (0.5%) means payments revenue in Morocco has to come from value-added services (collections, reconciliation, COD settlement speed, working-capital credit), not raw processing spread.

### Gaps
- No Moroccan freemium-to-paid conversion data, ARPU data or churn benchmarks found.
- No data on mobile-money (MT Cash, Orange Money, inwi money) use for paying B2B software subscriptions in Morocco.
- The OpenView PLG benchmark was not directly retrieved; only secondary citations were found.
- Not covered due to search budget exhaustion: Moroccan BNPL/merchant-credit regulation, and current Khatabook/OkCredit revenue mix (payments versus lending versus subscription).
