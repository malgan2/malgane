# Gap research: competitors in Moroccan booking and customer-facing verticals (beauty, driving schools, private schools, cafés/restaurants)

Scope note: this file closes gaps left in `vertical_niches.md` sections 2, 4, 5 and 13, and adversarially tests the claim that these verticals are underserved. Facts already in that file (salon count, Glovo terms, private-school pupil estimates, NARSA exam reform) are not repeated except where new evidence changes them. All research used web-search results only (25 searches; page fetches were blocked), so most findings rest on search snippets and vendor pages. Vendor or self-published claims are labelled **(vendor claim)**. Sponsored content is labelled **(sponsored)**.

## Beauty (salons, barbers, hammams, spas): who already serves it, pricing and traction?

### Takeaway
The earlier note said "no strong local booking leader surfaced". That is still true, but the space is **not empty**. Besides Fresha (global, free tier, live in Casablanca and Marrakech), at least **six Moroccan consumer booking marketplaces** exist: RendezVoo, Mawid, Salonia, Ello, Kwipa and Planner. There is also at least one local POS (QuickCom) that sells to salons. None of them shows any visible traction: no funding, no press coverage and no published salon counts were found. Booksy, Planity and Treatwell show no sign of being in Morocco. The verdict is **crowded with weak, unproven players** rather than underserved. The number of thin clones suggests that building the product is easy, while getting salons to adopt it and pay for it is hard.

### Inferences
- **The earlier "underserved" claim is only half right.** No incumbent has traction, but there are at least six local marketplaces plus Fresha. A new entrant would be the 8th or 9th booking app, not the first. Several thin clones with no funding or press usually means the **consumer-marketplace model is struggling to reach liquidity**: salons keep taking bookings by WhatsApp or Instagram DM, and clients do not install a booking app for one salon.
- Salonia's "propose your price" model and its on-site, cash-only payment show the local reality: **price haggling and cash**. That cuts against the Fresha/Planity model of pre-payment and card deposits.
- **Viral touchpoint:** a salon's booking link in its Instagram bio or WhatsApp status is a strong touchpoint in theory. In practice the loop carries the *salon's* brand to clients, who are not buyers of the software. The loop that matters for growth (salon to other salons) is weak, and the marketplace side is split across 7+ apps. The most defensible wedge is probably a **WhatsApp-native salon tool** (reminders, deposits via local payment rails, a client book) sold to the salon, not another consumer marketplace. Mawid already advertises WhatsApp reminders, so even that is not unique.
- Willingness to pay is likely below 200 DH/month: QuickCom's entry price is 199 DH/month, and Fresha's base tier is free.

### Gaps
- No salon counts, booking volumes, funding or founding dates for RendezVoo, Mawid, Salonia, Ello, Kwipa or Planner. App-store download counts were not visible in the snippets.
- The number of Moroccan venues on Fresha is unconfirmed (the single "5 venues in Marrakesh" reading is unverified).
- SimplyBook.me, Setmore, Square Appointments and Zenoti usage in Morocco was not checked within the search budget.
- Bank or telecom bundles aimed at salons (e.g., payment terminals with booking) were not searched.

## Driving schools (auto-écoles): how many, what software, exam scheduling with NARSA, theory-prep apps?

### Takeaway
**No count of auto-écoles was found** in French or Arabic sources. Officials and NARSA describe a sector with too many schools: a former transport minister said "supply exceeds demand". Dedicated management software is almost absent: only **WimDrive** (Rabat) surfaced, plus generic international schedulers. The candidate-facing side is not empty, though. The **state is building the core rail**: NARSA piloted a new digital platform for booking exam appointments from 15 June to 24 Aug 2026, with candidate registration from 1 Sept 2026, and runs the Perminou e-learning platform. Several independent theory-prep apps (Siya9a and others) already cover the 1,000-question bank. Verdict: **back-office SaaS is strongly underserved, but the market is small and fragmented, and it depends on regulators**. The student-facing app is partly served by free state tools and free apps.

### Cited Findings
**Software players**
- **WimDrive** is an online driving-school management tool based in Rabat (enrolments and candidate tracking), listed on GoAfrica. No pricing or client numbers were found **(directory listing)** — [GoAfrica WimDrive](https://www.goafricaonline.com/ma/user/330733-wimdrive-ar)
- International driving-school and scheduling tools appear in Capterra's category (Colibri, EasyWeek, SimplyBook.me, Acuity Scheduling) with lesson scheduling, instructor management and reminders. None is Morocco-specific or NARSA-integrated — [Capterra FR driving-school software](https://www.capterra.fr/directory/31328/driving-school/software)

**NARSA and the state platforms**

Candidate registration through the platform was expected **from 1 Sept 2026**, and existing candidates' data would migrate automatically from the old platform — [Hespress AR](https://www.hespress.com/1762782-1762782.html)
- **Earlier platform (2024):** a portal was meant to let auto-écoles manage appointments and authorisations remotely. Professionals complained that the appointment system regularly crashed under demand. In 2024 NARSA closed booking for a March session early (23 Feb) so that schools could process the backlog. Data from 2024 — [Le Matin 2024](https://lematin.ma/societe/permis-de-conduire-ce-qui-va-changer-a-partir-du-18-mars-2024/215571); [Hespress FR 2024](https://fr.hespress.com/359359-obtenir-le-permis-de-conduire-est-il-devenu-impossible-la-narsa-rassure.html); [Hespress FR 2024](https://fr.hespress.com/360612-crise-du-permis-de-conduire-les-professionnels-des-auto-ecoles-ne-decolerent-pas-apres-le-scandale.html)
- **PERMINOU-NARSA** was announced in 2024 as an e-learning platform and a collaboration space for auto-écoles, instructors and candidates. Its stability was questioned (2024) — [TelQuel 2024](https://telquel.ma/instant-t/2024/02/28/la-narsa-annonce-une-reforme-majeure-de-lexamen-du-permis-de-conduire_1859199/); [Le Desk](https://ledesk.ma/enclair/nouvel-examen-du-permis-de-conduire-la-narsa-a-la-peine-face-au-fiasco/)
- A Hespress headline reports Moroccan auto-écoles "paralysed by a blockage at the Trésorerie Générale", which suggests exam or fee payments run through the TGR. Only the title was seen; the date was not visible — [Hespress FR](https://fr.hespress.com/429473-429473.html)
- The planned reform covers opening conditions, candidate training, **vehicle management and the digitalisation of administrative procedures**. Some measures await implementing texts. A search extract dates the article to July 2026, but the date is unverified — [Hespress FR](https://fr.hespress.com/482884-auto-ecoles-vers-une-reforme-de-la-formation-et-des-conditions-dagrement.html)
- A draft amendment to the Highway Code (law 52-05), put to public consultation in Feb 2025, covers driving licences, auto-écoles, technical inspection and medical certificates — [Médias24, Feb 2025](https://medias24.com/2025/02/13/code-de-la-route-de-nouveaux-changements-vont-etre-introduits-dans-la-loi-52-05/)
- The Government Council approved an amendment to decree 2.10.432 on driving instruction on 21 Sept 2023. New conditions cover classrooms, driving simulators and a **maximum of 16 trainees per classroom** — [Hespress AR](https://www.hespress.com/1319920-1319920.html)
- An Akhbarona headline says the Transport Ministry is regulating the price of taking the driving exam ("the era of speculation is over"). Only the title was seen; the date is unknown — [Akhbarona](https://www.akhbarona.com/mobile/economy/407015.html). In June 2025 NARSA also urged schools to respect reference pricing — [SNRT News](https://snrtnews.com/fr/article/auto-ecoles-la-narsa-rappelle-les-regles-du-jeu-126983)

**Sector size**
- Former transport minister Mohamed Abdeljalil (in office 2021–2024) told the auto-école union that schools had multiplied so much that **"supply now exceeds demand"**, alongside a shortage of instructors. This is a search-summary extract; I could not confirm which of these articles carries the quote — [Hespress AR](https://www.hespress.com/1210739-1210739.html); [SNRT AR](https://snrtnews.com/article/83999)
- **No national count of auto-écoles was found** in French or Arabic searches — [Hespress AR](https://www.hespress.com/1319920-1319920.html); [SNRT FR](https://snrtnews.com/fr/article/auto-ecoles-la-narsa-rappelle-les-regles-du-jeu-126983)

**Theory-prep apps (B2C substitutes)**
- **Siya9a maroc** (App Store) simulates the category-B exam with questions updated for the latest Highway Code changes **(developer claim)** — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6745274348)
- **siya9a 2026** (App Store) offers 1,000+ questions in 26 mock tests with a 30-second timer, for categories B, C and D **(developer claim)** — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6748256787)
- "Code de la route 2025" is another App Store prep app — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/code-de-la-rout%D0%B5-2025/id1538616065?l=ko)
- No official state theory-prep app was found apart from the Perminou platform (above) — [Hespress AR](https://www.hespress.com/1762782-1762782.html)

### Inferences
- **The back office is underserved:** only one local SaaS (WimDrive) surfaced, and nothing integrates with NARSA's 2026 booking platform or the TGR payment flow. The reform pushes in the same direction (fleet minimums, digitalised procedures, simulators, class-size caps). This **supports the earlier claim** for school-side management software.
- **Exam scheduling is a state-owned rail.** NARSA's new platform (Sept 2026) is the system of record. A private tool can only sit beside it (candidate files, document checklists, lesson scheduling, payment instalments, instructor rosters), unless NARSA opens an API, and no evidence of one was found.
- **Candidate-side virality is weaker than it looks.** Each student is a one-off customer (one licence), and free prep apps plus Perminou already exist. A student app spreads to friends who also need a licence, a modest loop, but it does not spread from school to school. Distribution would need to go through the auto-école union or federation, or through NARSA-driven compliance.
- **Market size risk:** with no count and an official view that there are too many schools, the reform (deposit raised from 20k to 100k MAD, at least 2 cars) may **shrink** the number of schools before it professionalises them. The total addressable market could be a few thousand schools paying low monthly fees.

### Gaps
- The number of licensed auto-écoles (NARSA or Transport Ministry data) was not found.
- WimDrive pricing and client count; any other local tools (desktop software sold by local IT firms) were not found.
- Whether NARSA's 2026 platform exposes any integration for third-party software, and whether it went live on 1 Sept 2026 as planned (no post-pilot report found).
- Download counts and monetisation of the Siya9a-type apps were not visible.

## Private schools: what software do they use, at what price, and do parents pay fees online?

### Takeaway
The vertical is **partly served** and more competitive than the earlier "fragmented, no clear leader" note implied. There are **two VC- or fund-backed Moroccan players**: Schoolify (3 MDH raised, claims 340+ schools) and KoolSkools ($960k from the public-private Azur Innovation Fund, 2023). Others include **Eduka**, which advertises CMI e-commerce integration for online fee payment, OpenEduCat, Pronote (used in mission/French-curriculum schools), school-branded parent apps (OUHOUD, IFIAG) and custom builds sold on Avito. The state's **Massar** information system has had parent and student mobile apps since 2021, which covers grades and absences for free. No MAD pricing was found for any school-management product. There is little evidence that parents pay fees online at scale.

### Cited Findings
**Local and funded players**
- **Schoolify** (Casablanca Technopark, launched 2021) offers a cloud platform for school administration, communication and academic follow-up. It raised **3 MDH** from business angels and a Swiss fund linked to Hedera Hashgraph. Founder Sokaina Sayouri said that **more than 340 schools worldwide** had deployed it two years after launch. The TelQuel piece is in its **sponsored** section, and the school count is a **founder claim** — [FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-entreprises/schoolify); [TelQuel (sponsored)](https://telquel.ma/sponsors/technopark-schoolify-leve-3-mdh-pour-digitaliser-leducation_1896430)
- **KoolSkools** raised **$960k (about 10M MAD)** from the Azur Innovation Fund (Feb 2023). It digitises courses and school life: student records, absences, report cards and **payments** — [Wamda, Feb 2023](https://www.wamda.com/2023/02/koolskools-raises-960000-azur-fund); [Africa Private Equity News](https://www.africaprivateequitynews.com/p/morocco-azur-innovation-fund-in-edtech-deal)
- **Eduka** advertises a **CMI ECOM integration** so that Moroccan schools can collect tuition and service fees online by Moroccan card **(vendor claim)** — [Eduka CMI integration](https://www.edukasoftware.com/integration/integration-cmi-ecom/)

- **OpenEduCat** markets Morocco-specific pages for K-12, international and Quranic (Atiq) schools: Arabic, French and English UI, MAD fee modules, Hijri and Gregorian calendars and ministry-format records. - Custom or local builds are sold through classifieds, e.g., an online admissions management system advertised on Avito in Rabat — [Avito](https://www.avito.ma/fr/rabat/cours_et_formations/Logiciel_de_gestion_scolaire_57974206.htm)
- Kezakoo (tutoring, B2C) raised 2 MDH from WITAMAX One in 2021. It is not school-management software, but it is an edtech reference point (2021) — [FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualites-marocaines/soutien-scolaire-la-startup-kezakoo-reussit-une-premiere-levee-de-fonds-de-2-mdh)
- In July 2024 Morocco invited foreign edtech companies to bid on tenders to digitise education (title-level evidence) — [Launch Base Africa, Jul 2024](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2024/07/23/digitizing-education-morocco-invites-foreign-edtech-companies-to-submit-tenders/)

**Parent apps**
- The **OUHOUD** school group (Casablanca) has its own parent app with timetable, absences, exams, grades and messaging with the administration — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/mx/app/id1441312979)
- The **IFIAG** app ("Morocco and Africa") offers attendance tracking with absence notifications, grades and **tuition-payment status** — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ph/app/ifiag/id6468363675)
- **Massar**, the national student information system run by the Education Ministry, launched student, parent and teacher mobile apps in **March 2021**. This is a search-summary extract; the source is likely the EcoActu article below — [EcoActu](https://ecoactu.ma/?p=75754)

**Fees and payments**
- The 2023 standard contract between private schools and families requires schools to give parents a copy of the insurance contract **and a payment invoice**, and parents need not pay for transport or canteen they do not use — [Médias24, Aug 2023](https://medias24.com/2023/08/31/les-principales-dispositions-du-contrat-type-regulant-la-relation-entre-les-ecoles-privees-et-les-familles/)
- Fee disputes are a recurring flashpoint. During Covid (2020), schools demanded May and June fees and the parents' federation FNAPEM refused "blackmail" (2020) — [Hespress FR](https://fr.hespress.com/146363-enseignement-les-ecoles-privees-exigent-le-paiement-de-mai-et-juin-la-fnapem-refuse-le-chantage.html)
- In private higher education, fees are usually paid in 2–3 instalments, with a discount for paying in full — [9rayti](https://www.9rayti.com/dossier/frais-scolarite-ecoles-superieures-privees-maroc)
- No list or statistic of K-12 private schools that accept online card or CMI payment was found. Eduka's CMI integration is the only concrete online-payment offer seen — [Eduka](https://www.edukasoftware.com/integration/integration-cmi-ecom/)

**Market size (new data and conflicts)**
- Rentrée 2025-26: about **8.27M pupils**, of whom **7.4M are in public schools** in more than 12,441 public establishments — [Maroc.ma](https://www.maroc.ma/index%2ephp/fr/actualites/rentree-scolaire-2025-2026-environ-827-millions-deleves-rejoignent-les-bancs-de-lecole); [AllAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202509090610.html). Subtracting gives **about 870k non-public pupils** (derived). This **conflicts** with the earlier file's derived 1.1M for 2024-25. The scopes probably differ (e.g., whether public preschool is counted in the public total).
- Older official figures: **741,523 private pupils and 3,656 private establishments** (2014, minister Belmokhtar) — [Médias24, 2014](https://medias24.com/2014/05/21/740-000-eleves-dans-les-ecoles-privees-marocaines/); about 1M pupils and about 5,200 establishments (2019) — [Le360, 2019](https://fr.le360.ma/societe/education-voici-ce-que-pese-lenseignement-prive-au-maroc-189555/)
- Local example: Essaouira province had 17 private establishments vs 207 public in 2024/25, which shows private schooling is concentrated in large cities — [AllAfrica, Sept 2024](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202409060220.html)
- Education International notes that the growth of private schools has created three tiers of stratification, so the market is far from homogeneous — [EI-IE](https://www.ei-ie.org/file/168)

### Inferences
- **The earlier claim of "no clear Moroccan leader" holds, but "underserved" overstates it.** Two funded local players (Schoolify, KoolSkools), a CMI-integrated vendor (Eduka), Pronote at the top end and cheap custom builds at the bottom mean a new entrant faces competition in every tier. None appears dominant, and none publishes prices.
- **Massar is a free substitute for the parent touchpoint** (grades and absences). A private parent app must win on what Massar does not do: **fee invoicing and collection, messaging, transport, canteen and receipts**. The 2023 standard contract (mandatory invoice; à-la-carte transport and canteen) supports billing as the wedge.
- **Virality:** the parent app is a daily, high-engagement touchpoint, but parents do not choose the software. It spreads school to school through owners and directors (associations, school groups), not parent to school. Parents do expose the brand, though, and a parent can ask "why doesn't our school have this app?".
- Online fee payment looks early-stage, with only one vendor offering CMI integration. A **fee-collection plus reconciliation** layer (CMI or new acquirers, instant transfer, cash agents) may be the most differentiated angle, the same pattern as in syndic research.

### Gaps
- No MAD pricing (per pupil or per school) for Schoolify, KoolSkools, Eduka, OpenEduCat or Pronote in Morocco.
- Schoolify and KoolSkools current status in 2025-26 (still operating? further rounds? Moroccan school count vs "worldwide").
- "Edusoft" and "Scolarite.ma" (named in the brief): **no search result surfaced either name** in a Moroccan school context.
- Whether Massar offers any API or official data-exchange for private-school software beyond import/export.
- Share of parents paying fees online; whether banks (e.g., via CMI's Fatourati) list private schools as billers.

## Cafés and restaurants: POS and QR-menu players, pricing in MAD

### Takeaway
This vertical is **well served, even commoditised, at the tool level**. At least **seven Moroccan vendors publish MAD prices** for QR menus or POS: QR menus at **250–1,700 DH/year**, and POS plus QR menu at **149–299 DH/month**. Alongside them are LaCaisse.ma (about 1,000 clients, Glovo integration), Inyad, Odoo POS, freelancers selling QR menus and one-off Android POS packs at **2,800–3,500 DH all-in** on Avito. The "direct ordering, zero commission" angle that the earlier note flagged after the end of Glovo exclusivity is **already marketed** by Mymenu ("sans commission"). The opportunity is not underserved. What remains open is payments and deeper integration (delivery aggregation, e-invoicing compliance), not basic POS or QR menus.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **The earlier claim does not hold for this vertical.** The gap left in `vertical_niches.md` (no MAD pricing found) is now closed: the market has many local vendors at very low prices (Mymenu 149 DH/month, MyQR 250 DH/year). That signals a **commoditised, price-competitive** segment, made worse by cheap one-off Android packs that many cafés may prefer to subscriptions.
- The **direct-ordering, zero-commission** value proposition (opened up by the end of Glovo exclusivity in Sept 2025) already has local vendors (Mymenu, QResto, tacMenu). LaCaisse.ma owns the Glovo integration.
- **Virality:** QR menus and receipts expose the vendor to *diners*, who do not buy POS software. Spread from owner to owner is weak. The touchpoint is real but monetises poorly unless it carries **payments** (pay at the table, tips) or a consumer ordering network. Both need a licensed PSP partner and heavy marketing.
- The remaining differentiation is likely to come from: (a) **e-invoicing compliance** for restaurants above thresholds (2026–2027 phasing); (b) multi-aggregator delivery integration (Glovo plus others after exclusivity) in one tablet; (c) payments and financing. These are harder plays that incumbents (LaCaisse, Inyad) are better placed to make.

## Cross-vertical verdict: how underserved is each vertical, and how naturally would a product spread?

### Takeaway
Tested adversarially, the earlier "underserved" picture **weakens for three of four verticals**. Cafés and restaurants are **well served or commoditised**. Beauty is **crowded with weak players** (Fresha plus at least 6 local marketplaces, none with proven traction). Private schools are **partly served** (two funded local players, a CMI-integrated vendor, Pronote and the free Massar parent app). **Driving schools are the only one that remains strongly underserved on software**, but they are the smallest and most regulator-dependent: NARSA owns exam booking (new platform from Sept 2026), free prep apps exist, and the number of schools is unknown and may shrink under the reform.

- Beauty: Fresha live in Morocco — [Fresha](https://www.fresha.com/lp/en/bt/spas/in/ma-casablanca); local marketplaces RendezVoo, Mawid, Salonia, Ello, Kwipa and Planner — [RendezVoo](https://www.rendezvoo.ma/); [Mawid](https://mawidapp.com/); [Salonia](https://salonia-maroc.com/); [Ello](https://ello.ma/); [Kwipa](https://www.kwipa.ma/); [Planner](https://apps.apple.com/kh/app/planner/id6479702256)

- Private schools: Schoolify (3 MDH) — [FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-entreprises/schoolify); KoolSkools ($960k) — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2023/02/koolskools-raises-960000-azur-fund); Eduka with CMI — [Eduka](https://www.edukasoftware.com/integration/integration-cmi-ecom/)

### Inferences
| Vertical | Incumbents found (2024–2026) | Price points seen | Verdict | Customer-facing touchpoint | Does the touchpoint drive B2B spread? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beauty | Fresha (free tier); RendezVoo, Mawid, Salonia, Ello, Kwipa, Planner (local marketplaces, no traction data); QuickCom POS (salon plan) | QuickCom from 199 DH/mo; Fresha free | **Partly served (crowded, weak players)** | Booking link in Instagram bio or WhatsApp; reminders | Weak to moderate: clients see the salon's brand; marketplace split 7+ ways |
| Driving schools | WimDrive only; NARSA platforms (booking 2026, Perminou); free Siya9a-type prep apps | None published | **Strongly underserved (back office), small and regulator-dependent** | Student app, mock tests, payment-instalment reminders | Weak: one-off students; spread needs the federation or NARSA |
| Private schools | Schoolify, KoolSkools (funded); Eduka (CMI); OpenEduCat; Pronote; school-built apps; Massar parent app (state, free) | None published | **Partly served** | Daily parent app, fee invoices and receipts | Moderate: parents expose the brand, but school owners decide |
| Cafés/restaurants | LaCaisse.ma (about 1,000 clients, Glovo integration), Inyad, QResto, QuickCom, Mymenu, tacMenu, Menuquick, MyQR, All In Table, Odoo, Avito one-off packs | 149–299 DH/mo POS; 250–1,700 DH/yr QR menus; 2,800–3,500 DH one-off | **Well served (commoditised)** | QR menu, order link, receipt | Weak: diners are not buyers |

- **Pattern across verticals:** where the touchpoint is cheap to build (QR menus, booking pages), Morocco already has many small local clones at very low prices. The bottleneck is **distribution and willingness to pay**, not product availability. That challenges the thesis that customer-facing touchpoints alone will make a product spread.
- The verticals where a state system is the system of record (NARSA for driving, Massar for schools) limit how much a private product can own. They also create integration and compliance wedges if APIs ever open.
- Of these four, the most credible openings are: (1) a **fee-billing and collection layer for private schools** (invoice mandated by the 2023 contract; only Eduka shows CMI integration); (2) **auto-école back office** around the 2026 NARSA platform and the reform, if the school count proves large enough. Beauty and restaurants look like poor bets for a new standalone SaaS unless the entrant has a payments or distribution advantage.
