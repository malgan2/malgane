## 1. Demographics, connectivity, devices and languages

### Takeaway
Morocco is a 36.8M-person, 63% urban, mobile-first market: about 92% of people are online (35.5M users, late 2025), mobile lines exceed the population (160% penetration), and 5G launched in November 2025. Darija dominates speech and most digital content. French is still the written business language (57.7% of literate people can read and write it), and English is a growing third language among young and affluent users (20.5%).

### Cited Findings
**Population and urbanization (HCP census RGPH 2024)**
- Population was 36,828,330 on 1 Sep 2024: 36,680,178 Moroccans and 148,152 foreigners. Average annual growth slowed to 0.85% (from 1.25% in 2004–2014) — [Hespress FR / HCP](https://fr.hespress.com/401944-hcp-la-croissance-demographique-ralentit-a-085-et-le-chomage-atteint-21-en-2024.html); [HCP](https://m.hcp.ma/file/242665)
- Urbanization rate: 62.8% in 2024, up from 60.4% in 2014 and 51.4% in 1994. Urban population is 23,110,108 and rural about 13.72M — [Hespress FR / HCP](https://fr.hespress.com/401944-hcp-la-croissance-demographique-ralentit-a-085-et-le-chomage-atteint-21-en-2024.html); [HCP](https://m.hcp.ma/file/242665)
- About 71.2% of the population lives in five regions, led by Casablanca-Settat — [HCP](https://m.hcp.ma/file/242665)
- The census-based unemployment rate reached 21% in 2024 — [Hespress FR](https://fr.hespress.com/401944-hcp-la-croissance-demographique-ralentit-a-085-et-le-chomage-atteint-21-en-2024.html)

**Internet and mobile (DataReportal Digital 2026, data as of late 2025)**
- Internet users: about 35.5M, or 92.2% of the population, measured at end of October 2025 (a record) — [DataReportal Digital 2026: Morocco](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-morocco); [Hespress EN](https://en.hespress.com/125289-125289.html); [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2025/11/267078/morocco-2026-digital-report-92-of-the-population-now-online/)
- Active cellular connections: 57.1M, or 148% of the population, reflecting multi-SIM use. About 87.7% of these are broadband (3G/4G/5G) — [Hespress EN](https://en.hespress.com/125146-125146.html); [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-morocco)

**Regulator data (ANRT, end of December 2025)**
- Mobile lines: about 59.16M. Penetration is 160.65%, up from 158.26% a year earlier — [Finances News Hebdo](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/telephone-mobile-maroc-2025-decembre); [Médias24](https://medias24.com/2026/03/16/telephonie-mobile-le-maroc-depasse-59-millions-dabonnes-1643987/)
- Internet penetration (all access types): 112.59% at end 2025, against 109.21% in 2024 and 74.1% in 2019 — [La Vie Éco](https://www.lavieeco.com/?p=446156)
- Mobile internet penetration: 108.23% at end of March 2025 (ANRT observatory dashboard) — [Casainvest telecom sector PDF](https://www.casainvest.ma/sites/default/files/2025-11/Te%CC%81le%CC%81communications.pdf)
- 5G launched commercially in November 2025. A March 2026 Médias24 headline says 5G users already exceed "2,6/26 millions". The URL slug reads "26-millions", which is most likely 2.6M with the decimal comma dropped. The article itself could not be opened — [Médias24](https://medias24.com/2026/03/17/telecoms-lancee-en-novembre-la-5g-depasse-deja-les-26-millions-dutilisateurs-1644553/)
- ANRT's board targets nationwide 5G by 2030 and plans MAD 80bn of investment to strengthen high-speed broadband. Hespress also cites "nearly 40M internet users (~90%)", but that figure is undated — [Hespress FR](https://fr.hespress.com/435048-ca-de-lanrt-generalisation-de-la-5g-dici-2030-80-mmdh-pour-renforcer-le-haut-debit.html)

**Financial-inclusion survey data on phones and internet (World Bank Global Findex 2025, survey year 2024)**
- 90% of adults own a phone and 65% use the internet. Phone ownership is 87% among women and 93% among men. About 40% of phone owners use a SIM card not registered in their own name — [Hespress EN on Findex 2025](https://en.hespress.com/116233-116233.html)

**Devices (mostly old data)**
- 2019 (ANRT): 99.8% of Moroccans had a mobile phone, and 75.7% of those had a smartphone — [Morocco World News, 2019](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2019/07/78727/telecommunications-76-moroccans-smartphones/)
- 2019 survey: Android held a 72.5% share and iOS 16%. This is dated. The source is reported in [Médias24 Mobile Report 2019](https://www.medias24.com/mobile-report-2019-1031.html) / [Infomediaire, MNC-Averty mobile survey](https://www.infomediaire.net/?p=40070)
- Nearly 90% of Moroccans access the internet through a mobile device, and most network traffic comes from phones rather than desktops or laptops. This is DataReportal-based coverage — [Bladi.net](https://www.bladi.net/marocains-acces-internet-mobile,80420.html); [Atalayar](https://atalayar.com/en/content/mobile-and-social-media-use-morocco)

**Mobile data cost**
- 5G plan prices in November 2025: Maroc Telecom 80 GB for MAD 329, Orange 40 GB for MAD 199, inwi 35 GB for MAD 119. Across offers, 5G plans range from MAD 49 to MAD 699. That works out to about MAD 3.4–5.0 per GB (my calculation) — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2025/11/267863/5g-in-morocco-which-operator-currently-offers-the-best-value/)
- A Morocco World News analysis says some Moroccans spend "up to 8% of their salary" on internet — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2025/11/267271/a-moroccan-pays-up-to-8-of-their-salary-on-internet-and-not-even-for-real-5g/)
- Travel eSIM listings (July 2026) on Moroccan networks run about USD 0.50–1.20 per GB. These are foreign-sold packages, not local plans — [eSIMDB](https://esimdb.com/morocco)

**Languages**
- RGPH 2024 shares among literate residents aged 10 and over: Arabic 99.2%, French 57.7%, English 20.5%, Amazigh (Tifinagh literacy) 1.5%, Spanish 1.2%, other 1% — [HCP RGPH 2024](https://m.hcp.ma/file/242669); [Aujourd'hui le Maroc](https://aujourdhui.ma/?p=479825); [Le Desk datadesk](https://ledesk.ma/datadesk/capital-humain-logements-etablissements-economiques-les-principales-donnees-du-rgph-2024/)
- Urban vs rural: French 64.3% vs 42.1% and English 25.2% vs 9.6%. By region, French is 64.0% in Casablanca-Settat, 61.3% in Rabat-Salé-Kénitra, 60.1% in Souss-Massa and 47.9% in Tanger-Tétouan-Al Hoceima. About 24.8% of the population uses Amazigh in speech — [HCP RGPH 2024](https://m.hcp.ma/file/242669); [Le Desk](https://ledesk.ma/datadesk/capital-humain-logements-etablissements-economiques-les-principales-donnees-du-rgph-2024/)
- Sunergia survey (2024): Darija is the most used language in personal speech (94%) and professional speech (69%), and in personal writing (69%) and professional writing (39%). French leads in professional writing. About 7% use English professionally; English fluency is 17% among people under 34 and 22% in upper-income groups — [Yabiladi](https://en.yabiladi.com/articles/details/164733/darija-dominates-french-leads-professionally.html)
- Generational shift: French is fading and English growing among younger Moroccans — [Hespress EN](https://en.hespress.com/112190-report-french-fades-english-grows-as-morocco-sees-generational-language-shift.html). In a 2021 British Council survey, 74% agreed Morocco should adopt English instead of French as its main second language. That measures preference, not usage — [British Council Morocco, 2021](https://www.britishcouncil.ma/sites/default/files/shift_to_english_in_morocco_16042021_v2.pdf)
- In digital advertising, brands increasingly use Darija and users code-switch between Darija, French and English within the same content. French and Modern Standard Arabic keep their institutional prestige — [ResearchGate, 2025 study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394616194_RISE_OF_DARIJA_IN_MOROCCAN_DIGITAL_ADVERTISING_LANGUAGE_IDENTITY_AND_THE_POWER_OF_INFLUENCE)
- Business messaging in Casablanca and Rabat leans French, while the broader consumer market is more comfortable in Darija. This comes from a vendor blog — [ChatDaddy](https://chatdaddy.tech/blog/whatsapp-business-api-morocco)

### Inferences
- A SaaS product has to be mobile-first, assume Android, and tolerate low bandwidth. Desktop-only B2B tools will reach only a narrow layer of larger firms.
- The default language stack is a French UI for business and admin documents (invoices, contracts), Darija (Latin or Arabic script) for onboarding, support and marketing, and Arabic for official and public-facing output. English is mainly an asset with young or tech-savvy and export-oriented users.
- Demand is concentrated in Casablanca-Settat and Rabat-Salé-Kénitra, which have the highest French literacy and urbanization. They are the natural beachhead.

### Gaps
- No 2025/26 Android-vs-iOS share or mobile-vs-desktop traffic split was found. StatCounter and DataReportal's full tables could not be accessed. The 2019 figure (72.5% Android) is stale.
- No current smartphone-penetration figure was found. The ANRT 2024/2025 ICT household survey results were indexed without numbers.
- Median age and DataReportal's full table data (speeds, device share) were not retrieved.
- cable.co.uk's Morocco cost-per-GB ranking was not found.

## 2. Social and messaging platforms and how businesses use them

### Takeaway
WhatsApp is the dominant channel (about 75% of users in 2025, "dominant" again in 2026), followed by Facebook (about 22.8M users). YouTube (about 21.6M), TikTok (16.7M adults, 58% of 18–24-year-olds) and Instagram (about 15.1M) are growing fast, and Snapchat is rising (18%). Commerce runs through WhatsApp conversations and Instagram/TikTok/Facebook discovery. No hard data on WhatsApp Business adoption or live selling was found.

### Cited Findings
- Reported audience estimates from DataReportal's Digital 2026 (late 2025): Facebook about 22.8M users, YouTube 21.6M viewers, TikTok 16.7M adults (18+), Instagram 15.1M — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2025/11/267078/morocco-2026-digital-report-92-of-the-population-now-online/)
- One outlet reports about 23M social media users (about 59% of the population) in the Digital 2026 data. That share looks low next to Facebook's 22.8M, so check the definition — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2025/11/267078/morocco-2026-digital-report-92-of-the-population-now-online/); [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-morocco)
- Meta Ads API broad 18+ audience for Facebook and Instagram combined: 26,341,700 (March 2026) — [Affect Group](https://affectgroup.com/blog/morocco-facebook-instagram-audience-in-meta-ads-2026/)
- 2025 usage survey: WhatsApp 75%, Facebook 62%, Instagram 42%. Instagram gained 2 points while WhatsApp fell 1 and Facebook 3 — [Hespress FR](https://fr.hespress.com/429594-429594.html)
- Sunergia barometer 2026 (adoption): Facebook 64%, YouTube 48%, Instagram 47%, TikTok 32%. The coverage headline says social media usage reached 86% in 2026 and that WhatsApp leads — [Archyde](https://www.archyde.com/morocco-social-media-usage-hits-86-in-2026-whatsapp-leads-tiktok-surges/)
- September 2026: WhatsApp dominates; TikTok reaches 32% of users and 58% of 18–24-year-olds, with higher adoption among affluent groups; Snapchat is at 18% — [TelQuel](https://telquel.ma/instant-t/2026/09/10/reseaux-sociaux-whatsapp-domine-tiktok-et-snapchat-accelerent-au-maroc_2006830/)
- Older baselines: WhatsApp was used by 84% of Moroccans in 2021 — [TelQuel 2021](https://telquel.ma/2021/05/12/84-des-marocains-utilisent-whatsapp-en-2021_1722006). Another 2021 figure puts it at 73.7% of internet users aged 16–64 — [Atalayar](https://atalayar.com/en/content/mobile-and-social-media-use-morocco)
- Agency claims, lower confidence: TikTok has 8.2M+ monthly active users and Facebook 16.8M active users — [Webotic](https://webotic.ma/en/blog/tiktok-ads-maroc-guide-complet/). Estimates differ widely: TikTok runs from about 8M to 17M depending on source and definition.
- WhatsApp is described as the primary messaging platform across Moroccan business sectors, with claimed open rates above 90% (vendor content, no method given) — [ChatDaddy](https://chatdaddy.tech/blog/whatsapp-marketing-morocco)
- E-commerce merchants commonly run COD stores (often on YouCan) and confirm orders by phone or WhatsApp. COD affiliate networks plug into carrier APIs such as Sendit — [Affipartner](https://affipartner.com/); [Sentinel Studio 2026 guide](https://www.sentinelstudio.ma/journal/launch-ecommerce-morocco-2026-guide)

### Inferences
- WhatsApp is the de facto CRM and storefront for SMEs. SaaS that sits on WhatsApp (ordering, confirmation, reminders, invoicing, support) meets users where they already are, which an app that pulls them away does not.
- TikTok's reach among 18–24-year-olds (58%) and the growth of Instagram and Snapchat point to opportunities in social commerce tooling: catalog sync, order capture from DMs and comments, COD order confirmation, and creator/affiliate payouts.

### Gaps
- No reliable data on WhatsApp Business account adoption among Moroccan SMEs.
- No Moroccan data on Instagram or TikTok live selling, or on TikTok Shop availability in Morocco.
- LinkedIn, X and Messenger audience figures were not retrieved.

## 3. Payments: cash dominance, rails, and how a Moroccan business pays for SaaS

### Takeaway
Cash still dominates, though digital rails are growing. Account ownership is 44% of adults (Findex 2024). Mobile payments are 0.1% of transactions (2024). E-commerce is mostly cash-on-delivery (vendor estimates 55–80%). Infrastructure is improving: interoperable instant transfers since June 2023, 16.2M payment accounts in 2025, M-wallet value nearly doubling in 2025, and CMI handling 44M e-commerce card transactions in 2024. Paying foreign SaaS is constrained: individuals' online foreign-currency allowance is MAD 20,000/year under the 2026 foreign-exchange instruction.

### Cited Findings
**Inclusion and accounts**
- Global Findex 2025 (survey year 2024): 44% of adults had an account (up from 29% in 2017), 32% made or received a digital payment, and only 6% saved money — [Hespress EN](https://en.hespress.com/116233-116233.html); dataset reference: [World Bank Microdata](https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/7949/variable/F1/V184?name=fin46)
- The Bank Al-Maghrib 2025 banking supervision report counts 39.8M bank accounts (+1.6M on the year). Branches fell by 151 to 5,541 at end 2025, which the report attributes to digitalisation (article dated July 2026) — [SNRTnews](https://snrtnews.com/fr/article/secteur-bancaire-la-revolution-numerique-saccelere-sous-loeil-vigilant-de-bank-al-maghrib)
- Morocco had 22.6M bank cards in 2024 — [Hespress EN](https://en.hespress.com/116580-morocco-reaches-22-6-million-bank-cards-in-2024.html)

**Mobile and wallet payments**
- Bank Al-Maghrib 2025 figures: M-wallet transactions rose from 19.7M to 28M and their value from MAD 3.9bn to 7.3bn, mostly because social aid is now paid directly into payment accounts. About 93% of M-wallet transactions go through wallets issued by payment institutions. Payment accounts reached 16.221M in 2025, against 13.822M in 2024 — [Le Brief](https://www.lebrief.ma/le-bilan-de-bank-al-maghrib-pour-2025-100170705/)
- Bank Al-Maghrib 2024 payment systems report: mobile payments were only 0.1% of total transactions. There were 21 mobile wallet offerings, and wallet accounts rose to 13.8M from 10.3M — [North Africa Post](https://northafricapost.com/93475-moroccos-central-bank-pushes-mobile-payments-to-reduce-cash-prevalence.html)
- BAM's managing director (2024): only about 10% of users activate mobile payment, and card use for digital transactions does not exceed 30% — [FNH](https://fnh.ma/articles/paiement-mobile-bam-confirme-des-avancees); [Boursenews](https://boursenews.ma/article/marches/comment-bam-compte-accelerer-le-developpement-des-paiement-numeriques-au-maroc)
- In June 2025 Governor Jouahri said BAM had finalized a strategic framework to reduce reliance on cash and push mobile banking — [Hespress EN](https://en.hespress.com/114084-moroccos-central-bank-finalizes-cash-strategy-pushes-for-mobile-banking-shift.html)
- A guide citing BAM says more than 8M M-wallets were opened by end 2025 (+42% on 2024). This metric differs from BAM's 16.2M payment accounts and the two should not be compared — [Wafir](https://wafir.ma/fr/guide/m-wallet-maroc-comparatif-wafa-pay-bp-pay-cih-mobile-inwi-orange-money)

**Instant transfers and interoperability**
- Interbank instant transfers went live in June 2023 — [FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/virement-instantane-maroc)
- 2024: 16.3M instant transfers (22.5M cumulative since launch). The daily average rose from about 24,600 in 2023 to 64,600 in December 2024, with a one-day record of 231,000 — [Hespress FR, BAM report](https://fr.hespress.com/451874-virement-instantane-lexplosion-des-transactions-en-2024.html)
- BAM applies "same activity, same risk, same rule" regulation. It is studying an interconnection with the EU's TIPS instant payment system and running a CBDC proof of concept with World Bank and IMF assistance. BAM cites QR codes, NFC, tokenization and biometrics as enablers — [Boursenews](https://boursenews.ma/article/marches/comment-bam-compte-accelerer-le-developpement-des-paiement-numeriques-au-maroc)

**Cards and e-commerce (CMI)**
- CMI processed 215M card transactions worth MAD 90bn in 2024, including 44M e-commerce transactions. It expects about MAD 110bn in 2025 and targets MAD 500–700bn of electronic payments by 2030 — [Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/economie/le-cmi-vise-700-mmdh-de-paiements-electroniques-a-lhorizon-2030/302580)
- Online card payments were 28.1M operations worth MAD 9.6bn in 2022, and 25.2M operations (+23.4%) worth MAD 8.7bn in January–September 2023 — [FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/e-paiement-maroc); [Infomediaire](https://www.infomediaire.net/?p=132851)

**Cash-on-delivery (vendor estimates only, wide range)**
- COD share estimates: 55–60% — [AivenSoft](https://aivensoft.com/en/blog/morocco-ecommerce-guide); about 60% in 2025 — [Real-Dreamhouse](https://real-dreamhouse.com/en/actualites/e-commerce-au-maroc-etat-des-lieux-et-perspectives-2025/); 65–75% — [CodRocket](https://codrocket.com/blog/morocco-ecommerce-cod-guide-2026); 70–80% — [eGrow](https://www.egrow.com/en/blog/morocco-ecommerce-statistics-trends-2026); 75–82%, projected to fall to 55–65% by 2030 — [Cashod](https://cashod.ma/blog/statistiques-ecommerce-maroc-2026); more than 80% of transactions — [4Tech](https://4tech.ma/en/morocco-the-dazzling-e-commerce-revolution/)
- One guide gives 2025 e-commerce revenue of MAD 21.3bn (+32% on 2023) and COD return-to-origin rates of 15–25% — [AivenSoft](https://aivensoft.com/en/blog/morocco-ecommerce-guide)
- YouCan Ship offers intercity delivery for MAD 30 to 700+ destinations through 12+ partner carriers. YouCan Fulfill bundles warehousing, picking, packing and COD for MAD 50 — [YouCan](https://youcan.shop/en/blog/posts/ship-offer); [YouCan Fulfill](https://youcan.shop/en/blog/posts/fulfill)

**Payment institutions and cash agents**
- Cash Plus at end 2025: more than 5,200 agencies (23% rural), 1.8M+ users of the Cash Plus Mobile app, and consolidated net banking income of MAD 863M (+14%) — [La Vie Éco](https://www.lavieeco.com/comese/cash-plus-accelere-son-developpement-au-service-de-linclusion-financiere-au-maroc/)
- Over 1.5M families receive social aid on Cash Plus accounts. Cash Plus IPO coverage, November 2025 — [Médias24](https://medias24.com/2025/11/01/cash-plus-tout-ce-quil-faut-savoir-sur-lipo-chiffres-objectifs-et-strategie/)
- Chari received a payment-institution licence from Bank Al-Maghrib alongside its October 2025 Series A — [Wamda Morocco page](https://www.wamda.com/index.php/country/Morocco)

**Foreign-exchange rules affecting payment for foreign SaaS (Office des Changes, IGOC 2026)**
- The new general foreign-exchange instruction (IGOC 2026) took effect on 1 January 2026 — [Office des Changes, IGOC 2026 PDF](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/reglementation/pdf/2026-01/IGOC%202026.pdf); [Médias24](https://medias24.com/2026/01/05/operations-de-change-ce-qui-change-dans-la-nouvelle-instruction-1606301/); [Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/reglementation-des-changes-dotation-pour-voyages-e-commerce-export-import-ce-qui-change-des-janvier_BPDQLTUSQFE5PNTNKVZO3UMNA4/)
- Individuals' annual allowance for online purchases on foreign sites rose from MAD 15,000 to MAD 20,000. It explicitly covers software subscriptions, marketplaces, streaming and equipment — [LesMRE](https://www.lesmre.com/fr/actualites/igoc-2026-office-changes-plafonds-voyage-ecommerce-scolarite-mre); [Medi1News](https://www.medi1news.com/fr/article/378630)
- Banks may issue international payment cards to holders of foreign-currency accounts or Office des Changes allowances, and one card can carry all allowances if it is in the holder's name — [IGOC 2026 PDF](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/reglementation/pdf/2026-01/IGOC%202026.pdf)
- The travel allowance is capped at MAD 500,000 per year: a MAD 100,000 base plus up to MAD 400,000 tied to income tax paid — [SNRTnews](https://snrtnews.com/fr/article/instruction-de-change-2026-nouveaux-plafonds-des-dotations-en-devises-pour-les-voyages-a)
- "Categorized operators" receive more flexible foreign-exchange treatment — [Upsilon Consulting](https://upsilon-consulting.com/operateur-categorise-office-changes-maroc/)

### Inferences
- A locally billed SaaS (MAD pricing through CMI cards, bank transfer or instant transfer, with cheque or cash-agent fallback) has a structural advantage over foreign SaaS. Foreign subscriptions draw on a capped allowance (MAD 20,000/yr for individuals) and on international-card activation, which is friction for micro-firms and sole traders.
- With 44% account ownership, about 10% mobile-payment activation and majority-COD e-commerce, SaaS that helps merchants manage cash (COD reconciliation, delivery tracking, cash collection, agent networks) addresses a pain point that persists. ORA's cash-collection line and YouCan's COD logistics are evidence of this (see section 5).
- Instant transfers and growing payment accounts make "pay by instant transfer or wallet" a realistic B2B billing option. Annual or prepaid plans paid by bank transfer will likely suit SMEs better than monthly card auto-debit.

### Gaps
- No official 2025 BAM "taux de bancarisation" or financial-inclusion rate was retrieved. BAM's own measure differs from Findex.
- No 2025 number of cards or 2025 CMI e-commerce totals were found. No authoritative (non-vendor) COD share was found.
- No data was found for Payzone, Wafacash, Barid Cash or national QR-code payment volumes.
- The corporate (legal-entity) foreign-exchange allowance for software or service imports could not be verified. One consultant source suggested MAD 200,000 under standard rules, but this is unconfirmed. Check the [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/reglementation/pdf/2026-01/IGOC%202026.pdf) directly.
- No data on SaaS-specific payment habits, such as the share of B2B software paid by card vs transfer vs cheque/LCN, or payment-delay statistics.

## 4. Government strategy, startup support programs and catalysts

### Takeaway
"Digital Morocco 2030" (launched September 2024) targets 240,000 digital jobs, about a USD 10bn digital contribution to GDP, MAD 40bn in digital exports, and 3,000 startups by 2030 (1,000 by 2026). Startup financing has moved from grants to a fund-of-funds model: a MAD 1.3bn package in December 2025, a fund-of-funds convention in November 2025, and the Fonds Catalytique Startups (MAD 347M) by decree in August 2026. The Innov Invest Fund received the World Bank's top rating in September 2026. Startup status is still not set in law. GITEX Africa (55,000+ attendees in 2026), AFCON 2025 (USD 1.8bn impact claimed) and the 2030 World Cup act as visibility and spending catalysts.

### Cited Findings
**Digital Morocco 2030 targets**
- More than 240,000 digital jobs by 2030 — [Hespress EN](https://en.hespress.com/91873-91873.html); [Agenzia Nova](https://www.agenzianova.com/en/news/Morocco-presents-its-digital-strategy-2030/)
- GDP target, stated in different ways: digital sector contribution of about USD 10bn — [Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-digital/1301-51876-morocco-targets-ai-led-competitiveness-with-2030-strategy); about MAD 100bn — [MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/?p=5933); digital economy at 6% of GDP — [North Africa Post](https://northafricapost.com/?p=91670)

## 5. Startup ecosystem: funding by year, notable startups, local SaaS, and failures

### Takeaway
Moroccan venture funding is small but rising: about USD 26–33M in 2021–2022, roughly USD 70–95M in 2024, and USD 108–128M in 2025 (a record, 6th in Africa). It is mostly local (about 70% from local VCs in 2024), with no Series B in 2025. H1 2026 figures conflict (USD 28M vs 102M). Leading players are B2B commerce/fintech (Chari), super-app plus COD logistics (ORA), e-commerce SaaS (YouCan), proptech (Yakeey) and logistics SaaS (Freterium). The clearest documented failure is YoLa Fresh (agri-B2B), which shut down in October 2026 after raising USD 9M and failing to raise again. Ride-hailing operates in a regulatory grey zone.

## 6. Structural challenges: talent, brain drain, trust, informality and bureaucracy

### Takeaway
Developers are cheap by EU standards: juniors earn about MAD 7,000–12,000 a month and seniors MAD 20,000–35,000+. But emigration is significant: officials estimated 2,000–3,000 engineers a year in 2022, and 10–20% of about 8,000 IT graduates a year leave. Informality is the norm, with about 2.03M informal production units, 85.5% of them one-person, and informal work making up 33.1% of non-agricultural jobs. Trust in online payment is low, which drives COD. A mandatory DGI e-invoicing clearance regime is being phased in from 2026, a forcing function for SME software adoption, though decree timing is uncertain.

**Talent and brain drain**
- In December 2022 Higher Education Minister Miraoui said about 700 doctors and 2,000–3,000 engineers emigrate each year — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2022/12/37907/brain-drain-3-700-doctors-engineers-leave-morocco-every-year/). - 40% of phone owners use SIMs not registered in their name. This identity gap complicates KYC and digital onboarding — [Hespress EN on Findex](https://en.hespress.com/116233-116233.html)

**Compliance as a driver: mandatory e-invoicing**
- The DGI is implementing mandatory e-invoicing based on Article 145-IX of the tax code (Finance Law 2018), using a clearance model with prior validation by the DGI platform and the UBL format — [Edicom](https://edicomgroup.com/fr/blog/maroc-obligation-facturation-electronique); [Upsilon Consulting](https://upsilon-consulting.com/facturation-electronique-maroc-2026/); [Challenge.ma](https://www.challenge.ma/fiscalite-2026-la-facture-electronique-obligatoire-sur-les-rails-316071/)
- Indicative phasing according to vendor Inyad: large corporate-tax payers from 1 January 2026, mid-size firms from 1 July 2026, and SMEs/VSEs under MAD 10M turnover plus auto-entrepreneurs above MAD 500,000 from 1 January 2027. Another version uses a MAD 50M threshold for large firms and a micro-enterprise wave in 2027–2028. Exact dates and thresholds depend on an implementing decree that was still pending, according to several sources — [Inyad](https://inyad.com/blog/calendrier-obligation-facturation-electronique-maroc); [Inyad](https://inyad.com/blog/calendrier-facture-electronique-maroc-2026-2028); [Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/facturation-electronique-le-detail-du-calendrier-de-mise-en-place_2EQSNG2DTVB6PGBBHQ6OCFT6CY/); [Banqup](https://www.banqup.com/fr-fr/resources/compliance-pulse/morocco)

### Inferences
- Engineering cost is a real advantage, since a senior developer costs about one-third to one-quarter of an EU equivalent. Retention against French IT-services firms and emigration is the binding constraint, not the hiring cost.
- Informality (2M+ micro units, mostly one person) limits the paying SME base. Products for the long tail must be very cheap, mobile-only, Darija-friendly and probably freemium or transaction-funded. Formal SMEs and mid-caps facing e-invoicing compliance are the more reliable payers for subscriptions.
- E-invoicing is a time-boxed wedge for invoicing, accounting, POS and ERP SaaS (Inyad, Facturis and others are already positioning). Whoever owns the invoice could also extend into payments and working-capital lending (Hsabati and Chari show this pattern).

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 official brain-drain figure for tech workers. The data is from 2018–2022.
- No survey data on SME trust in or adoption of cloud and SaaS, or on data-residency requirements (CNDP / Law 09-08) for hosting abroad. These searches were not completed.
- No confirmed published e-invoicing decree date. Verify with the DGI.
- No data on business-creation bureaucracy (time and cost to register, auto-entrepreneur counts) or B2B payment-delay statistics.
