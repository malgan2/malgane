## 1. Market size: transaction volume, growth, shoppers, merchants, cross-border

### Takeaway
Card-paid e-commerce in Morocco reached 44.8M transactions and MAD 12.1bn in 2025 (Bank Al-Maghrib), growing 16% in count and 10% in value. This is only the visible card-paid slice: a large majority of online orders are still paid cash on delivery (COD) and never appear in CMI/BAM statistics. About a quarter of Moroccans bought online in 2024 (ANRT). Since July 2022, cross-border purchases through foreign platforms have paid customs duty regardless of value. Temu reportedly entered Morocco through Barid Al-Maghrib's Chrono Diali, but no source quantifies the cross-border share.

### Cited Findings
**Card-paid e-commerce (official / quasi-official)**
- 2025 (Bank Al-Maghrib annual report on payment systems, published Oct 2026): e-commerce card payments reached **44.8M transactions (+16%)** worth **MAD 12.1bn (+10%)** — [Le Reporter](https://www.lereporter.ma/bank-al-maghrib-hausse-de-20-du-nombre-des-paiements-par-cartes-bancaires-en-2025/); [LeBrief](https://www.lebrief.ma/le-bilan-de-bank-al-maghrib-pour-2025-100170705/); [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/10/343358/morocco-sees-20-rise-in-bank-card-payments-in-2025/)
- 2025, all card payments: 230.1M transactions worth MAD 70.8bn (about +20% in count, +12% in value vs 2024). POS (TPE) payments rose from 153.3M to 184.5M — [Le Reporter](https://www.lereporter.ma/bank-al-maghrib-hausse-de-20-du-nombre-des-paiements-par-cartes-bancaires-en-2025/); [LeBrief](https://www.lebrief.ma/bam-les-paiements-par-carte-bancaire-en-hausse-100170685/)
- 2024 baseline (BAM): about **38.5M e-commerce transactions, about MAD 11bn** — [Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/paiements-par-carte-1925-millions-doperations-17-en-un-an_XLZA5ZKHLVCMZOIZYRMYPIYAFE/); [Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/societe/e-commerce-le-maroc-peut-il-creer-son-propre-paypal/346218)
- CMI 2024: 215M card transactions processed for MAD 90bn, **including 44M e-commerce transactions** — [Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/economie/le-cmi-vise-700-mmdh-de-paiements-electroniques-a-lhorizon-2030/302580). This conflicts with BAM's 38.5M for 2024. The likely cause is scope: CMI probably counts foreign cards and bill-payment platforms, while BAM likely counts Moroccan cards only. I could not confirm this because the article could not be opened.
- CMI 2025 forecast (made by the CMI CEO at end-2025): about 240M card transactions (+15%), about 85% on Moroccan cards, merchant takings near MAD 100bn (+13% vs 2024), and foreign-card payments about MAD 32bn — [FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/paiements-electroniques-cmi-transformation); [CMI](https://www.cmi.co.ma/fr/nos-actualites/le-cmi-garantit-la-continuite-la-securite-et-la-fluidite-des-paiements-dg)
- CMI targets MAD 500–700bn of electronic payments by 2030, against about MAD 110bn expected in 2025 — [Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/economie/le-cmi-vise-700-mmdh-de-paiements-electroniques-a-lhorizon-2030/302580)
- Historical (older data): in 2022, CMI-affiliated merchant and bill-payment sites processed 28.1M online payment operations (+35.6%), with Moroccan cards at 95.4% of transactions — [TelQuel, 2023](https://telquel.ma/instant-t/2023/03/22/e-commerce-une-hausse-de-plus-de-35-du-nombre-doperations-de-paiement-en-ligne-en-2022_1805774/). H1 2022: 12.8M operations, MAD 4.6bn — [FNH, 2022](https://www.fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/e-commerce-12-8-millions-d-operations-pour-4-6-milliards-de-dh-au-1er-semestre-2022)
- Instant transfers grew 78% in 2025, and BAM is preparing to extend instant transfers to merchant payments (a potential COD alternative) — [Médias24, 22 Oct 2026](https://medias24.com/2026/10/22/virements-instantanes-78-en-2025-bam-prepare-les-paiements-marchands-1782783)

**Online shoppers (ANRT)**

The source says this equals about 3.7M new online buyers — [LeBrief](https://www.lebrief.ma/e-commerce-un-quart-des-marocains-achetent-en-ligne-100129064/); [H24info](https://h24info.ma/economie/e-commerce-maroc-acheteurs/); primary PDF: [ANRT Enquête TIC 2024-2025](https://www.anrt.ma/sites/default/files/2025-10/Enqu%C3%AAte%20TIC%202024-2025.pdf)

**Cross-border (Temu / Shein / AliExpress) and customs**
- Decree n° 2-22-438 (16 June 2022, BO n° 7101 of 20 June 2022) took purchases made via international e-commerce platforms out of the MAD 1,250 duty exemption, **regardless of value, from 1 July 2022**. The government's reason was fraudulent splitting of orders into small parcels. Non-commercial shipments between individuals up to MAD 1,250 remain exempt — [Médias24, 2022](https://medias24.com/2022/06/17/les-explications-et-precisions-de-la-douane-sur-limposition-des-achats-electroniques-depuis-letranger/); [Douane circular 6346/210](https://www.douane.gov.ma/dms/loadDocument?documentId=89835&application=circulaire); [maroc.ma](https://www.maroc.ma/fr/actualites/les-transactions-electroniques-ne-seront-plus-exonerees-des-droits-de-douanes-limport)
- About 90% of items imported via Shein had benefited from the sub-MAD 1,250 regime before the change, and merchants split orders to stay under the threshold — [Marocv.com](https://marocv.com/taxe-sur-les-petits-colis-shein-temu-et-aliexpress-ont-deja-trouve-la-parade-150848.html)
- Temu has reportedly started operating in Morocco, with Chrono Diali (a Barid Al-Maghrib subsidiary) handling customs clearance. This comes from a single trade outlet, with no date or official confirmation — [Trendtype](https://trendtype.com/news/chinese-online-discount-consumer-goods-marketplace-temu-has-started-operations-in-morocco/)
- Import duty rates range from about 2.5% to 40% by product category, plus 20% VAT (consumer guide, secondary) — [Temu-maroc.com guide](https://temu-maroc.com/douane-taxes-importation-vrai-cout-colis-etranger-maroc/); [lesmre.com](https://www.lesmre.com/fr/guides/envoyer-colis-maroc-depuis-etranger-douane-guide)
- PLF 2026 tariff changes were reportedly formalized by ADII circular n° 6702/210 of 26 Dec 2025 (secondary, unverified) — [macalculatriceenligne.com](https://macalculatriceenligne.com/maroc/calcul-tarif-douane-maroc/)
- Moroccan fashion brands report pressure from Shein and Temu in digital marketing (headline only) — [FashionNetwork Maroc](https://ma.fashionnetwork.com/news/Marketing-digital-les-marques-sous-la-pression-de-shein-et-temu,1737523.html)

**Merchants / stores (third-party trackers — treat as rough)**
- ShopRank (June 2026): WooCommerce 51.4% of Moroccan e-commerce stores, Shopify 23.6%, YouCan 14.6%. Among stores selling physical goods: WooCommerce 36.6%, Shopify 31.4% — [ShopRank Morocco](https://shoprank.com/countries/morocco)
- AfterShip: YouCan has about 4.85K stores (19.13% of Moroccan stores), WooCommerce 38.96% and Shopify 29.77%. The page's data appears dated — [AfterShip](https://www.aftership.com/ecommerce/statistics/regions/ma)
- Shopify stores in Morocco: 3,936 (Storecensus) vs 5,614 active (Bootleads, 2026) — [Storecensus](https://www.storecensus.com/stores/country/morocco); [Bootleads](https://bootleads.com/stores/shopify/countries/ma/)
- Jumia Morocco: "more than 5,000 Moroccan sellers" cited around Jumia Festival 2026 (31 Aug–20 Sep) — [Perspectives Med](https://perspectivesmed.com/jumia-festival-2026-quand-le-capitalisme-numerique-se-met-a-lheure-de-la-rentree-marocaine/)

### Inferences
- The card-paid slice (MAD 12.1bn in 2025) understates total e-commerce by a large factor. If roughly two-thirds or more of orders are COD (see section 2), total online retail GMV is plausibly several times the card figure. YouCan alone reported USD 753M GMV in 2023 (section 4), though that figure includes non-Moroccan MENA merchants. No source gives a defensible total GMV.
- Card e-commerce growth is slowing in value (+10% in 2025) while transaction count grows faster (+16%), which suggests falling average ticket and more small purchases.
- The 2022 customs change and Temu's postal-channel entry put downward price pressure on local dropshippers selling Chinese-sourced generic goods. Local sellers' remaining advantages are speed, COD and try-before-pay.
- Third-party trackers detect only stores on identifiable platforms with their own domains. They undercount social-only sellers (Instagram, WhatsApp, TikTok), who likely form the largest group of "merchants" by count.

### Gaps
- No full-year 2025 CMI e-commerce breakdown (number of affiliated e-merchants, domestic vs foreign cards) could be retrieved. BAM vs CMI scope differences remain unresolved.
- No official count of online merchants (CMI-affiliated e-merchant count not found).
- No figure for the share of cross-border vs domestic e-commerce, and no Moroccan data on Temu or Shein order volumes.
- DataReportal 2026 social media and internet figures for Morocco were not retrieved (search budget exhausted).
- The "Wafir" 18bn MAD / 6.8M buyers figure is unverified. The ANRT survey's population base for the 24.9% figure is not confirmed.

## 2. Cash on delivery: share, confirmation, delivery success, returns, costs, fraud

### Takeaway
COD dominates. ANRT-based figures put it at about 66% of orders, or 83.8% of buyers who use it, and industry estimates run to 80–90%. In practitioner rules of thumb, only about 35–50 of every 100 leads become paid deliveries: 50–70% confirm, and 60–80% of confirmed orders are delivered. Refusal and return rates commonly sit at 15–40%. Cash comes back from couriers anywhere from 24 hours to two weeks later. Most return-rate and confirmation numbers come from vendors or consultants, not independent studies.

### Cited Findings
**COD share**
- "More than 66% of orders" are still paid cash on delivery, per ANRT, as reported alongside BAM's MAD 11bn 2024 card figure — [Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/societe/e-commerce-le-maroc-peut-il-creer-son-propre-paypal/346218)
- 83.8% of online buyers say they use cash payment on delivery (ANRT 2024-2025 via a secondary blog; this is declared usage, not share of value; not verified against the PDF) — [Energiedin](https://www.energiedin.com/blog/e-commerce-maroc/)
- "80 to 90% of e-commerce payments are made on delivery", none of it counted by CMI (agency publication) — [Egio Digital](https://www.egiodigital.com/publications/tag/data/)
- COD is often said to exceed 80% of online transactions (COD-software vendor) — [eGrow](https://www.egrow.com/en/blog/cod-ecommerce-morocco-complete-guide-2026). A logistics blog gives 60–70% of orders — [Transmartic](https://www.transmartic.com/blog/livraison-ecommerce-maroc-2026-last-mile-24h)

**Funnel benchmarks (practitioner)**
- 100 leads typically give 50–70 confirmed orders, and 60–80% of those are delivered, so **35–50 paid orders per 100 leads**. Costs must be allocated per *delivered* order, not per confirmed order — [Tifawt COD guide 2026](https://tifawt.ma/guide-cod-maroc/)
- COD stays profitable while the refusal rate is under about 20–25%. Above that, round-trip shipping costs absorb the margin — [Kslab](https://kslab.ma/fr/e-commerce-maroc-2026/)
- Average refusal rate estimated at 15–25% — [Swiftlog](https://www.swiftlog.ma/blog/cod-maroc-reduire-taux-refus-livraison-2026)
- Return rates are cited as 25–40%, 20–40% and 15–30% in different articles from the same vendor. Non-delivery and returns "often account for 20–40% of dispatched orders" — [eGrow return-rate article](https://www.egrow.com/en/blog/reduce-cod-return-rate-below-15-morocco-2026); [eGrow logistics article](https://www.egrow.com/en/blog/ecommerce-logistics-morocco-optimize-cod-2026)
- A refusal rate of about 15–20% (unsourced blog) — [Berrynoon](https://berrynoon.ma/blog/nos-clients-ne-paient-jamais-temps-pourquoi-lencaissement-bancaire-marocain-tue-votre-trsorerie)

**Causes of failed deliveries**
- Unconfirmed orders, wrong contact details, customer unavailability, refusal at the door, and "multi-seller ordering" (the customer orders the same item from several sellers and keeps one) — [eGrow](https://www.egrow.com/en/blog/morocco-ecommerce-cod-operator-guide-2026)
- Buyer distrust: non-conforming products, delays, unclear refunds and no quick way to contest transactions have built mistrust between buyers and sellers — [Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/societe/e-commerce-le-maroc-peut-il-creer-son-propre-paypal/346218)
- Customers can traditionally open the parcel before paying (old marketplace practice, date unclear) — [JDN](https://www.journaldunet.com/retail/1535181-maroc-l-essor-du-e-commerce-et-le-defi-de-la-logistique/)

**Cost of failure / cash-collection delays**
- Many Moroccan couriers do not charge for returns, and COD remittance is "often 7–15 days" — [Tifawt COD guide](https://tifawt.ma/guide-cod-maroc/) (as summarized by search; not opened)
- Merchants wait for the courier to remit cash collected, "sometimes 2 weeks" — [Berrynoon](https://berrynoon.ma/blog/nos-clients-ne-paient-jamais-temps-pourquoi-lencaissement-bancaire-marocain-tue-votre-trsorerie). Remittance delays range from "several days or even weeks" — [eGrow](https://www.egrow.com/en/blog/cod-ecommerce-morocco-complete-guide-2026)
- Courier terms vary widely:
  - **Cathedis** Silver pack: MAD 35 for major cities, cash returned once a week, parcel returns free up to 10%. Gold pack: MAD 40, cash twice a week, free returns up to 20% — [Cathedis tariffs](https://cathedis.ma/nos-tarifs/)
  - **Sendit**: MAD 35 for 20 major cities, COD remittance within 24 hours 7 days a week, free returns with no percentage cap (third-party comparison, April 2026 scrape) — [Swiftlog comparison](https://www.swiftlog.ma/blog/sendit-ma-vs-cathedis-comparatif-livraison-maroc-2026)
  - **Ozon Express**: MAD 35 delivery (e.g., Agadir, Fès), return fee MAD 0 on its city table — [Ozon Groupe](https://www.ozongroupe.com/); [Scribd copy of Ozon site](https://www.scribd.com/document/895187702/Accueil-Ozon-Express)
  - **YouCan Ship**: limited-time intercity offer at MAD 30 incl. tax, 700+ destinations — [YouCan blog](https://youcan.shop/en/blog/posts/ship-offer)

**Confirmation costs**
- Automated voice confirmation (Confirmation-Maroc): MAD 5 per confirmed call, no subscription (struck-through "standard" price MAD 12) — [Confirmation-Maroc](https://confirmation-maroc.com/)
- MyLeadDone: phone confirmation in Darija, Arabic or French, billed MAD 10 only when an order is *delivered* — [MyLeadDone](https://www.myleaddone.com/blog/how-to-confirm-cod-orders-morocco/)
- In-house confirmation agent salary: MAD 4,500–8,500/month plus bonuses — [Jobsquare](https://www.jobsquare.ma/blog/211/teleoperateur-en-centre-d-appels-au-maroc-guide-complet-du-metier/)

**Fraud, fake orders and blacklists**
- I found no national, shared buyer blacklist. One guide recommends an internal blacklist by phone number shared with the confirmation team, and says *some delivery agencies offer blacklists shared between merchants* — [Swiftlog](https://www.swiftlog.ma/blog/cod-maroc-reduire-taux-refus-livraison-2026)
- Suspicious-order signals: invalid number, vague address, repeated orders from one number in a short time, customers who confirm then systematically refuse — [Storeino](https://www.storeino.com/fr/articles/paiement-a-la-livraison-maroc)
- Generic Shopify anti-fraud apps (e.g., FraudGuard: email blacklist, domain filter, country blocking; full version USD 20/month) do not target phone-based COD fraud — [Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/fraudguard?locale=fr)
- Data-protection context: a hacker claimed a leak of more than 26,000 Decathlon Maroc customer order records (10 Oct 2026, unconfirmed). Any blacklist must respect CNDP rules — [FrenchBreaches](https://frenchbreaches.com/blog/decathlon-maroc-plus-de-26-000-donnees-de-commandes-clients-revendiquees-par-un-pirate)

### Inferences
- **Funnel math:** at 35–50 paid orders per 100 leads, a seller pays for 2–3 leads (ad cost plus confirmation) per delivered order. Every failed shipment also burns about MAD 35 outbound plus any return fee, and locks up stock for days.
- Shipping cost is roughly commoditized at MAD 30–40 per parcel. Couriers now compete on remittance speed (24 hours to weekly) and return-fee policy, so cash-flow terms are a key buying criterion for sellers.
- The absence of a neutral cross-merchant reputation network, plus courier-level blacklists that exist only inside each courier, means a serial refuser blocked by one seller can keep ordering from others.

### Gaps
- No independent, non-vendor study of COD return or refusal rates by category or city. The figures are vendor and consultant ranges, 15–40%.
- No courier-published delivery-success statistics (Amana, Ozon, Cathedis, Sendit).
- No quantified estimate of total national losses from failed deliveries or fake orders.
- Remittance terms not found for Amana/Barid, Speedaf, Forcelog, Onessta, Tawssil or CTM Messagerie.

## 3. Seller types

### Takeaway
The ecosystem has five layers: (1) local DTC brands, mostly on WooCommerce or Shopify; (2) COD dropshippers and "affiliates" who sell other people's stock for a commission paid on delivered orders; (3) social-only sellers on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and WhatsApp; (4) marketplace sellers, with Jumia at 5,000+ Moroccan sellers; and (5) influencers, who are increasingly targeted by tax authorities. The affiliate-COD model is well developed and supported by several dedicated platforms.

### Cited Findings
- **Affiliate COD platforms** (all vendor self-descriptions):
  - **Marocaffiliate**: product catalog, managed stock, delivery and WhatsApp tracking. The affiliate sets the sale price, and the margin is credited on delivery. Delivery runs through Ozone, G-LOG or an internal fleet — [Marocaffiliate](https://marocaffiliate.com/fr)
  - **Cod Market**: affiliation plus operations, with commissions calculated on delivered orders — [Cod Market](https://cod-market.com/)
  - **Affipartner**: claims 65 affiliate programs launched. A program can confirm with its own staff and ship with Affipartner Delivery, or the reverse, order by order — [Affipartner](https://affipartner.com/)
  - **CodPartner** (dropshipping and affiliate program) — [CodPartner](https://codpartner.com/affiliate); **COD Outfit** (affiliate COD clothing, "no stock") — [COD Outfit](https://codoutfit.ma/)
- The dominant affiliate compensation model pays commissions only on *delivered* orders — [search synthesis of Cod Market / Marocaffiliate pages](https://cod-market.com/)
- **Marketplaces:** Jumia remains a major marketplace. The group refocused on 8 markets including Morocco after exiting Tunisia and South Africa (end 2024) and Algeria (early 2026). Group 2025 revenue was USD 188.9M (+13%), Q2 2026 GMV grew 20%, and headcount fell from 4,318 (end 2022) to under 2,000 (early 2026) — [Forbes Afrique](https://forbesafrique.com/jumia-cinq-cles-pour-comprendre-le-tournant-du-pionnier-africain-du-e-commerce/). Jumia Morocco has 5,000+ sellers — [Perspectives Med](https://perspectivesmed.com/jumia-festival-2026-quand-le-capitalisme-numerique-se-met-a-lheure-de-la-rentree-marocaine/). Third-party guides note seller commissions and a focus on organized merchants — [MarocHub](https://marochub.app/blog/meilleurs-sites-ecommerce-maroc-2026)
- **Influencers and social sellers:** the Finance Minister said influencers, resident or not, owe income tax, must register with the DGI and must file annually — [H24info](https://h24info.ma/economie/le-fisc-marocain-siffle-la-fin-de-la-recre-pour-les-influenceurs/). DGI units monitor social networks and compare visible lifestyle with declarations, and a specialized cell has reportedly existed since late 2024. About 120 creators were audited in 2023 — [FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/influenceurs-maroc-fin-far-west-numerique). The DGI is accelerating control of digital and e-commerce revenues — [Consonews](https://consonews.ma/65968.html); [Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/le-fisc-resserre-letau-sur-les-influenceurs-et-le-commerce-en-ligne_FPNXVQV3UZGTZCGZDLPMUN54ZE/)
- E-commerce is reportedly subject to VAT above MAD 2M annual turnover, and digital advertising services above MAD 500K/year (single source, unverified) — [Consonews](https://consonews.ma/65968.html). No specific social-media tax will be created — [H24info](https://h24info.ma/economie/le-fisc-marocain-siffle-la-fin-de-la-recre-pour-les-influenceurs/)

### Inferences
- The affiliate/COD-dropship layer creates a multi-party settlement problem. Product owner, affiliate, confirmation center and courier must all be reconciled per delivered order, which is a natural SaaS wedge (commission ledgers, payouts, disputes).
- Rising DGI scrutiny of social sellers and influencers should increase demand for simple invoicing, bookkeeping and revenue-declaration tools for micro-sellers and auto-entrepreneurs.

### Gaps
- No data found on the number or share of Instagram-, TikTok- or WhatsApp-only sellers, or on social-commerce GMV.
- No data retrieved on Avito, Marjane Mall or Electroplanet marketplaces (seller counts, commissions).
- No official figures for Jumia Morocco alone (GMV, orders, active sellers).
- Unverified: whether e-commerce turnover counts toward auto-entrepreneur caps, and exact VAT thresholds for online sellers.

## 4. Tools in use and competitors

### Takeaway
The Moroccan seller toolchain is fragmented. Store builders are led by WooCommerce and Shopify, with homegrown YouCan the dominant COD-native option. Couriers have converged on about MAD 35 per parcel. Payments are constrained: Stripe is unavailable, PayPal is limited, and CMI or local gateways are required. A long tail of small COD SaaS (confirmation, order management, affiliate ERPs) already competes for sellers, many of them launched in 2025–2026.

### Cited Findings
**Store builders**
- **YouCan**: total GMV grew from USD 88M in its first year to **USD 753M in 2023** (stated by its president at a January 2024 event with 4,500+ participants). Products include YouCan Confirm, YouCan Fulfill and YouCan Pay — [YouCan event blog](https://youcan.shop/en/help/blog/youcan-event). The only funding round found is a March 2022 incubator/accelerator deal (Plug and Play Morocco, amount undisclosed) — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/youcan/financials)
- YouCan pricing: USD 29/49/89/187 per month (Solopreneur, Starter, Entrepreneur, Premium). Transaction fees are 0.5% on COD and 1% on online payments for USD 0–5K monthly sales, falling to 0.25% and 0.5% above USD 5K. A free "Default" plan carries higher fees — [YouCan pricing](https://youcan.shop/en/pricing); [YouCan fees help page](https://youcan.shop/en/help/due-amount)
- StoreLeads: 8.7% of YouCan stores are in Morocco, and the store count fell 6.6% quarter-on-quarter in Q2 2026. The 8.7% share looks low for YouCan's home market, which may reflect tracker attribution — [StoreLeads YouCan report](https://storeleads.app/reports/youcan)
- **Storeino** markets itself as "the first COD e-commerce platform" and claims 70,000+ merchants. StoreLeads tracks only about 270 live stores (68.5% in Morocco) and ShopRank 109 — [Storeino](https://www.storeino.com/en); [StoreLeads Storeino](https://storeleads.app/reports/storeino); [ShopRank Storeino](https://shoprank.com/ecommerce-platforms/storeino)
- **Lightfunnels**: 33 live stores in Morocco per StoreLeads (−7% year-on-year, Q1 2026) — [StoreLeads Lightfunnels MA](https://storeleads.app/reports/lightfunnels/MA/top-stores)
- Shopify and WooCommerce lead by store count (see section 1) — [ShopRank Morocco](https://shoprank.com/countries/morocco)

**COD order-management, confirmation and ERP SaaS (vendor claims)**
- **CodSolver**: COD platform for Morocco, Algeria and the GCC, with commissions for confirmation agents, from MAD 49/month — [CodSolver](https://codsolver.com/)

- YouCan is the incumbent COD-native suite (store, confirm, fulfill, pay, ship), so a new SaaS should integrate with YouCan, Shopify and WooCommerce rather than replace the store builder.

## 5. Pain points

### Takeaway
The best-documented pains are: (1) high refusal and return rates, with 15–40% cited, mostly by vendors; (2) slow, uneven COD cash remittance from couriers, from 24 hours to 2 weeks; (3) labor-heavy order confirmation, paid at MAD 5–10 per order or through MAD 4,500–8,500/month agents; (4) fake or duplicate orders with no shared blacklist; (5) buyer distrust; (6) restricted online payment rails; and (7) rising tax scrutiny of social sellers. Ad-cost and sourcing pains could not be quantified.

### Cited Findings
- Returns and refusals at 15–40% across sources; COD unprofitable above about 20–25% refusal — [Kslab](https://kslab.ma/fr/e-commerce-maroc-2026/); [Swiftlog](https://www.swiftlog.ma/blog/cod-maroc-reduire-taux-refus-livraison-2026); [eGrow](https://www.egrow.com/en/blog/reduce-cod-return-rate-below-15-morocco-2026)
- Cash collection delays: remittance in days or weeks, "sometimes 2 weeks", often 7–15 days — [Berrynoon](https://berrynoon.ma/blog/nos-clients-ne-paient-jamais-temps-pourquoi-lencaissement-bancaire-marocain-tue-votre-trsorerie); [Tifawt](https://tifawt.ma/guide-cod-maroc/). One logistics blog recommends daily remittance to the bank to limit risk — [Transmartic](https://www.transmartic.com/blog/livraison-ecommerce-maroc-2026-last-mile-24h)
- Confirmation labor: manually calling every customer is resource-intensive — [eGrow](https://www.egrow.com/en/blog/morocco-ecommerce-cod-operator-guide-2026). Confirmation rate is called the number-one profitability lever — [Tifawt](https://tifawt.ma/guide-cod-maroc/)
- Fake orders and serial refusers: there is no shared national blacklist, and some couriers keep inter-merchant lists — [Swiftlog](https://www.swiftlog.ma/blog/cod-maroc-reduire-taux-refus-livraison-2026); [Storeino](https://www.storeino.com/fr/articles/paiement-a-la-livraison-maroc)
- Buyer distrust: non-conforming products, delays, unclear refunds — [Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/societe/e-commerce-le-maroc-peut-il-creer-son-propre-paypal/346218). Older reports describe Moroccan victims of online scams (2020) — [Bladi](https://www.bladi.net/marocains-victimes-arnaque-internet,82229.html)
- Online-payment constraints: no Stripe, PayPal limitations, YouCan Pay daily cap — [Chari Pay](https://charipay.ma/en/blog/stripe-au-maroc); [YouCan Pay](https://youcanpay.com/pricing?lang=fr)
- Tax compliance pressure on online sellers and influencers — [Consonews](https://consonews.ma/65968.html); [FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/influenceurs-maroc-fin-far-west-numerique)
- Cross-border competition (Shein, Temu) pressuring local brands — [FashionNetwork Maroc](https://ma.fashionnetwork.com/news/Marketing-digital-les-marques-sous-la-pression-de-shein-et-temu,1737523.html)
- Regulatory uncertainty in parcel logistics (Barid monopoly, Chrono Diali referral) — [Médias24](https://medias24.com/2026/03/11/poste-letat-prepare-une-ouverture-partielle-du-monopole-de-barid-al-maghrib-1641489/)

### Inferences
- The pains compound inside unit economics. A seller who does not compute profit per *delivered* order, net of ad spend per lead, confirmation cost, shipping, returns and immobilized stock, can look profitable while losing money, as the Tifawt guide warns. This makes true COD profit analytics a high-value pain.
- Cash-flow pain grows with courier diversity. Sellers using two or three couriers face different payout cadences (24 hours vs weekly vs 7–15 days) and must reconcile remittance statements against orders manually.

### Gaps
- I could not access Facebook groups, YouTube or TikTok seller content, or Reddit r/Morocco threads (no page fetches, search budget exhausted). First-hand seller complaints are therefore under-represented.
- No data on Meta or TikTok ad costs (CPM, CPL) in Morocco.
- No data on product sourcing pains (China imports, customs clearance for B2B sellers, freight forwarders).
- No data on chargebacks for card payments in Morocco.
- No documented case found of a courier going bankrupt or withholding COD funds, although the delays themselves are widely mentioned.

## 6. Toolchain gaps and SaaS opportunities

### Takeaway
Six gaps are visible from the evidence: (a) multi-courier COD remittance reconciliation and cash-flow visibility; (b) true per-delivered-order profit analytics that combine ad spend, confirmation, shipping and returns; (c) a privacy-compliant cross-merchant customer reputation network; (d) Darija voice and WhatsApp confirmation automation, where only early players are visible; (e) affiliate and multi-party commission settlement; and (f) compliance and invoicing for micro-sellers under rising DGI scrutiny. The COD order-management dashboard space itself is crowded. Differentiation should come from data network effects, such as reputation and courier performance benchmarks, or from deep courier integrations.

### Cited Findings
- Existing COD SaaS covers order management, confirmation and affiliate commissions, at low price points (from MAD 49/month), with 200+ stores claimed by one player — [CodSolver](https://codsolver.com/); [CodFlow](https://codflow.ma/)
- Profit calculators exist (Tifawt ERP) but are tied to a single ERP — [Tifawt](https://tifawt.ma/en/)
- Darija-language confirmation exists as a service (MyLeadDone human calls; Confirmation-Maroc automated voice at MAD 5 per confirmation) — [MyLeadDone](https://www.myleaddone.com/blog/how-to-confirm-cod-orders-morocco/); [Confirmation-Maroc](https://confirmation-maroc.com/)
- Shared blacklists exist only inside some delivery agencies, with no neutral network — [Swiftlog](https://www.swiftlog.ma/blog/cod-maroc-reduire-taux-refus-livraison-2026)
- Courier payout cadences are heterogeneous (Sendit 24 hours; Cathedis weekly or twice weekly) — [Swiftlog comparison](https://www.swiftlog.ma/blog/sendit-ma-vs-cathedis-comparatif-livraison-maroc-2026); [Cathedis](https://cathedis.ma/nos-tarifs/)
- Affiliate platforms pay commissions only on delivered orders, which requires per-order settlement across parties — [Cod Market](https://cod-market.com/); [Marocaffiliate](https://marocaffiliate.com/fr)
- BAM plans to extend instant transfers to merchant payments, a possible future rail for "pay on delivery by transfer or QR" instead of cash — [Médias24](https://medias24.com/2026/10/22/virements-instantanes-78-en-2025-bam-prepare-les-paiements-marchands-1782783)

### Inferences
Ranked by evidence strength and differentiation:
1. **Multi-courier COD reconciliation and cash-flow tool.** It would ingest courier remittance statements and APIs (Sendit, Cathedis, Ozon, Amana…), match them to orders, flag missing or late payouts, and forecast cash. The pain is documented: payout delays from 24 hours to 2 weeks, and couriers with different cadences. I found no dedicated player.
2. **True COD profit analytics.** This would join Meta and TikTok ad spend with lead → confirmation → delivery → remittance to show profit per delivered order, per product and per ad. The pain is documented (Tifawt's warning), and existing tools are partial or ERP-bound.
3. **Customer reputation network.** A cross-merchant score of refusal history based on hashed phone numbers. It would reduce fake orders and serial refusals, and its value grows with network effects. Legal and CNDP compliance (law 09-08) is the main risk and must be verified.
4. **Darija AI confirmation (WhatsApp plus voice).** Human confirmation costs MAD 5–10 per order or MAD 4,500–8,500 per agent per month. An AI agent that confirms, upsells and reschedules in Darija through WhatsApp could undercut that, but early players exist and WhatsApp Business API costs are unknown.
5. **Affiliate settlement ledger.** Commission splits between product owner, affiliate, confirmation center and courier, with payouts and disputes.
6. **Micro-seller compliance.** Simple invoicing, revenue tracking and DGI declaration help for social sellers and influencers, driven by enforcement news from 2024–2026.
- Distribution insight: courier and store-builder integrations (YouCan, Shopify, WooCommerce, Sendit, Cathedis, Ozon) are prerequisites. Partnering with couriers, who already hold remittance data and blacklists, could be faster than going direct to sellers.

### Gaps
- No evidence retrieved on WhatsApp Business API pricing or on WhatsApp automation tools in Morocco (search budget exhausted).
- No validation of willingness to pay for reconciliation, analytics or reputation tools (no seller surveys found).
- Legal feasibility of a shared customer blacklist under Moroccan data-protection law (09-08, CNDP) was not researched.
- No mapping of courier API availability or documentation quality (Sendit, Cathedis, Ozon, Amana, Speedaf, Forcelog, Onessta).
