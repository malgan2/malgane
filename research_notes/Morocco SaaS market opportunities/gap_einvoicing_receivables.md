## Q1. Has the e-invoicing implementing decree been published in the Bulletin Officiel by October 2026? What does PLF 2027 (n° 60.26) say about e-invoicing, cash, small businesses, auto-entrepreneurs or e-commerce?

### Takeaway
As of 22 Oct 2026 I found **no evidence that the implementing decree has been published** in the Bulletin Officiel. The latest dated statements (24 Aug and 5 Sept 2026) say it is still pending, and recent press says the DGI has not yet published the final technical architecture. The Government Council adopted PLF 2027 (n° 60.26) on 20 Oct 2026. Press coverage of its fiscal measures mentions **no e-invoicing, cash-ceiling, auto-entrepreneur or e-commerce provisions**. It is described as a "stabilisation" budget focused on FIFA 2030 tax guarantees, an IR (income-tax) reform and adjustments to earlier reforms. One uncorroborated opinion piece mentions a new progressive IS (corporate tax) scale for TPE with turnover ≤ MAD 5M.

### Inferences
- Assume e-invoicing is **not yet in force for anyone** as of late Oct 2026. Even if the decree is published in Q4 2026, the first wave is large companies and B2G. On the Le360 calendar, **TPME are not obliged before 2028–2029**. A TPE/AE product whose business case depends on a Jan 2027 mandate has a **12–30+ month timing gap**.
- PLF 2027 adds **no new forcing function** for small-business invoicing tools: no cash ceiling, no e-commerce measure, no AE change found. If the TPE IS scale (≤ MAD 5M) is confirmed, it may slightly raise the value of clean bookkeeping for TPE, but this is speculative.

### Gaps
- Full text and *note de présentation* of PLF 60.26 were not accessible (fetches blocked). It may contain e-invoicing, CGI Art. 145 or cash provisions that press snippets did not cover.
- The exact publication date of the Le360 "détail du calendrier" article and of the Hespress article.
- No official DGI communiqué on the decree after April 2026 was found.

## Q2. Which tools does the DGI provide for free for small taxpayers to issue e-invoices?

### Takeaway
The DGI has **publicly committed to free, simple tools for TPME**. A **free web portal, "fatourati.gov.ma"**, is widely reported by vendors and in at least one press summary. It is connected to the national clearance platform built by xHub. I found no evidence that it is live yet. Combined with the reported exclusion of auto-entrepreneurs and CPU taxpayers (Art. 145-XI CGI) and a TPME phase possibly after 2028, this **removes most of the willingness to pay for a basic "compliant invoice generator"** at the micro end.

### Cited Findings
- Challenge reports that the DGI Director General announced the 2026 launch and said **the system provides "simple, adapted and free solutions for TPME"**. The decree was then at the SGG. — [Challenge](https://www.challenge.ma/facturation-electronique-une-transition-sans-cout-pour-les-tpme-319928/)
- A search summary of recent Moroccan press coverage says "**a free online tool is planned for TPME, accessible at fatourati.gov.ma**". It surfaced alongside the Le360 calendar article; I could not verify which outlet says it. — [Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/facturation-electronique-le-detail-du-calendrier-de-mise-en-place_2EQSNG2DTVB6PGBBHQ6OCFT6CY/)
- Factureo (vendor) describes fatourati.gov.ma as **the DGI's free web e-invoicing interface for low-volume TPE/PME**. It is connected to the xHub platform, with deployment "during 2026 subject to the decree" and the TPE phase around 2027 (a forecast). **[VENDOR]** — [Factureo](https://www.factureo.ma/blog/fatourati-gov-ma-portail-gratuit-facturation-dgi)
- InvoiceGenAI and Faktora help-centre pages also explain "fatourati.gov.ma", calling it a free web-based tool with no installation needed. **[VENDOR]** — [InvoiceGenAI help](https://invoicegenai.ma/aide/conformite-dgi/c-est-quoi-fatourati-la-plateforme-e-facture-de-la-dgi); [Faktora](https://www.faktora.io/ressources/fatourati-gov-ma-c-est-quoi)
- **Name confusion:** Fatourati.ma, CMI's bill-payment site for water, electricity and telecom, is **not** the DGI e-invoicing portal. — [Faktora](https://www.faktora.io/ressources/fatourati-gov-ma-c-est-quoi); [InvoiceGenAI help](https://invoicegenai.ma/aide/conformite-dgi/c-est-quoi-fatourati-la-plateforme-e-facture-de-la-dgi)
- One vendor source says the Moroccan UBL profile, technical schemas and the list of certified providers **had not been published**. **[VENDOR]** — [InvoiceGenAI (UBL/CII)](https://invoicegenai.ma/aide/conformite-dgi/ubl-et-cii-les-formats-e-facture-de-la-dgi); consistent with [Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/486205-facturation-electronique-les-entreprises-marocaines-a-lepreuve-dune-reforme-majeure.html)
- A practitioner warns that many TPME may **outsource the task to a comptable agréé**, creating a hidden cost despite the free tool. — [LesEco (Fekkak Chanani)](https://leseco.ma/business/fekkak-chanani-la-vraie-preparation-se-mesure-a-la-capacite-du-dirigeant-a-comprendre-que-sa-facture-nest-plus-un-document-mais-une-donnee.html)
- Already in earlier notes: Art. 145-XI CGI reportedly **excludes auto-entrepreneurs and CPU taxpayers**. — [Legal Plus](https://legalplus.ma/article-145-du-cgi-facturation-electronique/); [VATupdate, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/07/02/briefing-document-podcast-e-invoicing-e-reporting-in-morocco/)
- Clearance model: every invoice passes through and is validated by the DGI platform before it counts as issued. **[VENDOR]** — [Sage Maroc](https://www.sage.com/fr-ma/blog/facturation-electronique-maroc-2026/)

### Inferences
- A **free state portal plus an AE/CPU exclusion** means a paid "basic DGI-compliant invoice maker" for TPE and AEs is **not a viable standalone business**. The micro segment will default to the free portal, free vendor tiers (see Q3) or their fiduciaire.
- What the free portal is unlikely to do, though this is unverified because its specs are not public:
  - multi-client management for fiduciaires;
  - quotes, catalogue and stock;
  - payment links and reconciliation;
  - receivables follow-up and reminders;
  - Arabic or Darija WhatsApp-native UX;
  - accounting export and bank feeds.
  These are where paid value could still sit.
- Fiduciaires may become the de facto "operators" of e-invoicing for TPME, so they, not the TPE, are the more likely buyer.

### Gaps
- Whether fatourati.gov.ma is live (even in pilot), and its functional scope: volume limits, API, signature handling, client directory.
- Whether the portal will allow fiduciaires to act on behalf of clients (delegation).
- Whether e-signature for portal users is free (Barid eSign or another provider) or a cost borne by the TPE.

## Q3. Which Moroccan invoicing/accounting SaaS target TPE and fiduciaires, with pricing, traction and e-invoicing readiness?

### Takeaway
**The category is crowded.** More than 20 local invoicing tools all claim "DGI-compliant", many with **free tiers** (InvoiceGenAI, Fawatir AI, Facturaty, Facture Facile, GFAE). There is also a funded freemium platform (Inyad, about $13.5–14M raised), a bank giveaway (Bank of Africa Hsabati Pro, free in year one, launched May 2026), a neobank with built-in invoicing and payment links (Zazu, now Manza, 1,000+ clients self-reported), Sage as the incumbent, Odoo integrators, and a cluster of fiduciaire-specific tools (KHABIR, Fiducio, Fidus, Experio, Telecompta, Comptably, Oasis). No player shows public, independently verified traction in TPE invoicing. Most cannot yet be truly "e-invoicing ready" because the DGI specs are unpublished.

### Inferences

### Gaps

## Q4. Do any Moroccan tools focus on receivables collection, payment reminders or late-payment law (Law 69-21) compliance? What fines apply and who must report?

### Takeaway
Law 69-21 creates a **hard quarterly compliance obligation for companies with turnover above MAD 2M**: about **56,000 declarants**, 43% of them reporting at least one late invoice. **MAD 1.33bn in fines were imposed in 2025.** Late payers pay fines **to the Treasury, not to the creditor**. I found **no Moroccan SaaS dedicated to receivables, dunning or 69-21 declaration**. The need is met by ERP modules (Crystal ERP, Sage 100), consultancies, collection agencies (Coface, CEREB), factoring (Maroc Factoring) and e-registered-letter services (LRE). **TPE and AEs under MAD 2M are excluded** from the law's obligations. They benefit only indirectly, as creditors of larger firms.

### Cited Findings
**Who must report and when**
- Scope: companies with **annual turnover excl. VAT > MAD 2M**; those ≤ MAD 2M are excluded. — [EcoActu](https://ecoactu.ma/delais-de-paiement-loi-69-21-penalites-infraction/); [Lembra Conseil](https://lembra-conseil.com/loi-69-21-delais-de-paiement-maroc/); [aube.ma](https://aube.ma/blog/declaration-delais-paiement-maroc-loi-69-21)
- The reporting threshold was phased down: **MAD 50M (2023) → MAD 10M (2024) → MAD 2M (2025)**, with **quarterly** declarations generalised from 2026. — [Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/delais-de-paiement-attention-le-fisc-elargit-son-radarce-quil-faut-faire-avant-le-31-mars_KKJ6A7NQUFBRVLAHOPJVIGRN7U/); [EcoActu](https://ecoactu.ma/retards-de-paiement-1-33-mmdh-amendes-2025/)
- DGI position of **27 Mar 2026 (ref. D 326/26/DGI)**: **no sectoral exclusion**; covers natural persons, SCPs and holdings. — [Upsilon Consulting](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/position-dgi-delais-paiement-personnes-physiques-scp-holdings/)
- Declarations are filed on **SIMPL-Délais de paiement** and are due **even if no invoice was late**. They are quarterly, due by the end of the month after the quarter. **Q3 2026 is due by 31 Oct 2026**, Q2 2026 was due by 31 Jul 2026, and the annual 2025 declaration was due by **1 Apr 2026** with spontaneous payment of the fine. — [aafir](https://www.aafir.ma/loi-69-21-un-tournant-pour-les-delais-de-paiement-au-maroc/); [aube.ma](https://aube.ma/blog/declaration-delais-paiement-maroc-loi-69-21); [Médias24, 24 Jul 2026](https://medias24.com/2026/07/24/dgi-les-principales-echeances-fiscales-a-respecter-avant-le-31-juillet-1730075/); [Médias24, 14 Mar 2026](https://medias24.com/2026/03/14/delais-de-paiement-declaration-obligatoire-avant-le-1er-avril-2026-1643453/); [Challenge](https://www.challenge.ma/delais-de-paiement-la-dgi-rappelle-aux-entreprises-lecheance-du-1er-avril-2026-317761/)

- The **Treasury is the beneficiary** of late-payment fines, which raises the question of what creditors actually recover. — [UGGC Africa](https://uggcafrica.com/fr/le-tresor-public-nouveau-beneficiaire-des-indemnites-de-retard-quels-risques-pour-les-creanciers/)
- Exception: no fine on invoices **issued before 1 Jan 2025 and below MAD 10,000 excl. VAT**. This comes from a search summary of 69-21 guides; the exact page is unverified. — [Lisa.ma](https://www.lisa.ma/delai-de-paiement-loi-69-21/); [Lembra Conseil](https://lembra-conseil.com/loi-69-21-delais-de-paiement-maroc/)
- **Non-filing fines conflict** across sources:
  - "MAD 5,000 to 250,000 depending on turnover" — [aube.ma](https://aube.ma/blog/declaration-delais-paiement-maroc-loi-69-21) / [aafir](https://www.aafir.ma/loi-69-21-un-tournant-pour-les-delais-de-paiement-au-maroc/)

### Inferences
- **A receivables, dunning and 69-21 tool is genuinely under-built in Morocco.** No dedicated local SaaS surfaced, only ERP modules, services and consultancies. The **paying buyer is the PME above MAD 2M** (about 56k declarants, two-thirds of them at MAD 2–10M), not the TPE or AE.
- The **declaration is a recurring quarterly pain**: invoice-level data on issue date, due date, payment date and delay, filed on SIMPL. It is produced from the **payables** side. A tool that generates the SIMPL declaration file from bank and invoice data, computes the 2.25% + 0.85%/month fine exposure and flags suppliers about to breach 60 days is a concrete, compliance-driven feature. This assumes SIMPL accepts file uploads, which I did not verify.
- For **creditor-side TPE** the law gives leverage but no direct cash, since fines go to the Treasury. Reminder workflows that cite the debtor's own fine exposure ("your fine is accruing at 2.25% + 0.85%/month") are a plausible differentiator. This is an inference with no evidence of effectiveness.
- **Risk:** ERP and accounting incumbents (Sage, Odoo, Crystal) can add a 69-21 export cheaply. A standalone product would need to be faster, cheaper, or distributed through fiduciaires, who already file the declaration for many clients.

- Whether SIMPL-Délais de paiement supports bulk file import (EDI/XML/Excel) or only manual entry. This determines the value of automation.
- Whether Sage, Odoo Morocco localisation or KHABIR already ship a 69-21 declaration export.
- No data on the share of 69-21 declarations filed by fiduciaires on clients' behalf.

## Q6 (synthesis). Is invoicing / receivables for TPE, AEs and fiduciaires underserved in Morocco, or already crowded?

### Takeaway
**Verdict: PARTLY SERVED, and the earlier "underserved" claim holds only for a narrow slice.**
- **Basic and e-invoicing for TPE and AEs:** well served to oversupplied, and partly pre-empted by a free state portal. It is **not underserved.**
- **Fiduciaire multi-client e-invoicing and collaboration:** partly served by fragmented tools, with Sage and KHABIR entrenched; the gap is timing-dependent.
- **Receivables, dunning and Law 69-21 declaration for PME above MAD 2M:** genuinely under-built as a dedicated SaaS category. The paying segment, however, is about 56k PME, not the micro long tail.

### Cited Findings
- More than 20 local invoicing tools, several free (InvoiceGenAI, Fawatir AI, Facturaty, Facture Facile). **[VENDOR]** — [invoicegenai.ma](https://invoicegenai.ma/tarifs); [fawatirai.com](https://fawatirai.com/facturation-auto-entrepreneur-maroc); [facturaty.ma](https://facturaty.ma/); [facturefacile.ma](https://facturefacile.ma/facture-auto-entrepreneur-maroc)
- Bank and neobank bundles: Hsabati Pro free in year one; Manza/Zazu invoices plus payment links. — [Finances News](https://fnh.ma/article/actualites-marocaines/bank-of-africa-lance-hsabati-pro-plateforme-digitale-gestion-entrepreneurs-maroc); [Médias24](https://medias24.com/2026/04/08/visa-et-zazu-lancent-un-compte-pro-100-digital-pour-les-entrepreneurs-marocains-1656073)
- Funded freemium incumbent: Inyad, about $13.5–14M raised. — [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/489893-68); [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/inyad)
- Free DGI tool promised for TPME. — [Challenge](https://www.challenge.ma/facturation-electronique-une-transition-sans-cout-pour-les-tpme-319928/); [Factureo](https://www.factureo.ma/blog/fatourati-gov-ma-portail-gratuit-facturation-dgi)
- Law 69-21 affects 56,041 declarants; MAD 1.33bn in fines in 2025; no dedicated local SaaS found. — [EcoActu](https://ecoactu.ma/retards-de-paiement-1-33-mmdh-amendes-2025/); [Crystal IT](https://crystalit.ma/blog/logiciel-recouvrement-creances-pme-maroc)

### Inferences
- **Strongest residual opportunity:** a **"receivables + 69-21 compliance" layer for PME (MAD 2–50M) distributed through fiduciaires**. Its parts:
  - aged balance and multichannel reminders (WhatsApp, SMS, LRE);
  - fine-exposure calculator;
  - auto-generated quarterly SIMPL declaration;
  - later, ingestion of DGI-cleared e-invoices once the decree lands.
  It monetises a recurring legal obligation that exists **today**, unlike e-invoicing, which is still pending. This is an inference; demand has not been validated with buyers.
- **Weakest opportunity:** a standalone paid e-invoicing or invoicing app for AEs and TPE. It faces free tiers, bank freebies, a free state portal, AE exclusion and a possibly post-2028 TPME timeline.
- **Main competitive threats to the residual opportunity:**
  1. Sage, Odoo, KHABIR or Crystal adding a 69-21 export;
  2. banks or neobanks adding reminder and payment-link features;
  3. fiduciaires doing the 69-21 declaration manually at low marginal cost.

### Gaps
- No primary demand evidence (surveys, interviews) on whether PME would pay for 69-21 automation or dunning, or at what price.
- No information on SIMPL-Délais de paiement's data-entry format, which is critical to the automation value.
- No independent traction data for any Moroccan invoicing SaaS. "Crowded" here means many offers, not proven many users.
