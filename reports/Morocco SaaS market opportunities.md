# Help Moroccan Businesses Get Paid Faster

Of your three options, pick a narrow part of the third: **formal small and mid-sized companies (SMEs) with more than MAD 2 million in yearly sales**, and help them get paid. Late payment is the best-documented business pain in Morocco, and companies were fined **MAD 1.33 billion** for paying late in 2025. The product would track unpaid invoices, send polite reminders by WhatsApp and email, and later run a paid collection desk. As a second, separate job, it would prepare the quarterly declaration of a firm's *own* late payments, which Law 69-21 requires from every company above MAD 2 million (**56,041 filed for 2025**). On closer checking I found no dedicated stand-alone tool for this, although Sage 100 and Crystal ERP already include reminders. Do not count on the product marketing itself: reminders will bring some leads, but far fewer than one new customer per customer. Expect a solid but modest business, about 1,000 paying firms after three years if it follows Moroccan precedent. Treat every figure here as untested until you have interviewed buyers.

| Question | Short answer |
|---|---|
| Business owners in general? | Too broad. The group runs from about 2 million informal traders to mid-sized firms, and their needs differ. |
| E-commerce sellers? | Avoid as a first market. There are only about 9,000–11,000 tracked web stores, most tools already exist, and only two narrow niches remain open. |
| Small businesses? | Yes, but only formal SMEs above MAD 2 million in sales. The smallest firms are already over-served by free and cheap tools. |
| Best alternative | A collection platform for *syndics*, the people or firms that manage apartment buildings, built on the same engine. |
| How to sell | Directly to SMEs and through their accountants. An accountant holds about 11 declaring firms on average, and Sage and KHABIR already court accountants. |
| Main risks | Demand is unproven. Sage, or the state itself, could automate the declaration. Data-protection and WhatsApp rules must be handled before the pilot. |
| Next step | 40 interviews with price tests. Start with the 31 October 2026 filing, nine days away. The next deadline is probably the end of January 2027 (my inference) or the end of March 2027. |

## Morocco is a market of tiny firms that are paid late, in cash and over WhatsApp

Morocco had **380,230 formal companies** at the end of 2024, and **94% of them sell less than MAD 10 million a year**. Only 5.5%, about 21,000 firms, count as mid-sized companies in the strict sense ([Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/457430-omtpme-tissu-entrepreneurial-en-croissance-en-2024-mais-sous-forte-pression.html)). (MAD, also written DH, is the Moroccan dirham; MAD 10 is roughly one US dollar.) Most companies are very small: **86.7% have yearly sales of MAD 3 million or less** ([CESE](https://www.cese.ma/media/2025/11/Rapport-MICRO-PE-TPE-VF-25.pdf)). There were also **463,383 registered auto-entrepreneurs** at the end of 2025 ([TelQuel](https://telquel.ma/instant-t/2026/06/29/le-nombre-dauto-entrepreneurs-au-maroc-depasse-les-460-000_1996444/)), although another count found only about 380,000 *active* ones in April 2026 ([Wafir](https://wafir.ma/en/actu/auto-entrepreneur-maroc-record-2026-380000-inscrits-fiscalite-csng)). Behind this formal layer is a much larger informal economy of **about 2.03 million unregistered production units**. 85.5% of them are run by one person, and almost half are in trade ([HCP](https://m.hcp.ma/file/244334); [Maroc.ma](https://maroc.ma/fr/actualites/le-secteur-informel-compte-environ-203-millions-dunites-de-production)). My estimate is that about 0.8 to 1 million formal businesses can legally sign a contract and pay an invoice, and most of them are very small. The informal units will rarely pay for a subscription.

The market is concentrated in one region, and companies open and close quickly. The Casablanca-Settat region holds **37.8% of all companies and about 60% of national turnover**, and the Casablanca prefecture alone holds more than 86% of the region's companies ([OMTPME](https://omtpme.ma/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/RR-2026-Casablanca-Settat-Vsite-web.pdf)). That means about one formal company in three is in a single city, which makes Casablanca the natural first market. About 109,656 companies were created in 2025 ([Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/465433-maroc-109-656-entreprises-creees-en-2025.html)). The credit-information firm Inforisk counted 15,307 failures that year. It also estimates that about **300,000 "zombie" companies (firms that are barely alive) pass risk to others through unpaid invoices** ([Finances News](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/defaillances-entreprises-baisse-faillites); [Finances News](https://www.fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/defaillances-entreprises-societes-zombies)). That point already hints at this report's main finding: in Morocco, unpaid bills pass from one company to the next.

Customers are on their phones and on WhatsApp. About **92% of the population is online**, or 35.5 million users at the end of October 2025 ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-morocco)). WhatsApp is used by **75% of people, and 95% of those users open it every day** ([Hespress](https://en.hespress.com/112276-moroccans-flock-to-whatsapp-facebook-and-instagram-as-social-media-use-hits-81.html)). Language matters. French is still the written language of business: 57.7% of literate residents can read and write it ([HCP](https://m.hcp.ma/file/242669)). Darija, the Moroccan form of Arabic, is used in 69% of professional speech but in only 39% of professional writing ([Yabiladi](https://en.yabiladi.com/articles/details/164733/darija-dominates-french-leads-professionally.html)). In practice, a product should show invoices and documents in French, and use Darija and Arabic for its messages, onboarding and support.

Software use is shallow and prices are low. A 2024 World Bank survey of 1,256 firms found that **only 31% use specialised software and barely 8% have an ERP**, a full business-management system ([Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/483014-483014.html)). Local invoicing tools charge about **MAD 60–300 a month** (US$6–30). Mid-market management tools charge MAD 250–500 a month or MAD 6,500–10,000 a year ([deadline.ma](https://deadline.ma/conseils/systemes-erp-crm/erp-marocains-editeurs-logiciels-gestion); [Facturis](https://site.facturis.ma/tarifs/)). One analysis of the Moroccan software market says buyers prefer a "do it for me" service to self-service software. It also says they fear that digital records will attract tax audits ([HNB](https://www.hnb.ma/p/lavenir-des-startups-saas-au-maroc)). That is a single source, but it fits everything else in this research.

Cash still dominates. Only 44% of adults had a bank account in 2024 ([Hespress](https://en.hespress.com/116233-116233.html)). A sponsored industry article says only 4–5% of about 1.2 million merchants have a card terminal ([Médias24](https://medias24.com/nativeads/debat-la-can-2025-catalyseur-de-la-digitalisation-des-paiements-au-maroc/)). Bank transfers are the payment method that is growing: instant transfers rose 78% in 2025, and the central bank, Bank Al-Maghrib, is preparing to use them for payments to merchants ([Médias24](https://medias24.com/2026/10/22/virements-instantanes-78-en-2025-bam-prepare-les-paiements-marchands-1782783)).

Foreign software also faces currency limits. A company without a foreign-currency account can pay for imported services by international card only up to **MAD 200,000 a year** ([Office des Changes](https://www.oc.gov.ma/fr/faq/les-entreprises-marocaines-peuvent-elles-regler-par-carte-de-paiement-internationale-leurs)). For individuals the limit is MAD 20,000 ([LesMRE](https://www.lesmre.com/fr/actualites/igoc-2026-office-changes-plafonds-voyage-ecommerce-scolarite-mre)). The company limit does not stop most SMEs from buying foreign software, but it adds friction, so it favours a local product that bills in dirhams by bank transfer somewhat, not decisively.

Morocco's successful business-tech companies did not grow by advertising "better management". Chari's founder visited about **50,000 shops** in person ([Managers.tn](https://managers.tn/2024/05/20/rencontrez-ismael-belkhayat-lentrepreneur-marocain-qui-a-visite-50-000-magasins)). DabaDoc, the doctor-booking platform, was free for about 18 months. It then charged doctors MAD 450 a month, or MAD 225 a month if they paid for a full year ([TelQuel](https://telquel.ma/2015/05/25/dabadoc-service-marocain-sante-en-ligne-sexporte-en-algerie-en-tunisie_1448476)). The restaurant till LaCaisse.ma started in 2016 and reached about 1,000 clients roughly five years later, with about 15 staff ([Finances News](https://www.fnh.ma/article/actualite-entreprises/start-up-marocaine-lacaisse-ma-voit-grand); [Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/journal/2021/fintech-lacaisse-sattaque-aux-marches-africain-europe/363959.html)). The one fast case, the store builder YouCan, passed 1,000 merchants in its first year by growing inside the online-selling community ([Challenge](https://www.challenge.ma/youcan-shop-prend-son-envol-au-maroc-274332/)). Each of these winners connected itself to money that was already moving, such as shop orders, patient visits or parcels. This report follows the same pattern.

### Terms used in this report

|---|---|
| **SME** | A small or mid-sized company. In this report it means a formal company with more than MAD 2 million in yearly sales, the Law 69-21 threshold. |
| **TPE / PME** | *Très petite entreprise* (very small firm) / *petite et moyenne entreprise* (small or mid-sized firm). Sources define these classes differently. The OMTPME counts firms with MAD 3–10 million in yearly sales as TPE and firms with MAD 3 million or less as micro-enterprises ([OMTPME](https://omtpme.ma/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Annual-Report-2024_-OMTPME.pdf)). |
| **Fiduciaire** | A small accounting and bookkeeping firm that does the books, tax filings and payroll for small companies. The title is not legally protected, so anyone can use it. |
| **Expert-comptable / comptable agréé** | A chartered accountant who can certify accounts / a licensed accountant who can keep and close accounts but not certify them. |
| **Auto-entrepreneur (AE)** | A one-person business status with a simple tax on sales and low sales caps. |
| **CPU** | *Contribution professionnelle unique*, a simple flat tax for small traders. |
| **Hanout / moul hanout / karne** | A neighbourhood grocery shop / its owner / the paper notebook in which the shop records what customers owe. |
| **COD** | Cash on delivery: the buyer pays the courier in cash when the parcel arrives. |
| **DGI** | *Direction Générale des Impôts*, the national tax administration. |
| **SIMPL** | The DGI's free online filing portal, with sections for VAT, income tax and late-payment declarations. |
| **CNSS / AMO / Damancom** | The national social-security fund / the mandatory health insurance it manages / its online portal for monthly salary declarations. |
| **FSE** | *Feuille de soins électronique*, the CNSS electronic care sheet that doctors and pharmacies must issue and scan. |
| **Law 69-21** | The late-payment law. It sets maximum payment terms and fines, and requires firms to declare their own payment delays. |
| **Receivables / payables** | Money your customers owe you / money you owe your suppliers. |
| **Policy rate** | The central bank's main interest rate (2.25%). Law 69-21 uses it to set late-payment fines. |
| **Fine exposure** | The late-payment fine a company is building up on bills it has not yet paid. |
| **RAS** | *Retenue à la source*, withholding tax: the paying company keeps part of the tax and pays it directly to the state. |
| **Reconcile / reconciliation** | Matching each payment received to the invoice it pays, so you know exactly what is still owed. |
| **Mise en demeure** | A formal written demand for payment, normally sent before going to court. |
| **LRE** | *Lettre recommandée électronique*, an electronic registered letter that gives legal proof of delivery. |
| **Success fee** | A fee charged only on money actually recovered. |
| **Self-spreading loop / K** | A loop is when normal use of a product shows it to new possible buyers, some of whom sign up. K is the average number of new customers each customer brings in. Below 1, the loop lowers the cost of growth but cannot grow the product on its own. |
| **Free-to-paid conversion** | The share of free users who later start paying. |
| **WhatsApp Business API / utility template** | The paid WhatsApp channel that lets software send business messages / a pre-approved transactional message, such as a reminder or receipt. |
| **API / CSV** | A technical connection that lets two programs exchange data / a simple spreadsheet file format that most accounting software can export. |
| **Interchange** | The fee that the cardholder's bank receives from the merchant's bank on each card payment. |
| **Letter of intent** | A signed, non-binding statement that a partner plans to work with you. |
| **Seed / Series A** | A startup's first and second main rounds of outside investment. |
| **Syndic / copropriété** | The manager (a volunteer owner or a professional firm) of a co-owned apartment building / the co-owned building itself. |
| **CMI / Bank Al-Maghrib (BAM)** | The main card-payment processor / Morocco's central bank, which licenses payment companies. |
| **CNDP / Law 09-08** | The data-protection authority / the personal-data law it enforces. |
| **ICE** | The company identification number that must appear on invoices. |
| **MRE** | *Marocains résidant à l'étranger*, Moroccans living abroad. |
| **Darija** | Moroccan Arabic, the everyday spoken language. |

## Target formal SMEs, not e-commerce sellers or micro-shops

The three groups in your question overlap. Almost every Moroccan business owner runs a small business, and many of them also sell online. A more useful way to split the market is by how formal a business is and how money reaches it. The table compares the four groups that matter.

| Group | Size (best estimate) | Strongest pain | Who already serves it | Can it pay? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E-commerce sellers (mostly COD) | About 9,000–11,000 tracked YouCan and Shopify stores; sellers who use only social media are not counted | Refused parcels, slow cash from couriers, confirming orders by phone | YouCan (store, shipping, payments), eGrow, many small COD tools, Darija AI bots | Some, per order | Crowded and small; avoid as a first market (two narrow niches remain) |
| Micro-shops, auto-entrepreneurs, informal traders | 100,000+ grocery shops; 380–463k auto-entrepreneurs; 2.03M informal units | Customer credit, stock, cash | Chari, WafR, Woliz, z.systems, Inyad, free apps | Very little | Owned by funded players; avoid |
| Micro and small formal firms below MAD 2M in sales, needing basic invoicing | Most of the 380k companies (86.7% sell MAD 3M or less) | Simple invoices, future e-invoicing | 20+ local invoicing apps (many free), a free bank tool, a promised free state portal | Low | Over-served; use only as a free layer |
| Formal SMEs above MAD 2M in sales, and their accountants | 56,041 firms filed late-payment declarations for 2025; about 2,550 licensed accountants plus about 2,400 without official status, plus unregistered *fiduciaires* | Getting paid late, plus a quarterly legal declaration of their own late payments | Reminder modules in Sage 100 and Crystal ERP, collection agencies; no dedicated stand-alone tool found | Likely; to be tested | **Target this group** |

**E-commerce sellers have real pains but form a small, crowded market.** Card-paid online sales reached **44.8 million transactions worth MAD 12.1 billion in 2025** ([Le Reporter](https://www.lereporter.ma/bank-al-maghrib-hausse-de-20-du-nombre-des-paiements-par-cartes-bancaires-en-2025/)), and 24.9% of Moroccans bought something online in 2024 ([LeBrief](https://www.lebrief.ma/e-commerce-un-quart-des-marocains-achetent-en-ligne-100129064/)). Most orders, **more than 66%**, are paid in cash on delivery ([Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/societe/e-commerce-le-maroc-peut-il-creer-son-propre-paypal/346218)). A vendor guide says only 35–50 of every 100 leads (people who place an order request) end up as paid deliveries ([Tifawt](https://tifawt.ma/guide-cod-maroc/)). That figure is a vendor rule of thumb, not an independent study.

The seller base, however, is small. Trackers count about **5,089 live YouCan stores in Morocco** ([StoreLeads](https://storeleads.app/reports/youcan/MA/top-stores)) and about 3,900–5,600 Shopify stores ([Storecensus](https://www.storecensus.com/stores/country/morocco); [Bootleads](https://bootleads.com/stores/shopify/countries/ma/)). YouCan's worldwide store count fell from a peak of 70,089 to 43,866 ([StoreLeads](https://storeleads.app/reports/youcan)). On closer checking, the problem of matching cash from several couriers to orders is also partly solved. YouCan Ship pays its merchants within 24 hours of delivery ([YouCan](https://youcan.shop/en/help/application/YouCanShip-app)). eGrow claims connections to about 60 couriers ([eGrow](https://www.egrow.com/en/cod-management-tool)), but that is a vendor claim. Darija AI order confirmation is sold by at least four WhatsApp vendors and four voice vendors, including Babliy, AtlasAI and Confirmation-Maroc, which charges MAD 5 per confirmation ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/babliy); [AtlasAI](https://atlasai.ma/en/blog/ia-vocale-darija-francais-avenir-telephonie/); [Confirmation-Maroc](https://confirmation-maroc.com/)).

One main e-commerce idea remains unclaimed: a shared score of buyers who refuse parcels. Pooling customer data across companies needs prior permission from the CNDP, and an undeclared data file can be fined **MAD 10,000–100,000** ([Médias24](https://medias24.com/2023/07/01/donnees-personnelles-ce-que-prevoit-la-loi-en-cas-de-violations/); [CNDP](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/deliberation-n-32-2015-13-02-2015.pdf)). In addition, YouCan and the couriers already hold the data, so this idea only works if you partner with one of them. Two narrower niches are also still open. The first is checking each courier's payout statement line by line for sellers who have direct contracts with several couriers; only a Pakistani app, Kaarobar, clearly advertises this ([Shopify](https://apps.shopify.com/kaarobar?locale=ja)). The second is a profit dashboard fed by Moroccan courier data; EcomTracker comes close, but no tool shows publicly that it reads Moroccan courier data ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/ecomtracker/makers)). Both are small add-ons, not a business on their own.

**Micro-shops are already being digitised by well-funded players.** A 2026 survey of 4,559 grocers counted **more than 100,000 neighbourhood grocery shops**, which handle nearly 80% of everyday consumer-goods sales ([Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/distribution-70-des-decisions-dachat-se-jouent-en-magasin-pendant-ramadan_7KKU3HO54NEWDD4H6QQEWRXNTM/)). About 89% of shop transactions are in cash, according to a single source ([LaunchBase Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/?p=11208)). Several funded companies already serve these shops. **Chari** raised a $12M Series A and holds a payment licence from Bank Al-Maghrib ([Disrupt Africa](https://disruptafrica.com/2025/10/16/moroccan-b2b-e-commerce-startup-chari-closes-12m-series-a-round/)). **WafR** works through about 20,000 shops after a $4M seed round ([Daba Finance](https://dabafinance.com/en/news/morocco-fintech-wafr-seed-round-financial-inclusion)). **z.systems** signed a government deal to equip up to 50,000 small shops by 2030 ([TelQuel](https://telquel.ma/instant-t/2025/09/30/mezzour-annonce-la-tenue-des-assises-du-commerce-en-2026_1953035/)), and **Woliz** has a deal covering 20,000 shops ([The Startup Scene](https://thestartupscene.me/PowerLists/Inside-the-Billion-Dollar-Race-to-Digitise-Morocco-s-Small-Shops)).

Similar free apps in other countries spread quickly but lost money. India's Khatabook earned about **US$12 million** in revenue in its 2024 financial year and lost about **US$14 million** ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2024/11/ms-dhoni-backed-khatabook-clocks-rs-1027-cr-revenue-cuts-losses-7-in-fy24)), and Indonesia's Lummo was liquidated in 2023 ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/bukukas)). In Morocco itself, YoLa Fresh, which supplied fresh produce to shops, shut down in October 2026 after it failed to raise new money ([Tech In Africa](https://www.techinafrica.com/moroccan-agritech-startup-yola-fresh-shuts-down-after-failing-to-secure-funding/)).

**Basic invoicing for very small firms will soon cost nothing.** Some vendor calendars say small firms must buy e-invoicing software from January 2027, but on closer checking that date does not hold. The decree that sets the e-invoicing rules had **still not been published on 5 September 2026** ([Manageo](https://www.manageo.io/facturation-electronique/)), and the DGI had not published the final technical design ([Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/486205-facturation-electronique-les-entreprises-marocaines-a-lepreuve-dune-reforme-majeure.html)). The head of the DGI promised **"simple, adapted and free" tools for small firms** ([Challenge](https://www.challenge.ma/facturation-electronique-une-transition-sans-cout-pour-les-tpme-319928/)), which vendors describe as a free portal at fatourati.gov.ma ([Factureo](https://www.factureo.ma/blog/fatourati-gov-ma-portail-gratuit-facturation-dgi)). Auto-entrepreneurs and CPU taxpayers are reportedly excluded from the obligation ([Legal Plus](https://legalplus.ma/article-145-du-cgi-facturation-electronique/)). One calendar places small firms **after 2028** ([Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/facturation-electronique-le-detail-du-calendrier-de-mise-en-place_2EQSNG2DTVB6PGBBHQ6OCFT6CY/)). The draft 2027 budget law adds no e-invoicing or cash measures ([Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/finances-publiques-ce-quil-faut-retenir-du-plf-2027_DXSQSPFNDFFG7MEKKPNWCFBQNA/)).

The paid tools are crowded as well. There are more than 20 local invoicing brands, and several have free plans ([InvoiceGenAI](https://invoicegenai.ma/tarifs)). Bank of Africa launched **Hsabati Pro, free for the first year**, in May 2026 ([Finances News](https://www.fnh.ma/article/actualites-marocaines/bank-of-africa-lance-hsabati-pro-plateforme-digitale-gestion-entrepreneurs-maroc)). Inyad has raised about $13.5–14M ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/489893-68)). The online business account Zazu, renamed Manza in September 2026, includes invoices and payment links ([Médias24](https://medias24.com/2026/04/08/visa-et-zazu-lancent-un-compte-pro-100-digital-pour-les-entrepreneurs-marocains-1656073)). A paid invoice generator for very small firms is no longer a viable business.

**Formal SMEs above MAD 2 million in sales are the right customers.** These firms now carry a recurring legal duty under Law 69-21. For 2025, **56,041 of them filed declarations, and 63.5% had sales of MAD 2–10 million** ([EcoActu](https://ecoactu.ma/retards-de-paiement-1-33-mmdh-amendes-2025/)). The 2026 count may be higher, because in March 2026 the DGI said the duty also covers individuals in business, professional partnerships and holding companies ([Upsilon](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/position-dgi-delais-paiement-personnes-physiques-scp-holdings/)). These firms can pay more than micro-firms, and many work with an accountant. Morocco has **887 chartered accountants working in 609 firms** ([Médias24](https://medias24.com/2026/04/23/ordre-des-experts-comptables-elections-sur-fond-de-reflexion-sur-louverture-du-capital-1665029/)); a private directory says 400, but I use the professional order's own count. It also has about **1,665 licensed accountants and about 2,400 bookkeepers without official status** ([Finances News](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/comptables-agrees-la-profession-mieux-encadree)), plus many unregistered *fiduciaires*, because the title is not legally protected ([Upsilon](https://upsilon-consulting.com/choisir-expert-comptable-maroc/)).

The head of the accountants' order says **only 2% of companies have certified accounts** ([Médias24](https://medias24.com/2026/04/12/faycal-mekouar-2-des-entreprises-seulement-ont-des-comptes-certifies-est-ce-normal-1658424/)), which suggests that most small companies rely on this layer of accountants for their books. But the accountant channel is thinner than it first looks. About 56,000 declaring firms shared among about 2,550 licensed accountants and about 2,400 bookkeepers without status gives **about 11 declaring firms per accountant on average**, and fewer once unregistered *fiduciaires* are counted. With about 87% of companies selling MAD 3 million or less, most *fiduciaires* very likely serve mainly micro clients below MAD 2 million, who do not file the declaration. The channel is also contested: a Sage reseller advertises special prices for accountants ([Nodma Horizon](https://nodmahorizon.ma/nodma/sage/)), KHABIR offers accountants a partner rate ([NSE/KHABIR](https://www.nse-ma.com/fr/logiciel-de-comptabilite-khabir/)), and ExpertC, Fiducio and Experio sell directly to *fiduciaires* ([ExpertC](https://www.expertc.ma/); [Fiducio](https://fiducio.ma/); [Experio](https://experio.ma/)). An accountant who adopts the product will bring a handful of paying clients, not dozens, so you must also sell directly to SMEs.

## Closer checking closed most gaps that first looked open

Many ideas looked "underserved" at first. I then searched hard for competitors to each of them, and the table shows the result.

| Idea | How useful | Does it spread by itself? | What closer checking found | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unpaid-invoice reminders and a collection desk for SMEs above MAD 2M, plus the Law 69-21 declaration, sold directly and via accountants | High: best-documented pain; the declaration is a legal duty already in force | Partly: some reminders reach SMEs that could buy, but many reach large firms and the state; expected K well below 1; an accountant brings about 11 declaring clients on average | No dedicated stand-alone tool; Sage 100 and Crystal ERP include reminders; unknown whether Sage, Odoo or KHABIR already export the declaration | **Best opportunity, to be validated** |
| Platform for volunteer and small professional syndics | High: unpaid charges are the sector's top problem | Strong inside a building; between buildings mainly through syndic firms and developers, not by viral spread | Large syndic firms built their own apps; one small independent tool; the long tail is open | **Best alternative** |
| Car-rental back office and renter-trust layer | Medium-high: deposits, theft, traffic fines | Moderate: renters are not buyers | 6+ small local tools; a funded marketplace claims 1,000+ agencies | Third choice |
| Private-school fee billing and collection | Medium | Moderate: parents see it, owners decide | Two funded local players, one card-payment vendor, a free state parent app | A later use of the same engine |
| Driving-school back office | Medium | Weak | Only one local tool; the state owns exam booking | Too small and depends on regulators |
| CNSS electronic care sheet (FSE) for doctors and pharmacies | Medium: the law is passed, but the rollout keeps slipping | Weak for pharmacies; doctors have booking links, but DabaDoc holds the patient side | Pharmacies: Sobrus claims about 60% and has a bank payment partner, and the FSE works inside existing pharmacy software. Doctors: 18,542 in private practice, served by 5+ local tools at MAD 200–650 a month | Avoid as a first market; at most a later add-on for specialties with no tool |
| Tax-declaration help for influencers and social-media sellers | Low-medium: a yearly online declaration is now enforced | Weak | Thresholds are disputed; very low ability to pay; no competitor check was done | Avoid; at most a free layer |
| Multi-client e-invoicing for accountants | Medium, later | Strong (one accountant, many clients) | Sage, KHABIR, Fiducio, Experio and others; decree still pending | Add once the decree is published |
| Basic invoicing / e-invoicing for very small firms | Low as a paid product | Weak | 20+ tools, a free bank tool, a promised free state portal | Avoid |
| COD cash reconciliation and profit dashboards | Medium | Weak | YouCan Ship, eGrow, EcomTracker; line-by-line payout checks found only in a Pakistani app | Avoid unless partnered; two narrow niches open |
| Darija AI order confirmation | Medium | Weak | At least 8 vendors | Avoid |
| Shared score of buyers who refuse COD parcels | High | Very strong (more users make it more valuable) | Nobody, but needs CNDP permission and courier data | Only with a courier or YouCan |
| Salon booking | Low-medium | Weak toward other salons | Fresha plus 6 local apps, none with visible traction | Avoid |
| Restaurant tills and QR menus | Low | Weak (diners are not buyers) | 7+ vendors from MAD 149 a month | Avoid |
| Grocery-shop credit book or till | Low for a newcomer | Strong but already owned | Chari, WafR, Woliz, z.systems, Inyad | Avoid |
| Self-service payroll and CNSS filing | Medium | Weak (payslips go to employees) | Clicpaie, Humantal and others; not checked in depth | Secondary |

**Two mandates in the notes do not change the choice.** The CNSS electronic care sheet is the clearest sector mandate: doctors' and pharmacies' software must create and scan QR-coded care sheets. A pilot was planned in Kénitra for March 2026 ([Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/nation/amo-la-feuille-de-soins-electronique-pour-mars-2026/329430)), but in May 2026 the system was still being approved ([212Assurances](https://www.212assurances.com/2026/05/20/feuille-de-soins-electronique-la-cnss-accelere-sa-transformation-digitale-et-promet-des-remboursements-plus-rapides/)). The care sheet works inside existing pharmacy software without a new tool ([Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/societe/cnss-comment-fonctionne-la-nouvelle-feuille-de-soins-electronique/336558)), and Sobrus says it equips about 7,000 pharmacies, a figure from sponsored content ([TelQuel](https://telquel.ma/sponsors/gitex-e-health-la-fantastique-ascension-de-sobrus_1872385)). Morocco has **18,542 private doctors** ([Aujourd'hui le Maroc](https://aujourdhui.ma/societe/carte-sanitaire-le-maroc-compte-34-173-medecins-dans-le-public-et-le-prive-en-2025)), and they can already choose among cheap local practice tools at MAD 200–650 a month ([Cabdivin](https://cabdivin.com/fr/logiciel-cabinet-medical-maroc)). So pharmacies are locked, doctors are partly served, and the rollout date is uncertain. The second mandate is tax enforcement on influencers and online sellers, who must now declare all income and file a yearly online declaration ([Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/le-fisc-resserre-letau-sur-les-influenceurs-et-le-commerce-en-ligne_FPNXVQV3UZGTZCGZDLPMUN54ZE/)). These people are newly visible to the tax office, but they are micro-sellers with little ability to pay, so they suit a free layer at most.

**Customer-facing booking and ordering tools have been copied many times.** These products look attractive because every booking link or menu shows the brand to the public. On closer checking, the market is full of thin copies. In beauty, Fresha runs pages for Casablanca and Marrakech ([Fresha](https://www.fresha.com/lp/en/bt/spas/in/ma-casablanca)), and at least six local booking apps exist, including RendezVoo, Mawid and Salonia. None of them shows funding or press coverage ([RendezVoo](https://www.rendezvoo.ma/); [Mawid](https://mawidapp.com/); [Salonia](https://salonia-maroc.com/)). Salonia lets clients propose their own price and pay cash on site, which shows how far local habits are from pre-paid booking. Restaurant tools are close to free: Mymenu sells commission-free online ordering from MAD 149 a month ([Mymenu](https://mymenu.ma/)), and QR menus cost from MAD 250 a year, although that MyQR price comes from an old page (about 2020) and may be out of date ([MyQR](https://www.myqr.ma/)).

Private schools have funded local players. Schoolify raised MAD 3 million and claims 340+ schools worldwide, a founder claim made in a sponsored article ([Finances News](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-entreprises/schoolify)). KoolSkools raised $960,000 ([Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2023/02/koolskools-raises-960000-azur-fund)), Eduka connects school fees to card payments ([Eduka](https://www.edukasoftware.com/integration/integration-cmi-ecom/)), and the state's Massar system has offered free parent apps since 2021 ([EcoActu](https://ecoactu.ma/?p=75754)). Driving schools have almost no software; only WimDrive appeared ([GoAfrica](https://www.goafricaonline.com/ma/user/330733-wimdrive-ar)). But the road-safety agency NARSA runs exam booking on its own new platform ([Hespress](https://www.hespress.com/1762782-1762782.html)), and nobody knows how many schools exist.

**Car rental is weaker than it first looked.** There are **11,246 agencies with 201,462 cars, and most agencies run fewer than seven cars** ([Bladi](https://www.bladi.net/000-agences-marche-eclate-fragilites-location-voitures-maroc,118515.html)); the union gives about 11,800 ([SNRT News](https://snrtnews.com/fr/article/avant-de-louer-une-voiture-droits-et-obligations-pour-proteger-le-client-et-lagence-155733)). Agency owners have real problems. Renters using fake identities steal cars, and the agencies' federation asked the national police to help verify renter IDs ([Bladi](https://www.bladi.net/location-voitures-maroc-secteur-menace-vols,110353.html)). Traffic fines are another burden: agencies pay the fines of renters who live abroad, and for renters who live in Morocco they must forward a signed declaration within 30 days of receiving a fine ([Aujourd'hui le Maroc](https://aujourdhui.ma/societe/infractions-au-code-de-la-route-les-agences-de-location-de-voitures-payent-pour-leurs-clients-residents-a-letranger-74476)).

But the market is no longer empty. At least six small local tools are on sale, including LocaFlotte and RentFlow ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.fr/software/1108972/locaflotte); [VC4A](https://vc4a.com/ventures/rentflow-ma/)). The UAE marketplace OneClickDrive says it works with **more than 1,000 Moroccan agencies**, which is a self-reported figure ([Finances News](https://fnh.ma/article/actualites-marocaines/oneclickdrive)). It is also buying 1,000 cars for partner agencies, with a revenue guarantee ([Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/oneclickdrive-engage-un-programme-dacquisition-de-1000-vehicules-en-2026-pour-transformer-la_GIOKAFBQJJETFESRUZPRT2ST5I/)).

**Syndic software is only "partly served", but the long tail of small buildings is open.** An undated single source counts about **one million co-owned homes, more than 40% of them in Casablanca and half of them social housing** ([La Vie Éco](https://www.lavieeco.com/?p=17247)). The 2024 census supports the general picture: **24.4% of urban households live in apartments** ([Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/401962-rgph-2024-plus-de-60-des-menages-vivent-dans-des-maisons-modernes.html)). The president of the co-ownership association AMCOP says owners not paying their charges is "by far" the main problem ([Finances News](https://www.fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/copropriete-interview)). The large syndic firms already give residents their own apps. Thaïs serves 70 residences and 10,000 co-owners and offers online payment ([Infomédiaire](https://www.infomediaire.net/?p=134472)), and K Syndic serves 4,000+ co-owners ([H24info](https://h24info.ma/?p=616804)). The only independent tool found, Syndic Connect, shows no traction or funding ([SNRT News](https://snrtnews.com/fr/article/syndic-connect-plateforme-de-gestion-de-copropriete-au-maroc-93594)). No licensing law for syndics was found, so there is no legal push yet to professionalise them.

**The hoped-for shortcuts to reach customers mostly do not exist.** Consultancies claimed that the Mowakaba programme pays 80–90% of software costs ([gest360](https://gest360.ma/blog/mowakaba-financement-digitalisation-maroc-2026)). The only official scheme found is a June 2025 decree that pays **up to 30% of a project's cost** for investment, modernisation and digital transformation. It is built around investment projects, and nothing shows that monthly software subscriptions qualify ([Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/449569-449569.html)). Telecom operators sponsor programmes but do not resell business software ([Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/gitex-africa-2025-partenariat-strategique-entre-inwi-et-lapebi-en-faveur-des-pme-tech-marocaines_UHJHUYMDLJAO3KGTPERH6P2S5A/)). Banks either build their own tools, like Hsabati Pro, or partner only with companies that already lead a sector, like Attijari Payment with the pharmacy-software leader Sobrus ([Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/paiement-electronique-et-pharmacie-attijari-payment-et-sobrus-structurent-une-offre-integree_DKKGIQ6UUNAZJO3DAFEP3UNTYU/)). YouCan controls the network of e-commerce trainers and affiliates and pays them a lifetime revenue share ([YouCan](https://youcan.shop/en/affiliate)). After closer checking, two gaps remain strong: unpaid B2B invoices and unpaid building charges. Both are about collecting money that someone owes.

## Self-marketing works only when your message reaches another buyer

The only self-spreading mechanism with published numbers is a document that your customer sends to *its own* customers. In India, OkCredit, a credit-book app for small shops, sends payment reminders that recipients can read without installing anything. About **3 in 10 people who receive a reminder are merchants themselves**, and the company gained about **two organic merchants for every merchant it paid to acquire**. These are company and investor statements ([Lightspeed](https://lsvp.com/stories/empowering-indias-60m-offline-merchants-2/); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2021/03/product-roadmap-fintech-startup-okcredit-800-million-transactions/amp)). Khatabook gained 400,000+ businesses in about a year without paid ads ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/sequoias-surge-backed-khatabook-in-talks-to-raise-20-mn)). Even OkCredit's loop was sub-viral: each user brought in fewer than one new user (K below 1), so the loop lowered the cost of growth but did not drive growth on its own. It also worked best in chains of very small businesses, where a shop's customers are often shops themselves. No other company has published comparable numbers.

The evidence also carries two warnings. First, when the people who see the message are consumers, such as diners, salon clients, car renters or parents, the loop spreads your customer's brand, not your product. That is why Morocco has a dozen copies of QR menus and booking pages and no clear winner. Second, spreading for free is not the same as earning money. The free credit-book apps grew fast and then lost money or closed (see above), so you need to plan how you will earn revenue before you scale the free part.

Legal deadlines often come with paying customers, but the size of the effect is unknown. In Saudi Arabia, two accounting-software companies grew while the tax authority, ZATCA, phased in e-invoicing rules. Qoyod reports **25,000+ businesses**, although its homepage says 35,000, so the vendor figures are inconsistent ([Qoyod](https://www.qoyod.com/en/)). Wafeq reports **10,000+ customers and about 2 million invoices a month** ([yespress](https://yespress.io/wafeq)). Both run partner programmes for accounting firms, with partner levels and a public list of partners ([Wafeq](https://www.wafeq.com/en/business-hub/for-business/wafeq-partners-program-for-accounting-firms); [Qoyod](https://www.qoyod.com/en/accounting-software/core-accounting-for-accountants/zatca-integration/)). This is a correlation, not proof: no data shows how much of this growth the rules caused, and the role of the partner programmes is inferred from their design, not measured. Morocco's obligation is also weaker. Saudi rules force companies to connect their invoicing software to the state platform ([Sowaan ERP](https://www.sowaanerp.sa/zatca-phase-1-vs-phase-2/)), while the Moroccan late-payment declaration is filed on the free SIMPL portal and forces no software purchase. Even so, the Saudi companies raised only $2–7.5M Series A rounds, which points to solid businesses, not explosive ones.

Even good loops need people on the ground in cash economies. Nigeria's Moniepoint uses about **40,000 field agents** ([LeapFrog](https://leapfroginvest.com/company/moniepoint/)). The Moroccan channels that have worked share one feature: they plug into a flow that someone else already owns. Glovo orders flow straight into the LaCaisse till ([Médias24](https://medias24.com/2024/04/26/glovo-sassocie-a-la-start-up-marocaine-lacaisse-ma/)), and the courier Sendit spreads through a free Shopify app ([Shopify](https://apps.shopify.com/sendit)). Chambers of commerce, the state's DigiTPME network for small firms, launched in April 2026 ([Maroc.ma](https://www.maroc.ma/fr/actualites/lancement-du-reseau-digitpme-pour-accelerer-la-transformation-digitale-des-tpme)), and accountants are the other realistic routes.

From this evidence I draw four tests for a product that markets itself in Morocco. First, **the people who receive your message could become buyers**. Second, **a legal or money deadline repeats on a fixed schedule**. Third, **one customer brings many others**, as an accountant, a syndic firm or a trade federation does. Fourth, **money flows through the product**, so you can earn more than a subscription.

Message costs are not a barrier. Gallabox's rate card puts a WhatsApp "utility" message (a notification such as a reminder or receipt) at about **US$0.005** in the "Rest of Africa" price tier ([Gallabox](https://docs.gallabox.com/pricing-and-billing/whatsapp-pricing/rate-card.md)). A Salesforce rate card lists a Morocco-specific utility rate of 4.94, most likely US$ per 1,000 messages, although the unit on that card is not confirmed ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/WhatsApp-Business-Messaging-Rate-Card-MM-Lite-May-1-2026.pdf)). Meta has said Morocco will get higher rates, and messages sent within 24 hours of the customer writing first are free ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing)). A reminder therefore costs a fraction of a dirham.

On these four tests, **unpaid-invoice reminders for SMEs clearly pass the second and third tests, and only partly pass the first and fourth.** The first test is only partly met because many late payers are large firms or public bodies that will never buy an SME tool. The fourth is only partly met because the first version only shows bank details; money flows through the product only once payment links, the collection desk or invoice financing arrive. The syndic platform clearly passes the second, third and fourth tests. It only partly passes the first, because most residents will never buy software, apart from building-council members and people who own several apartments. Car rental passes the third test only if an agency federation joins. The shared COD buyer score passes the first and third tests but is blocked by data law and by the lack of data at the start.

## Build a "get paid" platform for SMEs, sold directly and through accountants

### Late payment is the largest gap left after closer checking

Late payment is the business pain with the most evidence behind it. Very small firms (TPE) took **about 100 days of sales to collect what they were owed in 2024**, the longest of any company size ([EcoActu](https://ecoactu.ma/delais-de-paiement-le-maroc-reduit-lecart-avec-la-loi/)). Another article puts their collection time at **130 days**, for a period it does not state ([LesEco](https://leseco.ma/maroc/delais-de-paiement-les-tpe-ces-grandes-oubliees.html)). Size labels differ between sources, so treat these as figures for small firms in general, not for one exact sales band. A central-bank analysis of micro and very small firms says **60% of them have cash-flow problems**, although its survey date is not visible ([Finances News](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-financiere-maroc/microentreprises-et-tpe-les-freins-a-l-inclusion-financiere)). The credit insurer Allianz Trade scores collecting debts in Morocco at **56/100, "very high" difficulty**, and calls legal action "unreasonable in most cases" ([Allianz Trade](https://www.allianz-trade.com/en_global/economic-research/collection-complexity/morocco.html)).

Law 69-21 has turned this pain into a legal duty that repeats every quarter. Every company with sales above **MAD 2 million** must now declare its own payment delays; the threshold was MAD 50 million in 2023 and MAD 10 million in 2024 ([Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/delais-de-paiement-attention-le-fisc-elargit-son-radarce-quil-faut-faire-avant-le-31-mars_KKJ6A7NQUFBRVLAHOPJVIGRN7U/)). The DGI said in March 2026 that no business sector is excluded ([Upsilon](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/position-dgi-delais-paiement-personnes-physiques-scp-holdings/)). The declaration is filed on SIMPL **every quarter, even when no invoice was late**, and the third-quarter 2026 declaration is due by 31 October 2026 ([aube.ma](https://aube.ma/blog/declaration-delais-paiement-maroc-loi-69-21); [aafir](https://www.aafir.ma/loi-69-21-un-tournant-pour-les-delais-de-paiement-au-maroc/)). The 2025 declaration was an annual one, due by 1 April 2026 ([Médias24](https://medias24.com/2026/03/14/delais-de-paiement-declaration-obligatoire-avant-le-1er-avril-2026-1643453/)).

The default payment term is 60 days, and the maximum by agreement is 120 days. A company that pays late owes a fine equal to the central bank's policy rate of **2.25% for the first month of delay, plus 0.85% for each further month**, calculated on the amount including VAT ([Lembra Conseil](https://lembra-conseil.com/loi-69-21-delais-de-paiement-maroc/)). The fine is paid **to the Treasury, not to the unpaid supplier** ([UGGC Africa](https://uggcafrica.com/fr/le-tresor-public-nouveau-beneficiaire-des-indemnites-de-retard-quels-risques-pour-les-creanciers/)). Sources disagree on the fine for not filing at all, quoting anything from MAD 5,000 to MAD 250,000 depending on company size ([aube.ma](https://aube.ma/blog/declaration-delais-paiement-maroc-loi-69-21); [Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/414120-delais-de-paiement-les-entreprises-en-infraction-risquent-jusqua-125-000-dh-damende.html)), so you must check it against the text of the law.

The scale is large, and much of it sits with big payers. For 2025, **24,246 of the 56,041 declaring companies (43.3%) reported paying at least one supplier late**, and fines totalled **MAD 1.33 billion** ([EcoActu](https://ecoactu.ma/retards-de-paiement-1-33-mmdh-amendes-2025/)). The 65 public enterprises alone accounted for **34% of late amounts and MAD 423 million of the fines** ([EcoActu](https://ecoactu.ma/retards-de-paiement-1-33-mmdh-amendes-2025/); [Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/economie/delais-de-paiement-423-mdh-damendes-payes-par-les-eep-en-2025/367839)), and one headline says large organisations "still account for most of the delays" ([LesEco](https://leseco.ma/business/delais-de-paiement-les-grandes-structures-concentrent-encore-lessentiel-des-retards.html)). Another headline sums up the behaviour: companies "rush to declare… but not to pay" ([LesEco](https://leseco.ma/maroc/retards-de-paiement-les-entreprises-pressees-de-declarer-mais-pas-de-payer.html)).

**The product does two jobs for opposite sides of the same invoice.** Job A is **collecting what you are owed** (receivables): it helps the supplier who is waiting for money. Job B is **declaring your own late payments** (payables): it is a legal duty of the customer that pays late, and the declaration is built from the bills the company itself owes. Most firms above MAD 2 million have both jobs, but they are different pains. The 43.3% figure above shows how many firms paid a supplier late; it says nothing about who is owed money. For job B the useful fact is simpler: **every firm above MAD 2 million must file every quarter**, even with no late payment. The free tool sells job A, the paid declaration sells job B, and the interviews must test willingness to pay for each one separately.

**On closer checking, no dedicated stand-alone tool for this turned up, but the job is not unserved.** Sage 100, which Sage positions for small and mid-sized firms, includes payment reminders ([Sage Maroc](https://www.sage.com/fr-ma/logiciels-comptabilite/)). Two in three Moroccan companies that use management software use Sage, and company size is what decides whether a firm uses such software at all ([TelQuel](https://telquel.ma/2024/01/10/sage-publie-sa-radioscopie-des-solutions-de-gestion-au-maroc_1850334)). The firms above MAD 2 million that this product targets are therefore the ones most likely to run Sage already. Crystal ERP sells a receivables module that, according to its vendor, sends reminders by email, SMS or letter on days 7, 15 and 30 ([Crystal IT](https://crystalit.ma/blog/logiciel-recouvrement-creances-pme-maroc)). **Nobody has checked whether Sage, Odoo or KHABIR already export the Law 69-21 declaration**, and that is the first thing to find out. The other options are services, not software: electronic registered letters ([LRE](https://trust.lre.ma/comment-la-lettre-recommandee-electronique-lre-simplifie-vos-procedures-de-recouvrement-amiable-au-maroc-)), debt collectors paid only on what they recover ([Coface](https://www.coface.ma/recouvrement-de-creances)) and factoring, where a firm buys a company's unpaid invoices ([Maroc Factoring](https://marocfactoring.com/)). A search for WhatsApp reminder apps returned only French products ([Payée](https://payee.fr/)). The opening is therefore narrower than "nobody does this": a WhatsApp-first, accountant-friendly tool for firms that do not use Sage's reminders, plus the declaration if the big packages do not already produce it.

Two more rules make the problem grow. First, since 1 July 2026, companies with sales of at least MAD 500 million, plus banks and insurers, must withhold tax when they pay suppliers for services. They keep **5% for corporate tax, plus 75% of the VAT** (100% if the supplier has no tax-compliance certificate). The rule extends to companies with sales of MAD 350 million or more in 2027 and MAD 200 million or more in 2028 ([AuditCloud](https://auditcloud.ma/2026/07/06/retenues-source-is-ir-tva-maroc-juillet-2026/); [Quantis](https://quantispartners.ma/en/retenue-source-tva-is-2026/)). Their SME suppliers will therefore receive less than they invoiced, and they must match the gap against their tax accounts. This reconciliation problem grows every January. Second, a DGI note says cash payments of **MAD 20,000 or more are not tolerated**, with a 6% fine ([Finances News](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/fisc-au-dela-de-20-000-dh-le-paiement-par-cash-n-est-plus-tolere)), although the date of the note is unconfirmed. Larger business-to-business invoices are therefore paid by transfer or cheque, which software can match to invoices.

### What to build, how it spreads and how it earns money

The product has four layers. This design is my own proposal, not something tested in the research. Its core is job A, collecting money, because job B is a filing that the state could automate (see the risks below).

The first layer is **a free tool for any business that is owed money**. The user imports invoices from Excel or from an accounting export (Sage, KHABIR or Odoo) and sees who owes how much and for how long. The tool sends reminder sequences by WhatsApp and email in French, Arabic or Darija. Each reminder carries the invoice, the bank details and, later, a payment link. Every message is logged, so the business has a written record if it must later send a formal demand (*mise en demeure*), and the tool can escalate to an electronic registered letter. The free plan is capped at 20 open invoices.

The second layer is **a paid plan for companies above MAD 2 million in sales**. It covers job A without limits and adds job B: it tracks the company's own 60-day and 120-day deadlines on the bills it owes, shows the fine it is building up, prepares the quarterly Law 69-21 declaration and reconciles the tax withheld by large clients. The third layer is **a free console for accountants**. The accountant sees all clients on one screen, prepares declarations in bulk and sends reminders for clients. These reminders show the client firm as the sender but **keep a short product footer**, because without it the loop disappears in the channel expected to bring the most customers. To give accountants a reason to keep the footer, sign-ups that come from their clients' reminders count toward the accountant's revenue share. The fourth layer is **a "do it for me" collection desk**: a small team speaking Darija and French calls late payers on behalf of clients and is paid a share of what it collects, as Coface is. This layer matches the local preference for service over self-service software, but it should start only after a legal check (see the risks). Payment links and invoice financing come later.

**The self-marketing loop is a hypothesis, and on realistic numbers it will not carry the business.** Each reminder ends with a line such as "Sent with [product]: track your own unpaid invoices for free." The table shows the loop step by step. Every rate is my assumption, to be measured in the pilot.

| Step | Assumed rate | Result per paying customer per year |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The customer sends reminders to its late payers | 20 different late payers a year | 20 recipients |
| 2. Some recipients are SMEs that could buy | 30% (OkCredit: 3 in 10 recipients were merchants). The rest are large firms, public bodies or micro-firms; in large firms the reminder reaches an accounts-payable clerk, not a buyer | 6 SMEs |
| 3. Some SME recipients visit the sign-up page | 10% | 0.6 visits |
| 4. Some visitors sign up for the free plan | 25% | 0.15 free sign-ups |
| 5. Some free users start paying | 3% if they are left alone, 10% if someone follows up | 0.005–0.015 paying customers |

On these assumptions, **K is about 0.15 for free sign-ups and about 0.01 for paying customers**, far below 1. To reach K = 1 in free sign-ups, about one recipient in 20 would have to sign up, roughly seven times the rate I assume. The loop is a cheap source of leads that lowers the cost of selling; it is not the growth engine. A second hook is also my own untested hypothesis. When a reminder shows the late payer the fine it is building up (2.25% plus 0.85% a month), a late payer above MAD 2 million, which must declare that delay itself, has a reason to pay and a reason to look at the tool for its own declaration. Plan to sell to SMEs and accountants in person anyway.

**Prices to test.** These are my proposals for the interviews, not market data. No source in this research measured what Moroccan SMEs would pay for this job.

| Offer | Test price | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Free plan (job A) | MAD 0, up to 20 open invoices; reminders carry the product footer | Caps on usage reportedly convert better than caps on features ([Artisan Growth Strategies](https://www.artisangrowthstrategies.com/blog/freemium-conversion-rate-benchmarks)) |
| SME plan (jobs A and B) | MAD 300 a month, or MAD 3,000 a year paid upfront (two months free) | Inside the MAD 250–500 monthly band of local mid-market tools, although those are full management packages, not a single-purpose tool |
| Declaration pack only (job B) | MAD 250 per company per quarter | Lets an accountant buy only the filing for clients who already use Sage for reminders |
| Accountant console | Free; the accountant earns 20–30% of the subscriptions of clients it brings | YouCan pays its affiliates 20–50% for life, although sources disagree on the exact rate ([YouCan](https://youcan.shop/en/affiliate); [ribhonline](https://ribhonline.com/blog/youcan-affiliate-maroc)) |
| Collection desk | 10% of the amount recovered, nothing if nothing is recovered | Coface also charges only on amounts recovered ([Coface](https://www.coface.ma/recouvrement-de-creances)); its rate was not found |

In the interviews, also ask whether a bigger yearly discount would make firms prepay. DabaDoc's yearly price was half its monthly price ([TelQuel](https://telquel.ma/2015/05/25/dabadoc-service-marocain-sante-en-ligne-sexporte-en-algerie-en-tunisie_1448476)).

**Payments.** Start with bank transfers and instant transfers. Add card payments and payment links later through a neutral, licensed payment company, such as Attijari Payment, which was licensed in March 2025 ([Attijari Payment](https://www.attijariwafabank.com/sites/default/files/publication/2025-09/CP%20VF%20-%20AP%20acquisition%20commer%C3%A7ants.pdf)), or Chari, which holds a Bank Al-Maghrib payment licence ([Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/economie/chari-leve-12-m-et-obtient-la-licence-de-paiement-de-bank-al-maghrib/307728)). Be careful with Manza (formerly Zazu). This research found no payment licence for it, and it already sells invoices with payment links to the same small businesses ([Médias24](https://medias24.com/2026/04/08/visa-et-zazu-lancent-un-compte-pro-100-digital-pour-les-entrepreneurs-marocains-1656073)), so it is a competitor before it is a partner. Check its licence status before any talks. Do not apply for your own payment licence. Chari needed one, and Bank Al-Maghrib's 0.5% cap on interchange, the fee banks charge each other on card payments, leaves little margin for a small processor ([Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/10/341181/bank-al-maghrib-cuts-bank-card-payment-commission-cap-to-0-5/)).

**Size, by my own rough calculation.** Net revenue per paying firm will be well below the list price. A firm that prepays MAD 3,000 a year through an accountant who keeps 20–30% leaves about **MAD 2,100–2,400**. A 50% accountant share leaves MAD 1,500. If interviews show that a DabaDoc-size 50% yearly discount is needed, the figure falls to about MAD 900–1,800. A firm you sell to directly leaves MAD 3,000. I use **MAD 2,000 a year (about US$200) per paying firm** for planning.

Moroccan precedent sets the pace. Local business-software pioneers took **2–5 years to reach 1,000 paying customers**: DabaDoc was free for about 18 months before it charged, and LaCaisse.ma needed about five years and 15 staff ([TelQuel](https://telquel.ma/2015/05/25/dabadoc-service-marocain-sante-en-ligne-sexporte-en-algerie-en-tunisie_1448476); [Finances News](https://www.fnh.ma/article/actualite-entreprises/start-up-marocaine-lacaisse-ma-voit-grand)). The free plan will not fill the gap on its own. Free users of business software typically convert to paid at 3–5% when things go well ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)), and in Morocco the rate is likely 3% or less, so 1,000 paying firms from the free plan alone would need more than 30,000 free users. Most paying firms will have to come from accountants and direct selling. A three-year scenario based on the precedent looks like this.

| Scenario at the end of year 3 | Paying firms | Net subscription revenue a year |
|---|---|---|
| Slow (LaCaisse pace: about 5 years to 1,000) | About 600 | About MAD 1.2M (US$120,000) |
| Base (fast end of the precedent: 1,000 in 3 years) | About 1,000 | About MAD 2M (US$200,000) |
| Fast (deadlines and accountants work: twice the precedent) | About 2,000 | About MAD 4M (US$400,000) |

Collection fees and, later, financing would come on top. The base case pays for a three-person team, which costs about MAD 0.4–0.7 million a year (see the plan below), but leaves little for a sales team. Compared on the same basis, the SME market is larger than the syndic market. At full coverage of the 56,041 declaring firms, list prices of MAD 3,000–3,600 a year give **MAD 168–202 million a year**. Syndic software at full coverage gives **MAD 24–60 million**, using a vendor's price of MAD 2–5 per apartment a month ([Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/fr/blog/logiciel-gestion-copropriete-syndic-rabat-prix-mad-2026)). At 10% coverage the figures are MAD 17–20 million and MAD 2.4–6 million. Neither is a giant subscription business; in both, the upside comes from the money that flows through the product.

**E-invoicing cuts both ways, and credit is the more durable upside.** When the e-invoicing decree is finally published, invoices cleared through the DGI could flow into the product automatically, which would make job A easier. But the same data would let the DGI fill in the late-payment declaration itself (see the risks). Credit is a better long-term bet. Under a December 2025 agreement between the state, the central bank and the banks, a **national credit score for very small firms starts in 2026**, and banks committed about **MAD 18 billion** of lending to these firms for 2026 ([Maroc.ma](https://www.maroc.ma/fr/actualites/rabat-signature-de-la-charte-relative-au-financement-et-laccompagnement-des-tpe); [LesEco](https://leseco.ma/business/financement-de-la-tpe-bank-al-maghrib-active-le-levier-de-la-garantie.html)). Clean records of unpaid invoices could support invoice financing with a bank partner. The research found no Moroccan startup offering invoice financing to small firms.

### What could go wrong, and when to choose the syndic platform

The risks below are ordered roughly by how much damage they could do. The mitigations are my proposals.

| Risk | Why it matters | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Demand is not proven** | No survey or interview in this research asked SMEs whether they would pay for either job, or how much | Test prices for each job separately in the 40 interviews; do not build before day 31 |
| **Sage and others may already do it** | Sage 100 includes reminders and dominates equipped firms. Sage, Odoo, KHABIR or Crystal could add a Law 69-21 export cheaply, if they have not already | Ask accountants in days 1–14 which software produces their declaration. If Sage, KHABIR or Odoo already produce it for most of them, drop job B and lead with collection. Compete on WhatsApp, Darija and an accountant-first design |
| **The state could automate the declaration** | Once DGI e-invoicing is live, the DGI will hold every B2B invoice and could pre-fill the late-payment declaration on SIMPL for free. That would remove the paid declaration layer, as the free e-invoicing portal removed paid basic invoicing | Make collection the core: reminders, the collection desk and invoice financing. Treat the declaration as an entry hook that may last only a few years |
| **The filing format is unknown** | If SIMPL only accepts typing by hand, automating the declaration saves less time | Check in week one; a clean checklist still helps |
| **The accountant channel is thin and contested** | About 11 declaring firms per accountant on average; *fiduciaires* mostly hold micro clients below MAD 2 million; Sage has accountant pricing, KHABIR a partner rate, and ExpertC, Fiducio and Experio target *fiduciaires* | Count each accountant's clients above MAD 2 million in the interviews; sell directly to SMEs as well |
| **Accountants may keep doing it by hand** | Many may already file the declaration manually at little cost to themselves | Price the declaration pack below the value of their time; test this in the interviews |
| **Banks can add reminders** | Banks and online business accounts could add reminders and payment links; Hsabati Pro and Manza are already close | Move fast and work with neutral payment partners, not with competing accounts |
| **Data protection** | The tool stores the names and phone numbers of debtors on behalf of many creditors. Processing must be declared to the CNDP, transfers abroad need authorisation ([CNDP](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/CNDP_Procedure-de-Notification-des-traitements_20250724.pdf)), and an undeclared file risks a **MAD 10,000–100,000** fine ([Médias24](https://medias24.com/2023/07/01/donnees-personnelles-ce-que-prevoit-la-loi-en-cas-de-violations/)) | Host in Morocco or obtain the CNDP transfer authorisation; declare the processing before the pilot; write a data contract with each creditor |
| **WhatsApp rules** | Meta prices messages by template type, and a marketing message costs about five times a utility one (about US$0.023–0.028 against US$0.005). A reminder with a sign-up footer may be classed as marketing or rejected. Messages to people who have not agreed to receive them could get the number blocked. Meta's detailed rules were not researched | Collect each recipient's agreement through the creditor; get the reminder template approved before the pilot; use a sender per client, not one shared number; keep email and SMS as fallbacks |
| **Collecting your own revenue** | Moroccan startups report that business customers pay them after about six months on average, sometimes 13 ([LesEco](https://leseco.ma/maroc/delais-de-paiement-et-charges-fiscales-les-defis-financiers-des-start-up-au-maroc-video.html)) | Require upfront payment, yearly or quarterly, by bank transfer; give no credit |
| **Legal limits on the collection desk** | The rules on collecting debts for third parties and on harassment were not checked in this research. Courts already curb aggressive tactics: one stopped a syndic from blocking a lift over unpaid charges ([Bladi](https://www.bladi.net/impayes-maroc-justice-interdit-syndics-bloquer-ascenseur-parking,121644.html)) | Get a legal opinion before launching the desk; use polite scripts and the written record |
| **Suppliers may be afraid to chase** | Small suppliers may hesitate to chase large clients | Polite tone, and framing the reminder as legal compliance |
| **The research has limits** | It relied on search-result summaries, so many figures are vendor claims or come from a single source | Treat this report as a map; verify key figures in the interviews |

Choose the **syndic platform** instead if your interviews fail, if Sage or the state already covers the declaration and collection alone does not sell, or if you have contacts with property developers or syndic firms. It runs on the same engine: reminders, a payment reference for each apartment, reconciliation of payments and a written record of each step. Since Law 30-24, a syndic must make a **documented attempt at conciliation before going to court** over unpaid charges, and must notify owners of general-assembly decisions **within 8 days** ([Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/litiges-lies-a-la-copropriete-la-conciliation-devient-obligatoire_XSKXPS7VSVCOFOCQH46GTY6UFU/); [ReaConsult](https://reaconsult.ma/tendances-et-conseils-en-immobilier/notification-decisions-assemblee-generale-copropriete-8-jours-loi-30-24)). A 2023 decree requires standard accounts and full financial statements before each general assembly ([Médias24](https://medias24.com/2024/01/14/comptabilite-budget-archives-ce-qui-va-changer-pour-les-syndics-de-copropriete/)). Courts are also limiting pressure tactics, as the lift case above shows. That makes gentle tools for collecting payment more valuable.

The syndic loop works inside one building, but it moves to the next building only through specific routes. Inside a building, the syndic adds every owner; each owner gets a payment reference and a statement, and owners who pay can see that others are being reminded too. From one building to the next there are three routes. First, **syndic firms manage many buildings at once**, so one firm signed brings many buildings; Thaïs alone runs 70 residences ([Infomédiaire](https://www.infomediaire.net/?p=134472)). Second, **property developers can install the app at handover** in every building of a project, as one developer already does in its own buyer app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ci/app/costamar/id6499503514)). Third, **council members and owners who own flats in other buildings, or who move**, can ask their other syndic to adopt the app. The first two routes add buildings in batches through a sale, and the third is untested. This is steady, sales-led growth, not viral spread.

Software alone earns very little per apartment. A vendor estimates MAD 2–5 per apartment a month, against professional syndic fees of **MAD 150–400 per apartment a month** ([Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/fr/blog/logiciel-gestion-copropriete-syndic-rabat-prix-mad-2026); [LesMRE](https://www.lesmre.com/fr/guides/syndic-copropriete-maroc-mre)). The money is therefore in payment fees, or in running buildings as a technology-enabled syndic firm, which is what the successful incumbents do. Owners who live abroad (MRE) are a valuable group of remote payers ([Bladi](https://www.bladi.net/syndics-maroc-mre-crient-scandale,115661.html)).

Car rental remains a third option, and only if an agency federation agrees to back the product.

### The first 90 days, starting with the 31 October filing

Today is 22 October 2026. The third-quarter declaration is due on 31 October, nine days from now, so in week one you can watch accountants file it. Quarterly declarations are due by the end of the month after the quarter, so the fourth-quarter 2026 declaration should be due around 31 January 2027, close to day 90 of this plan. That date is my inference. The 2025 declaration was an annual one due by 1 April 2026, so the year-end filing may instead fall at the end of March 2027. Confirm the date in week one. The prices and targets below are my proposals, not market data.

| Days | Goal | Concrete actions | Test before moving on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–14 | Pin down the rules and the competition | Before 31 October, ask two accountants to show you, screen by screen, how they file the Q3 2026 declaration on SIMPL, and whether SIMPL accepts a file upload or only manual entry. Ask which software produced the data (Sage, KHABIR, Odoo or Excel) and whether it exports the declaration. Confirm the official fines for not filing and the next deadline. Register your company, declare your data processing to the CNDP, and choose hosting in Morocco or request authorisation to host abroad. Open a WhatsApp Business API account through a Meta partner and submit the reminder template for approval. | You can describe the declaration field by field. **Stop condition:** if most accountants say Sage, KHABIR or Odoo already produce the declaration automatically, drop job B and test collection alone. |
| 1–30 | Hear the pain and test prices with 40 people | Interview 25 accountants in Casablanca and Rabat (a mix of chartered, licensed and unregistered *fiduciaires*) and 15 owners or finance managers of firms with MAD 2–50M in sales. Ask how they track unpaid invoices, how many are late, and how many late payers are SMEs rather than large firms or public bodies. Ask accountants how many of their clients are above MAD 2M. Test the price card for each job separately: MAD 300 a month or MAD 3,000 a year for the SME plan, MAD 250 a quarter for the declaration pack and a 10% success fee for the collection desk. Ask what they would pay and whether they would prepay for a year. | At least 10 accountants call it a monthly pain, and the pilot accountants have about 10 or more clients above MAD 2M each. 5 agree to a pilot. At least half of the owners accept a price at or near the card for job A. |
| 15–45 | Do it by hand first, and measure recipients from day one | Run a hand-operated pilot for 5 accountants and 3–5 of their clients each. Import their Excel lists of unpaid invoices. Send reminders only to contacts the creditor confirms have agreed to be contacted, using the approved template, a sender per client and the product footer. Record who replies, who clicks the footer and whether each recipient is an SME, a large firm or a public body. Calculate the fine each client is building up and prepare a declaration summary. | Money is actually collected, some recipients reply, at least 2% of recipients click the footer, and at least one recipient asks about the tool. |
| 31–60 | Build the first version | Build a web app with French screens and French, Arabic and Darija message templates. It needs Excel and CSV import, a who-owes-what view, reminders on days 7, 15 and 30, bank and instant-transfer details, a fine calculator, a quarterly declaration pack, a multi-client accountant console and a free sign-up page linked from every reminder. | 10 accountants and 50 client companies are active. |
| 45–75 | Line up partners and legal advice | Open talks with one neutral licensed payment partner (for example Attijari Payment or Chari) for payment links; check Manza's licence status before approaching it. Talk to LRE about electronic registered letters, and to a regional chamber of commerce or the DigiTPME network about speaking at an event. Get a legal opinion on the collection desk. Publish an accountant partner programme with a revenue share and a public partner list. | One signed letter of intent, one event booked and the legal opinion received. |
| 61–90 | Charge money before the next deadline | Sell the SME plan and the declaration pack at the test prices. Require upfront payment, yearly or quarterly, by bank transfer. Track every number in the loop: reminders sent, share of recipients who are SMEs, replies, footer clicks and sign-ups. | 30 paying SMEs, at least 10 of them through accountants; 1,000+ reminders sent; at least 2% of recipients visit the sign-up page; the first sign-ups that came only from reminders. |

A small team is enough for this plan. Developer salaries in Casablanca and Rabat run about **MAD 7,000–12,000 a month for a junior and MAD 20,000–35,000 for a senior** ([JobSquare](https://www.jobsquare.ma/blog/salaires-informatique-maroc-grille/)). One senior developer, one junior and one customer-support person who speaks Darija and French can carry it, while you, the founder, do the selling. This research did not find a salary for the support role; if it is paid like a junior developer, the team costs about **MAD 34,000–59,000 a month, or about MAD 100,000–180,000 for the 90 days**. Add company registration, the legal opinion, hosting and travel, which this research did not price. Messages cost almost nothing: 1,000 utility reminders cost about US$5.

At day 90, make a clear decision. **Continue** if SMEs are paying, some of them through accountants, and recipients are signing up. If SMEs pay but recipients never sign up, the loop is weak, so **hire field sales** for accountants and SMEs and grow by selling in person. If the declaration turns out to be automated by Sage, KHABIR or the DGI, **keep only the collection product** and test whether it sells on its own. If neither accountants nor owners will pay, **move the engine to syndics**: test it first with 10 volunteer syndics, 2 syndic firms and 2 property developers, since most of the reminder, payment and reconciliation code carries over.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question you should ask. In Morocco, building a product is easy: there are a dozen thin copies of every booking page, QR menu and invoice generator. The state is also becoming the free provider of basic compliance tools, through the DGI's promised e-invoicing portal, the Massar school app and NARSA's exam platform, and it could do the same for the late-payment declaration once it sees every invoice. What keeps its value is what free state tools do not do: following up, collecting money, working across many clients and, later, moving and financing payments. So instead of asking "business owners, e-commerce sellers or small businesses?", ask "where does money get stuck between two parties, and who has a reason to pay to get it moving?" Today the clear answers are business invoices and building charges. School fees could follow on the same engine, and COD cash only if a courier partner supplies the data.

Treat this report as a map, not as proof. Every number came from search-result summaries, many of them vendor claims, and no buyer was interviewed. Self-spreading will help this product but not carry it, so its fate depends on two things only the first 90 days can show: whether SMEs will prepay for collection on its own, and whether Sage or the state already does the declaration for them.
